Vivander vs AI visibility platforms like Profound, Peec AI, Scrunch and Otterly: which do you need?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

They do different jobs, and many businesses end up with both. AI visibility platforms such as Profound, Peec AI, Scrunch and Otterly monitor how often a brand appears in AI answers for a fixed prompt list over time, and some of them hold the largest citation datasets in the field. The Vivander AI Visibility Audit is a diagnosis: it reads whether the brand was offered rather than merely named, checks whether AI crawlers can reach the site at all, maps every heading against the questions buyers ask, traces the sources behind every answer, and hands over a work list. Platforms are for tracking after the diagnosis. I am the diagnosis, and I cite the platforms' published data in my own reports where it is the best available, because it often is.

I am not going to tell you their numbers are wrong. They are usually right about what they measure. What they measure is not recommendation.

What the platforms do well

Scale and trend. Profound holds the largest disclosed citation dataset, 680 million citations, with the cleanest denominators, and its prompt volumes draw from over a billion real conversations. Peec AI publishes free per-platform source breakdowns from 30 million sources and is honest about its own limits, which in this field is a compliment. Scrunch orders its checks access-first, which matches the retrieval gate I use. Otterly and BrightEdge track mention share over time.

If you need to know whether your appearance rate moved this month, a platform tells you. I cite Profound and Peec because nobody else has that data.

What the platforms do not do

Read whether you were offered. Most count appearances, and an appearance can be a landmark, a foil or a warning. In one engagement a dashboard reported visibility in 71 percent of prompts and my read of the same prompts put recommendation events at 9 percent.

Check access. A platform reports that you are absent. It does not open the Cloudflare console, read the robots file or fetch the initial HTML that explains why.

Map your content. No platform reads every heading on your site against your prompt set and marks the implicit rows an editor can fix.

Publish the math. A score moving from 73 to 93 with an unpublished formula is the kind of claim my evidence register bans, and the register applies to my own scores too. Every report prints the formula.

State the limits. My reports say that three runs per prompt is below the published recommended minimum, that a figure I once used was withdrawn, and where the evidence stops. That posture is the product.

When to use which

Situation Use
You do not know whether AI recommends you or why Vivander audit
You know the fixes and want the trend monthly A platform
You have a platform score and cannot act on it Vivander audit, with the platform's data as input
You need the per-platform source breakdown for a category Peec AI's free data, read with my method
You need a work list with an owner and a 30-day re-test Vivander audit

What I will not claim

That I replace monitoring. Or that my sample size is large. That any single answer proves a visibility state. Or that a platform's number is wrong.

Where this is going

The platforms will get better at reading offers, because the measurement standard is being written now. Vivander and a hospitality content-distribution platform are running a joint working group on it. When Share of Answer and recommendation events converge, the platforms will be worth more, not less, and the audit will still be the thing you run first.

A dashboard tells you the temperature. It does not tell you why the room is cold.
