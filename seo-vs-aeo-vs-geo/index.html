SEO vs AEO vs GEO: what is the difference?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

SEO, search engine optimization, is the work of being found and ranked by search engines. AEO, answer engine optimization, is the work of structuring a page so a machine can lift a fragment of it as the answer to a question. GEO, generative engine optimization, is the work of being selected when an AI model assembles an answer from many sources, which depends mostly on corroboration off your own site. They are layers, not alternatives. At Vivander Advisors I call the four of them together, with AI visibility as the measured outcome, the Visibility Stack, and I audit each in the order that gates the next.

Clients blur these three and then argue about the wrong one. Hold the distinction and most of the argument goes away.

The three, side by side

SEO AEO GEO
Question Can the page be found and ranked? Can the answer be lifted from the page? Is the brand chosen when the answer is assembled?
Where the work is Your site: technical health, links, keywords Your site: structure and facts Other people's sites: the sources the platform trusts
Unit Page Fragment, about fifteen words Entity
Evidence grade Search rank graded 9.4 of 10 on 30 sources Answer-first graded strong in a 45-study review; four gatekeepers at n=252,000 Mentions outpredict links (0.664 to 0.737); three seeded pages flipped five engines
Time to move Weeks to months Days to weeks; retrieval is live Weeks for retrieval, months for training
Common failure Blocked crawlers, slow pages Answer in paragraph six, price in a widget No corroboration on the two sources that matter

Why SEO comes first

Because assistants retrieve from search indexes. A page that cannot rank is rarely retrieved and therefore rarely cited. Search rank is the second-best-evidenced factor in AI visibility, behind only URL accessibility. SEO is the prerequisite, not the result, and a business that stops doing it loses the retrieval layer everything else sits on.

Why AEO is the common gap

Because most businesses that pass SEO fail AEO without knowing it. They have a FAQ page and headings. What they lack is evidence that the questions their pages answer are the questions buyers ask. My content architecture score maps every heading against the full prompt set, and question coverage below 20 percent is a high-severity finding. The most valuable rows are the implicit ones: the fact is on the page, no heading asks the question, and extraction happens by luck.

Why GEO is the layer clients defer

Because it means other people's websites, and the evidence is uncomfortable. Three fabricated off-site pages flipped five of eight AI engines about a brand that did not exist. Journalism is 27 percent of citations spread across 20,000 outlets. The two or three sources each platform reads for a category decide the shortlist. My off-site layer names them per client, and the most common GEO fix is matching the facts on those sources to the facts on the site.

What GEO is not

Not the "40 percent uplift" from the 2024 Princeton paper as a general claim: the source was pre-injected into context, scores were normalized to sum to one, and the figure applies to a fifth-ranked source on a 2023 model. Nor vector-embedding optimization, which has no public case evidence. Neither self-published rankings, which decay and cost credibility. Nor schema markup, which a controlled study found does not lift citation.

How I sequence the three

Retrieval and access first. After that, AEO on the ten pages that matter, cheapest fix first. Then GEO on the sources the audit shows each platform citing. Finally, if the audit client wants follow-up retests, repeat the questions per platform at 60 and 90 days, with site-side and off-site changes reported separately. Retests are optional.

Where this is going

The acronyms will merge before the disciplines do. Google's AI Mode is search, and the index feeds the answer, so SEO and AEO are already one job with two halves. GEO stays separate because it lives on pages you do not own.

Stop thinking in templates. Start thinking in taxonomies. Your site is no longer a slideshow of pretty pictures. It is one input to a machine that reads everyone.
