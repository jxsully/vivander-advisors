What is a recommendation event, and how is it different from Share of Answer?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

A recommendation event is the moment an AI response names a business, resolvable to a specific entity, puts it forward as an option for the person asking, and does not negate it. All three at once. I introduced the term at Vivander Advisors to fix a problem with Share of Answer, the common industry metric. Share of Answer counts a brand’s appearances in AI responses, and an appearance can be a landmark, a comparison foil or a warning. A recommendation event cannot. My own scores changed definition in 2026 when landmark, foil and negated rows left the numerator, and I said so, because the direction was toward lower and more honest numbers.

Seventy-one percent on the dashboard. Nine percent recommended. Same prompts. That gap is the reason the term exists.

What is Share of Answer?

Share of Answer is the share of AI responses, for a set of prompts, in which a brand appears. The AI-era equivalent of share of voice. Most monitoring platforms report a version of it.

Useful for trend. Useless for diagnosis, because it treats every appearance as equal. A hotel used to give directions to the resort the assistant recommended has the same Share of Answer as the resort. One of them got the booking.

How does a recommendation event get assigned?

Four gates in order over a six-value scale. Is the brand named and resolvable to this entity: if not, invisible. Next, is it offered as an option to the asker: if not, named without being offered. Is it negated: if so, negative mention. Then, is it offered with a link to its own domain: cited. Is it put forward as an answer to the question: recommended. Otherwise, mentioned.

A single or shortlist recommendation counts. When a row is both cited and recommended, recommended wins and the citation is kept. Endorsement strength and list position are recorded and never enter the binary.

Why do the middle values matter?

Because they keep the fact that the assistant knows you exist while holding it out of the visibility number. That is where a landmark, a foil and a historical aside belong.

Removing them from the count is what makes the named-to-offered conversion computable, and that conversion is the diagnosis. A low rate means the assistant does not understand what you are for, and more content will not fix it.

What are Ghost Rankings and the cited-when-mentioned rate?

Two instruments that fall out of the same rows for free.

A Ghost Ranking is a row where you are named or cited and a competitor is recommended in the same response. Visible and losing. A different problem from absent, with a different fix.

The cited-when-mentioned rate is, of the rows where you are visible, the share that carry a link to your own domain. Mentions build familiarity. Citations carry the link. The rate says which of the two you are missing.

Why not a reputation score?

Because any reputation score folded into a visibility composite rewards invisibility. A brand nobody cites has no negative citations. I record negative mentions and route them to a source list, never to a number that goes up when things get worse.

Where this is going

Vivander and a hospitality content-distribution platform are running a joint working group on measuring AI recommendation, presented at Destination AI in September 2026, because the industry needs a shared definition before it can compare numbers. The recommendation event is my proposal for that definition. It exists to be argued with. Every definition in it has a stated reason, and the reason matters more than the wording.

A number nobody else can rerun is a feeling with a decimal point.
