How do AI assistants decide which businesses to recommend?

Vivander Advisors helps businesses understand how AI describes and recommends them. This guide connects AI discoverability with clear business facts, buyer questions and evidence you can inspect.

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Three gates, in order. The assistant retrieves pages about the topic from an index or a live crawl. It parses those pages into fragments and picks the ones that answer the question directly. Then it assembles an answer from the fragments that agree with each other and cites the sources it used. Fail the first gate and you never reach the second. I built the Vivander AI Visibility Audit around that order, because most businesses missing from AI answers are failing at retrieval, and content work aimed at the third gate cannot fix a failure at the first.

Most of the advice in this field starts at gate three. That is why so much of it makes things worse.

Gate one: retrieval

Every answer starts with a fetch. ChatGPT with search, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI surfaces all query an index or pull pages live before they write a word. If your pages are not in that index, or the assistant's crawler cannot read them, you do not exist for that question.

Write the probability of being cited as a chain. The chance the topic triggers a search. Times the chance your page is retrieved given that search. And then the chance your fragment is chosen given retrieval.

Content quality is the third term. The first two dominate.

A 2026 study called SAGEO Arena, by Kim and colleagues, tested 171,003 documents across 2,700 queries and did something most GEO research skips: it reinstated retrieval instead of pre-loading the page into the model. Body-only optimization reduced top-20 presence by about 9 percent, top-10 presence after reranking by 16 percent, and final citation by 6 percent.

Read that again. Rewriting a page nobody retrieved made it less likely to be cited.

Gate two: selection

Assistants do not rank pages. They dismantle them.

Microsoft's own description is that assistants break content into smaller structured pieces and evaluate each for authority and relevance. DEJAN read Google's grounding output across 883,262 snippets in 2026 and found extraction happening at roughly fifteen-word chunks, with a heavy lead bias. Opening paragraphs lifted almost whole. Grounding plateauing around 540 words no matter how long the page ran.

Selection favors the fragment that answers. A 2026 study by Vishwakarma and colleagues ran 252,000 trials across six models with page length held constant and found four factors every model treated as a gate: no price, low position in a list, an old timestamp, hedged language. Odds ratios above 100 on all six. The corpus was product reviews, so I will not pretend the number transfers to a hotel page. Its direction matches everything else I see.

Gate three: corroboration

Assistants prefer facts that more than one source agrees on, and they prefer the sources their platform trusts for your category.

Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands, restated in their December 2025 follow-up, found branded web mentions correlating with AI visibility at 0.664 on ChatGPT and 0.709 on AI Mode. Backlinks: 0.218 on AI Overviews. Mentions outpredict links. Ahrefs sells the tool that measures the winning metric, so read it as direction, and note that every study points the same direction.

The causal evidence is stranger. In 2025 Ahrefs invented a brand, Xarumei, with zero web presence, and seeded three fabricated off-site pages against an official on-site FAQ. Gemini, AI Mode, Perplexity, Grok and Copilot all flipped from "this brand does not exist" to reciting the fabrications. ChatGPT held to the official page. Claude never ingested the site.

Three pages moved five engines. One brand, no control, vendor-run, and still the clearest demonstration anyone has produced that a few off-site sources define what AI says about you.

Why the platforms disagree

Each one runs its own retrieval and trusts its own sources. URL-level overlap between engines runs between 11 and 18 percent. A 45-study review put it plainly: the results refute the notion of a global GEO ranking.

ChatGPT's most-cited domain is Wikipedia. Gemini's and Perplexity's is Reddit. For hotel questions, Booking.com and Airbnb frequently outrank all of them, per Peec AI's March 2026 read of 30 million sources.

Any advice that treats "AI search" as one channel is wrong before it starts.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Confirm retrieval before you touch a word of content. Crawlers allowed. Facts in the initial HTML. Something of yours cited for something.

For your role. The first gate is technical, the second is editorial, the third is reputational. In most hotel companies those are three departments. Somebody has to hold the sequence.

For your customers. They see one answer. Nobody tells them it was assembled from twelve fragments and that yours failed at the door.

What I do not know

Whether the retrieval gate's premise holds for your pages. SAGEO proved body-only optimization can be net negative in its corpus. Whether a material share of your pages are unretrieved is a question only a test on your site answers. I run that test first.

Where this is going

The retrieval step is getting shorter and the agent on the other end is getting more capable. Google's agents already call a booking endpoint and award the lead to whoever answers with a real slot. The three gates are not going away. They are being compressed into one conversation, with a customer who never sees the machinery.

I ran an audit for a resort that ranked on page one for its market and was recommended in one of fifty prompts. Its rate lived in a booking widget the crawlers could not read. Nothing a copywriter could have fixed. The first gate was closed, and everyone was polishing the third.
