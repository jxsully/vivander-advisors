From Web3 to Web4: Preparing for Agentic Hospitality

By Michael J. Goldrich · Founder, Vivander AdvisorsOriginal feature: June 11, 2025 · Summary published November 3, 2026A summary of From Web3 to Web4, Hospitality Enters Its Smartest Era, with the main argument and practical implications for hotel leaders.Read the full feature on Hospitality Upgrade →

What is this feature about?

Agentic hospitality describes a possible shift toward software agents researching, negotiating and completing travel tasks for guests and operators. In From Web3 to Web4, Hospitality Enters Its Smartest Era, I connect digital ownership with systems that can act—and examine what hotels would need to make that useful.

What do Web3 and Web4 mean in this feature?

Web3 refers to a model in which people can hold and use digital identities, assets and benefits across participating systems. The article explores examples such as transferable booking rights, programmable loyalty and verified credentials. Web4 is the term it uses for adding intelligence and autonomous action to that foundation.These labels describe the feature’s framework. They are not a promise that every hotel system supports those capabilities, or that every traveler wants them. The article is a June 2025 exploration of a possible direction for the industry.

How could bookings and loyalty change?

A reservation or benefit could become something a traveler can manage under defined rules, rather than a record locked inside one brand’s interface. Verified identity could reduce repeated checks. Programmable agreements could coordinate parts of the booking and payment process.The practical value depends on interoperability, understandable conditions and a good experience. Calling a loyalty program tokenized adds little if guests gain no meaningful control or useful ability to use the benefit.

What changes when the customer has an agent?

An agent representing a traveler would need to evaluate rooms, policies, preferences and price, then act within its authority. That calls for hotel information that is accessible and interpretable beyond a screen designed for human browsing.The feature extends the same logic to the operator. Hotel systems might monitor conditions, prepare offers or coordinate routine tasks. People would still need to manage the exceptions and protect the guest relationship.

What stands in the way?

Fragmented property systems, inconsistent standards and closed integrations make transactions across platforms difficult. Identity verification creates questions about responsibility when credentials fail. Automated contracts require clear definitions of what is being sold and what happens when something goes wrong.Guest adoption matters too. Complex wallets, uncertain recovery and hard-to-understand ownership can add friction rather than remove it. The experience has to hide unnecessary complexity while keeping rights, permissions and consequences understandable.

How should hotels prepare without betting the property?

The feature recommends small, useful experiments rather than wholesale replacement. A limited identity, loyalty or payment use case can reveal whether a proposed system reduces friction and works with existing operations. Teams need to understand the purpose and limits of the change, even if they do not build the underlying technology.The larger conclusion is about service. If systems eventually coordinate more logistics, hotel staff can concentrate on the moments that need empathy, creativity and local judgment. Success depends on designing the handoffs, not simply adopting the newest label.

Practical takeaways for hotel leaders

  • Evaluate digital ownership by the control and convenience it gives a guest.
  • Make policies, offers and inventory information clear enough for systems to interpret.
  • Test interoperability, responsibility and recovery before expanding automation.
  • Start with a bounded use case and preserve human support for exceptions.

Questions about agentic hospitality

What is agentic hospitality? In this feature, agentic hospitality is an approach in which AI agents can act for travelers or hotel operators across parts of the journey. Useful deployment depends on reliable information, permissions, integrations and accountable human oversight.
Does Web3 mean a hotel needs to launch an NFT? No. The article emphasizes useful functionality, such as portable benefits or verified identity, rather than adopting a token merely for appearance. A pilot should solve an operational or guest problem.
Is Web4 already a standard hotel platform? No. Web4 is the feature’s term for a possible evolution toward intelligent, agent-mediated interactions. The page summarizes a June 2025 perspective, rather than claiming universal current availability.
