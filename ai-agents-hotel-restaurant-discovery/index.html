AI Agents in Hospitality: How Guests Choose Hotels and Restaurants

Agentic commerce for hotels and restaurants

Three guides follow the journey from guest discovery to business readiness and trusted payments, applying the three chapters of Mastercard’s report to hospitality.
Supporting researchCommon questionsSources

What is agentic commerce in hospitality?

Agentic commerce in hospitality means software helping a guest research, compare and, where supported and authorized, book a hotel, reserve a table or place an order. The guest supplies the goal and limits. The business must supply reliable information, a usable transaction path and a clear route to human help.A guest planning an anniversary weekend does not want to compare twenty room descriptions and twelve menus. They want a quiet room, a memorable dinner and confidence that the total fits their budget. AI can help with that research. Permission to spend is a separate decision.This first guide applies Patrick Dixon’s consumer chapter in Mastercard’s A Short History of the Future of Shopping and Payments to hotel and restaurant discovery. His argument places convenience and confidence at the center of adoption. Vivander’s hospitality analysis: make choosing easier while preserving the guest’s control.

Are travelers and diners ready to let AI choose?

Travel research shows interest in assistance alongside reluctance to hand over decisions. Booking.com’s July 2025 report found that 89% of respondents wanted AI in future travel planning, while only 12% were comfortable with AI making decisions independently. Its online study covered 37,325 people across 33 markets in April–May 2025. Those are reported attitudes, not an autonomous hotel booking rate. [2]Restaurant research points to a concrete information problem. In OpenTable’s May 2025 survey of 1,500 US consumers, 27% said they had decided against booking a restaurant because online information was too difficult to find. The study identifies friction; it does not prove that deploying AI will recover those bookings. [3]

What the Mastercard report can tell hospitality leaders

The report combines expert forecasts with an Opinium study of 13,000 parent–teen pairs across 13 European and nearby markets, conducted in June–July 2026. It concerns shopping and payments. Its forecasts are useful scenarios, but its sample does not establish how all hotel guests or restaurant diners behave. Use hospitality research and your own guest evidence to decide what to change. [1]

How AI changes hotel discovery and booking decisions

An AI-assisted hotel search can combine location, room configuration, arrival time and price constraints. A property earns consideration when those facts are available and consistent. A broad claim such as “perfect for families” is less useful than an explicit occupancy limit, connecting-room policy and breakfast arrangement.

Hotel example: the family arriving after midnight

Illustrative scenario: a parent asks for two nights near a hospital, parking, a room for two adults and two children, and check-in after midnight. A system may find attractive photos yet fail to verify reception hours or bed configuration. The hotel should explain what is guaranteed, what must be requested and who can confirm it before purchase.
  • Make fit explicit: room capacity, bed types, accessible features, parking restrictions and late-arrival instructions.
  • Explain the full stay: taxes, mandatory fees, breakfast, parking and any deposit or incidental hold.
  • Keep live facts live: use the booking engine for availability and date-specific prices. A guide page should not imply inventory is guaranteed.
  • Offer a human handoff: guests should be able to resolve a special requirement without repeating the entire conversation.

How AI changes restaurant discovery and reservations

Diners often need specific answers before committing: whether a menu suits the group, how a reservation works and what the evening will cost. AI-assisted discovery increases the value of clear menus, accurate hours and useful descriptions of the experience.

Restaurant example: dinner with a dietary requirement

Illustrative scenario: a diner requests a quiet table for six, vegetarian choices and help with a serious allergy. Publish the current menu, reservation policy and a direct contact route. Describe the process for discussing allergies with staff. An AI interpretation of menu text must not be presented as a guarantee about ingredients or cross-contact.OpenTable’s July 2025 Concierge announcement documented AI-supported answers using restaurant menus, reviews and descriptions. That launch described autonomous reservation capability as a future feature of Concierge. It provides evidence that restaurant data can inform AI research, without establishing that every assistant can book every venue today. [3]

What information do AI-assisted guests need?

Decision Hotel information Restaurant information
Does it fit? Location, room configuration, accessible features and arrival arrangements. Menu, atmosphere, seating, group size and staff contact for dietary questions.
What will it cost? Date-specific total, inclusions, fees and holds. Menu prices, deposits, minimum spend and service-charge policy.
Can plans change? Cancellation deadline, local time zone and rate restrictions. Cancellation window, late arrival and no-show terms.
What happens next? Booking confirmation, special-request process and human support. Reservation confirmation, order status and human support.
The common requirement is confidence at each step. Images communicate the atmosphere; clear text establishes the conditions under which that experience can be delivered.

A 30-day plan for better hotel and restaurant AI discovery

  1. Week 1: identify ten real guest decisions. Review reservation questions and support conversations with appropriate privacy controls. Choose questions that determine whether someone books.
  2. Week 2: publish the missing answers. Assign an owner to menus, room attributes, policies and pricing explanations. Add useful contextual photos and descriptive image text.
  3. Week 3: reconcile your public information. Compare your organization’s website with your booking platform, restaurant profile and business listings. Resolve contradictions at their source.
  4. Week 4: test complete scenarios. Record the question, system, date, sources and factual errors. Test whether a guest can reach the correct booking path and understand the terms.
Measure successful answers, unresolved questions, handoff completion and confirmed bookings where attribution is available. An AI mention is an early signal, not proof of revenue.Google says its AI search features use the foundations of ordinary SEO: accessible text, useful internal links, accurate structured data and a good page experience. There is no special markup that guarantees inclusion. [4]

AI hotel and restaurant discovery: frequently asked questions

Can AI agents book hotels and restaurants? Some systems support booking actions through specific integrations or browser workflows. Others only provide research or a handoff. Availability depends on the platform, venue, market and authorization. Verify the supported path and the final confirmation.
Will AI replace hotel and restaurant websites? Websites remain useful sources of business facts, policies, experience descriptions and booking routes. Their role can extend from attracting human visitors to supporting research performed with AI. Keep important information accessible in text and consistent with your transaction systems.
How can a hotel or restaurant improve AI discovery? Answer specific guest questions, publish current room or menu details, reconcile public listings and explain booking restrictions. Test whether an assistant retrieves accurate facts and whether the guest can reach the appropriate reservation path.
