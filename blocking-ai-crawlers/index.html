Does blocking AI crawlers protect my content or erase my business?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Blocking retrieval crawlers erases you from the answers. Training crawlers are a separate decision you can make without that cost. cloro studied 1,058 domains in July 2026: the median site that blocked GPTBot earned 0.003 ChatGPT citations per Google ranking, against 0.417 for sites that allowed it. Sites blocking PerplexityBot earned a median of zero on Perplexity. A Rutgers and Wharton study found sites blocking through robots.txt lost 23.1 percent of total traffic. OpenAI, Anthropic and others now publish separate crawlers for training, search indexing and live user requests, so a business can say no to training and yes to being found. At Vivander Advisors I recommend exactly that split, and I check it on every audit.

The instinct to protect your content is reasonable. How most sites act on it is self-erasure.

The security default that deleted a hotel

A hotel's web host enabled Block AI Bots in 2024. It was a default. Nobody at the hotel chose it.

The property was invisible on ChatGPT and Perplexity for eighteen months while its rankings held. Nobody looked, because nothing was broken.

That is what a retrieval block looks like from inside. Green dashboards. Empty answers.

The six categories, because a block means six different things

Training crawlers collect content to train models: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Meta-ExternalAgent, Amazonbot.

Search-index crawlers build the index the assistant retrieves from: OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot, Bingbot, Applebot, Meta-WebIndexer, Amzn-SearchBot, MistralAI-Index, DuckAssistBot.

User-triggered fetchers retrieve a page because a person asked: ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User, Google-Agent, Meta-ExternalFetcher, Amzn-User, MistralAI-User.

Advertising validators check ad landing pages: OAI-AdsBot, Meta-ExternalAds.

Opt-out tokens are not crawlers at all. Google-Extended governs Gemini training and grounding and has no user agent.

And a set of fetchers are observed and undocumented, including the claude-code fetcher, which Ahrefs' 2026 logs show as the second-largest AI fetcher by volume and which no robots rule can govern.

Publishers already draw the line where the evidence points. 17.8 percent block CCBot, 15.4 percent ClaudeBot, 13.9 percent GPTBot. 3.4 percent block OAI-SearchBot.

Does a training block affect whether AI cites me?

Not directly. Citations come from live retrieval through the search-index and user-triggered crawlers.

Licensing deals do not drive citation either. In a study of roughly 4 million citations, the Associated Press appeared in zero Gemini results despite Google's announced deal to feed AP content into Gemini. Training rights and retrieval are largely separate pipelines, and no AI company documents licensing status as a retrieval input.

What is the Cloudflare change and why does it matter?

From 15 September 2026, Cloudflare evaluates multi-purpose crawlers under a most-restrictive rule. Cloudflare's own post names the casualties: Googlebot, Applebot and Bingbot will be blocked by customers who selected Block Training, including through the legacy Block AI Bots service.

Enable that toggle once, ever, and you start blocking Google on that date unless you opt out. No error. The default itself is narrow, new domains and ad-bearing pages only. This is not a blanket block. It is an old training decision quietly becoming a search decision.

Cloudflare also shipped Content Signals in July 2026, a robots.txt line stating permitted uses: search, AI training, and a use field whose default, reference, means index, excerpt and link back. That default was auto-added to managed robots.txt customers, so its presence is often inherited rather than chosen. Establish which.

Can I control Copilot, Gemini or Apple separately?

Partly. Microsoft has no AI-specific robots token; Copilot control runs through meta tags. Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode ride the normal Googlebot index and cannot be opted out of separately from search. Google-Extended controls Gemini training and grounding only. Apple publishes Applebot-Extended as its training opt-out while Applebot keeps feeding Siri. The IETF's AI preferences work, which would standardize all of this, has an expired draft, no consensus and no vendor implementation.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Allow every search-index and user-triggered crawler by name. Decide on training crawlers separately. Check the Cloudflare console today.

For your role. Whoever set the security default was doing their job. The person measuring visibility was doing theirs. Nobody was doing the job between them. That job exists now.

For your guests. They asked. The engine came to your door and found it locked. It did not knock twice.

What I do not know

Whether the observed, undocumented fetchers, including coding agents, will ever be robots-addressable. Today they are not, and I record them as observed traffic rather than governable crawlers.

Where this is going

The next penalty that breaks your AI visibility may not come from Google. It may come from your own partners: a host, a CDN, a security vendor making a defensible choice on your behalf.

Content protection and content discovery used to be the same setting. They have split. The businesses that notice the split get to choose. Those that do not get chosen for.
