Your Hotel Appeared in an AI Answer. Ask Again.

Michael J. Goldrich · October 13, 2026

Why repeated questions and controlled testing give hotel leaders a clearer picture of AI visibility.

A hotel general manager asks ChatGPT for a quiet hotel near the convention center. Her property appears in the answer. She sends the screenshot to the marketing team.

The next morning, the revenue director asks the same question. The hotel is missing. Three competitors appear instead.

Both screenshots are real. Neither establishes how consistently the hotel gets recommended.

That distinction matters when the next conversation involves a marketing budget.

The answer can change even when the question does not

A calculator gives you the same answer to the same calculation. That is the expectation most of us bring to business software: hold the inputs steady and the result should repeat.

Generative AI does not reliably behave that way. Its outputs can vary between requests, even when the wording stays the same. OpenAI documents this variability and describes controls that can improve reproducibility without guaranteeing identical results. OpenAI’s explanation of reproducibility.

For a hotel, that means appearing in one answer does not establish a dependable position. Missing from one answer does not establish invisibility.

The business question becomes: How consistently does the property appear when travelers ask questions it should be relevant to?

One screenshot cannot answer that.

One prompt, asked three times

Consider this illustrative question:

“Which hotels near the convention center have quiet rooms and on-site parking?”

Run it once and your hotel appears. That tells you the platform recommended the property on that occasion.

Run the same question in three separate, fresh sessions. Your hotel appears in the first answer, disappears from the second, and returns in the third. A competitor appears in all three.

Now you have a more useful finding. Your hotel appeared in two of three observed answers. The competitor appeared consistently across this small sample.

You can also compare the descriptions. Did the AI mention your parking in every answer where you appeared? Did it repeatedly describe your rooms as quiet? Did the same source support those statements?

The overlap helps reveal what persists. The differences reveal what is unstable.

Keep both.

An attribute appearing in all three answers deserves attention. So does an inaccurate parking fee repeated three times. Consistency measures repetition; the hotel’s actual policies establish accuracy.

Three runs give you a better starting point for investigation. They remain a small sample, and further cycles help show whether the pattern holds.

Your everyday account brings its own context

The general manager’s test may carry another complication: she is asking from an account she uses every day.

She may already have discussed the hotel, its renovation, its competitors and its ideal guests. Saved memory, conversation history or custom instructions may influence the response. Location can matter too. OpenAI explains that memory can help shape search queries and that location information can influence search results. Memory in ChatGPT, ChatGPT search.

A traveler arriving from another market may encounter a different answer.

Opening a new conversation is a useful precaution, but it does not by itself establish that every relevant account setting or contextual influence has been removed.

Your own account is useful for exploring questions and spotting problems. A business measurement needs conditions you can describe and repeat.

Control the conditions you can. Record the rest.

A well-designed visibility monitoring service establishes a consistent testing protocol.

It keeps the question wording fixed. It uses fresh sessions and a documented approach to memory and personalization. It defines the location being tested, records the platform and available model information, and preserves the answers and citations.

Location deserves particular care. A prompt asking about Boston does not, by itself, prove that the request was made from Boston. The location stated in the question and the location inferred from the connection are separate conditions. Where a platform exposes geographic controls, the monitoring process should document them. Where it does not, that limitation belongs beside the result.

No outside service controls every variable inside an AI provider. Models change. Search results change. Providers may use routing, experiments or other settings the tester cannot see.

The value of controlled monitoring is that it reduces avoidable variation and makes the remaining uncertainty visible.

That gives the hotel a stronger basis for comparison.

Repetition becomes useful when it leads to a decision

Suppose your property appears consistently for “hotels near the convention center” but rarely for “quiet hotels near the convention center.”

That is a more specific problem than “our AI visibility is low.”

The next step is to examine the evidence. Do your room descriptions explain which rooms face the courtyard? Do reviews discuss noise? Do OTA listings describe the same features as your website? Which sources appear in the answers that recommend competing properties?

Now the team has something concrete to investigate and improve.

The same process can uncover a different problem: the hotel appears frequently, but the description is wrong. Visibility alone would make that look like success. Reading the captured answers reveals the work still required.

A useful monitoring service connects the measurement to those decisions. It shows the questions, the answers, the sources and the changes over time.

Why Vivander repeats the measurement

Vivander’s monitoring methodology asks each core question three times per weekly cycle, in separate passes, and evaluates results over four completed cycles.

That produces twelve observations for each question on each platform in the reporting window.

The first three observations provide an early view of consistency. The subsequent cycles help show whether that pattern persists. Preserving the underlying answers makes it possible to investigate movement instead of guessing at what changed.

This also makes the measurement more useful than a collection of personal screenshots. The questions follow a defined protocol, the observations accumulate, and the team can return to the evidence behind a result.

The measure still has boundaries. It describes performance across the selected questions, platforms and testing conditions. It does not represent every traveler’s experience or prove that a visibility change produced additional bookings.

Within those boundaries, it gives the hotel a clearer view of where it appears, how it is described and where competitors are being offered instead.

Give the screenshot a second question

The next time someone sends an AI answer showing your hotel, keep it.

Then ask what happens when the same question is tested again under comparable conditions.

Does the property return? Does the description remain accurate? Do the sources change? Does the pattern persist into the next cycle?

Those questions turn an encouraging screenshot into the beginning of useful evidence.

For hotel leaders deciding where to invest time and money, that is the benefit of visibility monitoring: a repeatable way to understand what is happening, investigate why, and judge whether the work is making a difference.
