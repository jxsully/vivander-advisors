AI Agents Can Now Book Hotels. Is Your Brand Even Visible?

Michael J. Goldrich · July 21, 2025

Something fundamental just changed in the world of travel marketing, and most brands haven’t realized it yet.

OpenAI has released ChatGPT Agent Mode, a feature that moves AI from passive assistant to active participant in the customer journey. This is more than just answering questions or summarizing reviews anymore. ChatGPT Agent Mode can now browse websites, log into platforms, compare prices, click buttons, add products to carts, and most critically, complete bookings.

In other words, AI is no longer just recommending your hotel. It can now book your competitor.

This shift is happening now. Rolling out to paid ChatGPT users across markets, Agent Mode allows travelers to ask for a hotel recommendation and have ChatGPT find options and also complete the reservation directly on platforms like Expedia or Booking.com.

For travel marketers, this is a restructuring of the customer journey itself.

Historically, booking a hotel was a multi-step process. A traveler searched on Google, browsed websites, checked reviews, opened OTA apps, compared rates, toggled between tabs, and eventually made a booking. That entire research loop was what marketers optimized for through SEO, paid ads, meta search, and loyalty programs.

Now, that funnel is collapsing.

A traveler can simply tell ChatGPT what they want, such as a boutique hotel in Paris near the Eiffel Tower under $300 a night with breakfast included. The AI will then browse, evaluate, select, and book without the traveler ever touching a website.

The implications are massive and immediate.

If your brand isn’t visible to the AI agent, or if your content isn’t structured in ways AI systems can read, parse, and trust, then you’re invisible to the traveler. Your booking funnel doesn’t just shrink. It disappears.

What marketers must recognize is that ChatGPT Agent Mode isn’t functioning like traditional search. It’s functioning like a decision-maker. If your hotel, resort, or tour isn’t showing up as part of the AI’s evaluated options, your customer never even knows you exist. There’s no second chance. No retargeting opportunity. The booking happens silently while your team measures impressions and click-through rates from last quarter.

Why is this happening? Because most marketers are still optimizing for human searchers using traditional browsers. They’re chasing keyword rankings, backlinks, and on-page SEO, but those factors mean little to AI agents scanning structured data, property descriptions, and trusted content feeds.

It’s not enough to say your brand ranks on Google. The better question is whether AI agents can find and understand your offer well enough to recommend and book it.

Let me be clear. ChatGPT isn’t scraping your blog. It is evaluating structured content, using schema markup, reading property descriptions, parsing rates and availability, and navigating booking engines in the same way a customer would. The difference is that it does all of this faster and without distractions.

For marketers, this demands a mindset shift. Content strategy must evolve from general SEO to something called Answer Engine Optimization. This means crafting content that doesn’t just appeal to human readers but also meets the direct needs of AI agents completing tasks on behalf of travelers.

This involves making sure your property details are clear, concise, and consistent across all online platforms, from your website to OTAs to your Google Business Profile. Structured data, such as schema markup, becomes non-negotiable. Without it, your rates, amenities, and location data are invisible to the systems now driving bookings.

Fluffy brand storytelling must be paired with factual, accessible information. If your website opens with vague taglines like “Experience the unexpected” without clearly stating that you’re a beachfront resort with family villas and two swimming pools, AI agents will bypass you.

But the problem goes deeper than content. Most marketing teams are not equipped to handle this shift. Many still see AI as a curiosity rather than a capability. They lack the literacy to understand how these agents function, how content is processed, and how visibility is determined. Without that understanding, they’re left optimizing for a world that is already changing around them.

Consider your current marketing reports. You’re tracking website traffic, social impressions, and keyword rankings. But how many times last month did ChatGPT or Google’s AI Overview recommend your brand? How often did AI agents evaluate your property but exclude you from the final selection? You probably don’t know. And that’s the problem.

Visibility is becoming a hidden challenge.

And the solution isn’t creating more content. The solution is creating structured, factual content that AI agents can trust and use. This must be paired with marketing teams trained to think the way AI systems do.

Because visibility today isn’t earned through ads or clever campaigns. Visibility depends on being the answer.

As travel marketers, we have to stop thinking about capturing attention and start focusing on earning trust from machines that act on behalf of our customers. This requires rethinking content workflows, retraining teams, and shifting success metrics away from vanity indicators like clicks and likes. Instead, marketers must track meaningful visibility measures such as AI answer appearances and access within booking funnels.

Now, your competitor isn’t just the hotel down the street. It’s every brand that has optimized its presence to appear in AI agents’ booking recommendations.

What’s most concerning is that this shift is invisible to many brands. Bookings might decline without explanation. OTAs might suddenly outperform direct channels. Your well-designed website might stop driving conversions because AI agents bypass it entirely.

This isn’t a future scenario. It’s unfolding now.

Otherwise, the next booking your AI assistant completes won’t be for your property. And you won’t even know you lost it.
