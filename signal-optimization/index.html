Signal optimization: how AI finds, recommends and cites a business

A business needs more than a website that says the right things. The public evidence surrounding the business needs to make its identity, relevance and credibility clear.

From SEO and GEO to ongoing signal optimization

The work of helping businesses appear in AI answers has been discussed under several names: search engine optimization (SEO), answer engine optimization (AEO), and generative engine optimization (GEO). SEO addresses visibility in search; AEO emphasizes answering questions; GEO focuses on visibility in answers generated by AI. These practices overlap. Google treats optimization for its generative search features as part of SEO, while the original GEO research studies visibility within generated responses. [12] [4]

GEO is the broader discipline. Vivander organizes the work into two connected parts: establish the foundation, then continually strengthen and maintain the signals around the business. That second part is where signal optimization gives leaders a more concrete description of the work.

  1. Build the foundation. Make the business discoverable and understandable through accessible pages, a clear identity, useful content, accurate core facts and relevant structured data.
  2. Keep the evidence current and credible. Maintain listings and policies, publish substantiated developments, earn relevant independent coverage, inspect the sources appearing in AI answers and correct contradictions as the business changes.

The foundation makes the business legible. Ongoing signal optimization keeps the public evidence aligned with what the business offers now. The two parts reinforce each other: a new service may require a new page, while a recurring error may expose a gap in the original foundation.

“Signal optimization” is useful because it names what a team can act on: the facts, sources and evidence surrounding the business. GEO names the broader objective. Signal optimization makes the continuing work easier to explain, assign and review within that objective.

Current does not always mean recent. A long-established qualification or a detailed older review may remain relevant. A new page with unsupported claims adds little useful evidence. The aim is accuracy, relevance and credibility, with freshness where the customer’s decision depends on it.

What is signal optimization?

Signal optimization is Vivander’s term for improving the public information and supporting evidence that help AI systems discover a business, understand what it offers, assess its fit for a customer’s request, and represent it accurately in an answer.

Those signals can live on the business website, in search indexes, business listings, product feeds, reviews, professional profiles, industry publications and other sources. The work includes correcting contradictions, explaining specific capabilities, publishing useful evidence and maintaining current business information.

The objective is qualified visibility: appearing for customer needs the business can actually meet, with facts that stand up when a customer checks them. A recommendation based on an imaginary amenity or an outdated service is a poor outcome, even when the business gets named.

Within GEO, Vivander uses signal optimization to connect continuing work across marketing, operations, public relations and website management. The eight groups below cover both the foundations that need checking and the evidence that needs ongoing attention. This is a practical framework, not a metric published by an AI platform or a claim to a proprietary ranking formula.

Being found, mentioned, recommended and cited are different outcomes

Business visibility becomes easier to diagnose when each outcome has its own definition.

A restaurant might be recommended because a dining publication describes its private room. The publication receives the citation. A hotel might be named from a model’s existing knowledge without a current source link. A consultancy’s research might be cited in an educational answer that never recommends hiring the consultancy.

AI answers can combine model knowledge with newly retrieved information. When web search is used, systems may rewrite a customer’s question into several searches, retrieve candidates and select supporting material. Anthropic describes this search-and-citation process for Claude; OpenAI also documents query rewriting and the influence of location in ChatGPT search. Neither explanation supplies a universal business selection formula. [1] [2]

The diagnostic question changes with the problem. An inaccessible page needs an access fix. An accessible page that never addresses the customer’s actual need needs better information. An inaccurate recommendation needs source correction. More content is not automatically the answer to all three.

What the research supports

Platform documentation establishes the foundations

Google says its AI search features use existing search foundations and may issue related searches through query fan-out. Its guidance emphasizes useful content, technical accessibility and current business information. OpenAI describes search ranking in terms of relevance and reliability and says placement is not guaranteed. [3] [2]

Experiments show possibilities, not promised business results

The KDD 2024 paper GEO: Generative Engine Optimization tested changes including source citations, quotations and statistics. Some methods improved visibility in its evaluated settings; results varied by domain and measurement. The widely repeated “up to 40%” finding is an experimental visibility result, not a forecast of additional customers, revenue or citations for a particular business. Relevant evidence belongs in content because it helps substantiate an answer, not because adding a statistic earns a fixed boost. [4]

Newer studies separate retrieval from citation

An October 2026 preprint by Chen and Koudas examined 158,099 answers and 468,037 citations across two commercial markets. It found that matching questions in meaning and entering the retrieved set mattered substantially. Some apparent benefits of FAQs and structured data weakened when comparing pages within the same website. Its findings also distinguish remembered brand mentions from retrieved page citations. This is early research in particular markets, not an independently established rule for every platform. [5]

A separate October 2026 observational preprint studied several ChatGPT Web settings. It found variation in retrieval behavior and showed that external search and API proxies recovered only a portion of the exact URLs cited in the web product. The practical implication is to test the actual customer-facing experience rather than assume a Google result or an API test reproduces it. [6]

Evidence rule: platform documentation identifies published requirements and guidance; studies describe results within their methods; the actions below are Vivander’s recommendations informed by that evidence. The eight groups are not eight disclosed algorithm weights.

Eight signal groups worth optimizing

01 · Access and discovery

Can the relevant system reach the information?

Before information can help an answer through retrieval, it needs a route into that system. Google’s documentation addresses indexing and snippet eligibility. OpenAI identifies OAI-SearchBot as its search crawler; GPTBot controls a separate training use. Perplexity likewise distinguishes its search crawler from training and documents firewall access requirements. [3] [7] [8]

Optimize: inspect important URLs for accidental indexing restrictions, blocked crawlers, broken internal links, failed responses and firewall challenges. Confirm what legitimate crawlers actually receive. Check the property’s Search generative AI control in Google Search Console, including inherited exclusions. Google documents this as a separate inclusion control, with inclusion the default for top-level properties. Put essential business facts in accessible page text, and make priority pages easy to reach from relevant navigation and content. [15]

Allowing a crawler creates an opportunity for retrieval. A server log showing a visit does not establish that the page was indexed, used or cited.

02 · Business identity

Is it clear which business the information describes?

Similar names, former brands, multiple locations and disconnected product identities can make public information difficult to reconcile. Google documents Organization structured data as a way to help disambiguate an organization. That is a concrete identity use; it does not establish a universal AI recommendation benefit. [9]

Optimize: maintain a canonical business record with the current name, website, contact details, locations, parent relationships and official profiles. Explain former names and branch differences. Use relevant Organization or LocalBusiness markup that agrees with visible information. Link official profiles where appropriate.

Consistency means agreement on facts. A directory description and a homepage can use different language while clearly describing the same organization.

03 · Fit for the customer’s request

Does the business explain the details behind a match?

“A memorable experience” does little to answer whether a hotel has connecting rooms, whether a restaurant can host 18 people privately, or whether an adviser trains frontline managers. Query rewriting and semantic retrieval make the customer’s underlying need a better planning unit than a repeated keyword. [2] [5]

Optimize: identify priority buyer situations and publish the facts needed to evaluate them: audience, location, capacity, service scope, price basis, eligibility, exclusions and alternatives. Answer related questions together when that produces a coherent page.

Explain boundaries as clearly as benefits. A property that permits dogs only in selected rooms should describe those limits. Specific information supports a suitable match and prevents an unsuitable one.

04 · Evidence and expertise

Can important claims be checked?

Original findings, documented experience, clear authorship and a transparent method give readers grounds to evaluate claims. Google’s helpful-content guidance discusses experience, expertise, authority and trust, while clarifying that E-E-A-T is not itself a specific ranking factor. [10]

Optimize: replace broad superiority claims with verifiable evidence. Publish case studies with dates, scope, denominators and limitations; explain who performed the work and how. Attribute external facts to their original sources. Include relevant qualifications without turning an author biography into an unsupported claim of authority.

A “40% improvement” needs a definition of what improved, compared with what, over which period. A quotation needs an identifiable source and accurate context. An AI citation cannot repair weak underlying evidence.

05 · Independent reputation and corroboration

What does the wider web say about the business?

Useful business information may appear in independent reporting, customer reviews, association listings and specialist directories. Google’s local ranking guidance explicitly discusses relevance, distance and popularity, including reviews and links. Those are local search factors; their precise influence should not be transferred to every AI assistant. [11]

Optimize: prioritize accurate profiles and substantive coverage in sources customers already consult. Correct wrong facts on pages that appear in relevant answers. Encourage honest reviews under the platform’s rules, respond constructively and earn coverage through useful expertise or newsworthy work.

Ten copied biographies do not provide ten independent validations. Treat paid placements as paid placements. A platform’s willingness to retrieve a page is not proof that the page is accurate or that its endorsement is independent.

06 · Clarity and usability

Can a reader understand a claim without guessing?

Descriptive headings, plain language and clearly labeled comparisons help people evaluate information. They also make a page’s subject and evidence easier to identify. Research does not justify a universal rule that every paragraph must contain a certain number of words or that every page needs FAQs.

Optimize: state the central answer early, identify the business or service explicitly, and keep qualifications beside the claims they limit. Use tables for genuine comparisons. Provide descriptive image text and useful captions. Make menus, specifications and policies readable without depending on an image alone.

For a hotel, “Parking available” leaves material questions open. “Valet parking costs $45 per night; oversized vehicles require advance confirmation” gives a customer information to evaluate. This illustrative wording is useful because of its substance, not a special AI sentence pattern.

07 · Freshness and agreement

Do the facts remain accurate across important sources?

Hours, menus, policies, prices and availability can change faster than editorial content. Google recommends maintaining Business Profile information, and Perplexity documents user-triggered fetching separately from crawling. Different retrieval paths can encounter different versions of a fact. [3] [8]

Optimize: assign an owner and review interval to facts that affect customer decisions. Update the website, major listings and distribution partners when those facts change. Remove obsolete downloads, explain effective dates where needed and investigate the source of repeated errors.

A new date attached to unchanged copy does not make the information fresher. An incorrect pet policy on a prominent booking profile needs correction at that source as well as on the hotel website.

08 · Transaction details and the next action

Can the customer act on a suitable recommendation?

Discovery is only one part of the customer journey. A customer may need a live reservation time, an available product variant or a clear way to request a proposal. Google’s newer AI guidance discusses Business Profiles, Merchant Center and emerging agentic experiences. These capabilities depend on the platform and business category. [12]

Optimize: keep supported inventory and booking integrations accurate, publish material terms, and check the path from a recommendation to the actual reservation, purchase or inquiry. For professional services, explain deliverables, fit and the next step.

Transaction readiness is a separate objective from earning an editorial citation. A working booking route helps complete a customer’s task; it should not be described as a proven universal citation factor.

What signal optimization looks like in practice

The examples below are hypothetical applications of the framework, not client results.

A hotel missing from family travel recommendations

The hotel calls itself family friendly, but connecting-room details are buried in a PDF and a booking profile lists an obsolete minimum check-in age. The first work is to make room configurations and restrictions visible, correct the conflicting profile, and explain which arrangements require confirmation. Then test family travel questions again and inspect whether the answers use accurate facts. The goal is a defensible match for a particular family’s needs.

A restaurant mentioned with the wrong private dining capacity

An old dining guide says the private room seats 30, while the current capacity is 18. Publishing another general article about celebrations leaves the contradiction intact. Update the restaurant’s event page, contact the guide with the correction, and check other sources that repeat the number. Distinguish seated capacity, standing capacity and any minimum spend.

An advisory firm known for a broad category

The firm appears for AI consulting but not for the particular work it performs. Replace vague service descriptions with the intended audience, work delivered, engagement boundaries and evidence of relevant experience. Publish an original explanation or case study that helps buyers assess the service. Track educational citations separately from recommendations to hire the firm.

A practical 90-day signal optimization plan

This is a suggested operating cadence, not a promise that an AI system will respond within 90 days. Correct high-impact errors as soon as they are found.

Period Work Reviewable output
Days 1–15 Choose 20–30 buyer questions; establish repeated baseline observations; inspect priority pages and recurring sources. Question set, saved answers, source map and error register.
Days 16–30 Fix access problems, material factual errors and inconsistent business identity. Verified corrections with URLs, owners and change dates.
Days 31–60 Improve pages for priority buyer needs; publish substantiated evidence; correct relevant external profiles. Clear service or product pages, supporting evidence and a record of external corrections.
Days 61–90 Repeat the same observations; examine accuracy, recommendation patterns and customer actions. A comparison with limitations and a prioritized next work cycle.

Prioritize by customer harm, commercial importance, recurrence and ability to fix the source. An incorrect accessibility statement or cancellation policy usually deserves attention before a cosmetic content change. Give each item an accountable owner; marketing cannot correct a distribution feed without the team that manages it.

A useful work item reads: “The private dining answer repeats a capacity of 30 from this guide. Events will confirm the current capacity, marketing will update the page and request the guide correction, and the analyst will repeat the recorded questions after source updates are visible.”

Measure business visibility without mixing the outcomes

Use a stable question set, record platform and mode, location, date, search status and relevant conversation context, and repeat observations. Keep branded questions separate from unbranded discovery questions. A prompt that includes the business name tests a different opportunity from a prompt asking for suitable businesses.

  • Mention rate: evaluated answers naming the business divided by evaluated answers in the defined sample.
  • Recommendation rate: evaluated answers presenting the business as a suitable option divided by evaluated answers in that sample. Define inclusion rules before counting.
  • Owned-site citation rate: evaluated answers linking to the business’s domain divided by evaluated answers in the sample.
  • Third-party support: separately record sources cited for claims about the business, including whether those sources support the claim.
  • Accuracy: verified business claims that are correct divided by verified business claims checked. Keep unknown claims separate from errors.
  • Customer actions: qualified inquiries, reservations or purchases observed through analytics and business records, with the limits of attribution stated.

For illustration, 12 mentions and six recommendations in 30 evaluated answers produce a 40% mention rate and a 20% recommendation rate for that sample. Those percentages do not estimate all customer searches, and six recommendations do not mean six customers.

Where available, publisher tools add another view. Bing Webmaster Tools’ AI Performance report tracks visible citations and grounding queries across supported Microsoft and partner experiences. It describes aggregated, incomplete observations rather than an explanation of why a page was selected. Google also documents a Generative AI performance report; use the reporting available for the property and retain each report’s scope. [13] [14]

Do not infer causation from a before-and-after rise alone. A platform update, a changed question mix or normal answer variation may affect results. Record intervention dates and competing changes. A comparison group of unchanged questions or pages can improve interpretation, though it cannot remove every source of uncertainty.

Shortcuts that deserve skepticism

A universal signal score. An internal audit score can help prioritize work, but its weights belong to the tool or adviser. It is not an AI platform’s hidden evaluation.

Special files as a visibility guarantee. Google explicitly says its search systems do not use llms.txt as a special optimization mechanism. Maintain such a file only for a specific service that uses it; do not treat it as a substitute for accessible, useful information. [12]

Schema or FAQs as automatic citation boosts. Use accurate markup for supported purposes and FAQs for real questions. Their presence alone does not establish that an AI answer will select the business.

Mass mentions and manufactured authority. Repeating promotional claims across weak sites does not create independent proof. Google’s current guidance also cautions against inauthentic mentions and mass-produced pages aimed at manipulating AI responses. [12]

Guaranteed inclusion. No business controls the questions customers ask, every source retrieved or the answer an independent platform generates. The controllable work is the quality, accuracy, availability and relevance of the public evidence.

Common questions about signal optimization

Is signal optimization different from SEO?

It overlaps substantially. SEO supports discovery through search; signal optimization organizes work on the business facts and evidence that may inform AI answers across multiple sources. It also tracks mentions, recommendations, citations and factual accuracy separately.

Does allowing AI training improve search visibility?

Search access and training permissions are different controls. OpenAI explicitly separates OAI-SearchBot from GPTBot. A business can allow its search crawler while declining that training use. Do not change training permissions on the assumption that doing so guarantees a citation. [7]

Do reviews influence every AI recommendation?

Reviews are relevant public evidence and documented components of Google local ranking. The weight, retrieval and use of reviews vary by platform and query. There is no justified universal review count or star rating that guarantees an AI recommendation.

Can a business be recommended without its website being cited?

Yes. The supporting source may be a third party, and brand mentions can also arise from existing model knowledge. Record the recommendation and the source relationship separately.

How quickly should improvements appear?

There is no dependable universal timeline. Source updates, crawling, indexing and answer generation operate on different schedules. Verify that corrections are visible at the source, then repeat the defined observations over time.
