In early autumn, a corporate gifting manager starts planning the holidays. She asks an AI assistant for custom logo chocolates and adds two conditions: a minimum order she can meet and a lead time she can plan around. The assistant answers with a comparison table. Each row lists a chocolatier, its minimum, and its turnaround.The chocolatier in this paper runs exactly that program. It produces custom corporate gifts, ships to multiple addresses, supplies luxury hotels with turndown and welcome amenities, has made custom chocolate for a major museum and a major sporting event, and holds a national business magazine’s selection for best chocolate gift box. Its name was not in the table. On that question, and on a broader one about the leading chocolatiers for custom logo gifts, none of six AI platforms named it at all.Nobody at the company saw that table. The corporate inquiry form records the buyers who wrote in. It cannot record the ones who chose from a table the company was never in.Everything that follows comes from a real Vivander audit, collected in the summer of 2026. The company, its city, its neighborhood, and its competitors are not named. Every figure comes directly from the audit.

Why should a luxury retailer assess AI visibility?

The proposal holds for a retailer as it does for a hotel or a restaurant. Commission a baseline audit built around the buyers the business most wants to grow. Ask their questions on six AI platforms, three times each, without ever naming the brand. Compare the answers with the competitors who win them. Turn the gaps into a scoped set of fixes, then re-test the same questions.This audit wrote 68 questions in the words of the company’s own buyers, none of them mentioning the brand, plus 22 questions that did. Each ran three times on each platform: 1,620 recorded answers, with their sources.

The business had earned broad recognition in these tests. The next question was whether its public pages supplied the evidence its target buyers needed.

Which website gaps matched the missing buyer information?

The audit found gaps between the information buyers asked for and the facts published on the website. Competitors’ pages stated relevant details such as minimums and turnaround times. These observations identify correction priorities, without proving the cause of a recommendation.Exhibit 3

What the buyer needs What the audit found
Minimum order Not stated. One platform, asked about the brand’s minimum by name, quoted a figure it took from a third-party reseller.

Evidence on the pages Of 20 pages scored, 19 cite no source, 18 quote nobody, and none compares the brand with an alternative.
Real audit result, anonymized.Hotel amenity questions exposed an important gap: eleven answers contained no mention of the brand, although the source audit confirmed an existing program. A fuller, readable program page was a practical correction to test.Publishing a verified fact is a concrete first step a business can own and re-test.The audit also found an answer nobody in the category holds. Asked for the top places for afternoon tea citywide, no platform named any chocolatier. Hotel tea rooms owned that answer. On the neighborhood version of the same question, this brand won outright. Its tea service is retrievable for other questions. The pricing lives in a PDF menu, and no reservation exists in a form a machine can read.

Which corrections did the audit prioritize?

Vivander scoped the fixes as a single website project, ordered by the size of the gap each one closes.Exhibit 5

Real audit output, anonymized.Every fix on that list publishes something already true. None requires a new product, a new program, or a single new customer relationship. The company had built the business AI was looking for. It had not yet described that business in words a machine could quote.

How will monitoring track what changes?

Baseline and planned checks, not completed improvement results. Re-tests had not run when the source case was prepared. Repeated answers can show change; they cannot on their own establish which correction caused it.
The corporate, event and hotel questions form a baseline for the website work. Repeat the same questions on the same selected platforms and retain the new answers to see which patterns persist or change. Treat any explanation of why an answer changed as a separate question to investigate.Exhibit 6

What are the limits of this audit?

Read the findings within their test conditions. These are observations of platform answers, not evidence of lost or recovered sales, guest behavior or a guaranteed recommendation.
The test describes what six platforms said in the summer of 2026 to questions the audit chose. It counts answers. Orders fall outside its scope, and so does proof of why a competitor was recommended instead.

How should your organization start?

Name the buyers the business most wants to grow. Write the questions they actually ask, without the brand name in them. Find out who gets the answer today and what those competitors publish that your organization does not. Then publish what is already true.Luxury brands spend years earning the kind of recognition AI already grants this one. Recognition was never the obstacle. The facts that turn a famous name into a placed order were sitting in the company’s own files, waiting for someone to write them down.

About the audit evidence

Exhibits 1 through 6 report results from a real Vivander Visibility Audit of a luxury chocolatier with a flagship boutique, a corporate gifting program, and a hotel amenity program. Answers were collected in the summer of 2026 from ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and Google AI Mode. The company, its location, its competitors, the hotels and institutions it serves, and other identifying details are withheld.The audit asked 68 questions in the words of the company’s buyers, none naming the brand, plus 22 questions about the brand by name. Each question ran three times on each platform, and a platform counted as naming the brand only when it did so in at least two of three runs. The audit also reviewed twenty pages of the company’s website, swept eleven types of independent sources, and walked the real purchase, reservation, and corporate inquiry paths.Audit results describe what the tested platforms said on the test dates. They do not measure how many buyers saw those answers, estimate lost revenue, or predict future recommendations.
