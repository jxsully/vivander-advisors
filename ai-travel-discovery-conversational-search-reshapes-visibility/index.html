AI Travel Discovery: Conversational Search Reshapes Visibility

Michael J. Goldrich · February 6, 2026

The Research Phase of AI Travel Discovery Is Already Shifting. Properties That Understand the Answer Spiral Will Capture Demand. Those That Wait for Booking Integration May Find Themselves Invisible.

Something significant happened during Google’s Q4 2025 earnings call on February 4, 2026. CEO Sundar Pichai declared 2026 as the year consumers would finally use all of the AI infrastructure Google has been building. The transactional tools for travel have yet to arrive. The behavioral transformation, however, is already well underway.

This matters enormously for hospitality. The way travelers discover properties, restaurants, and experiences is changing faster than the systems designed to monetize that discovery. Properties that recognize this gap and respond to it will earn visibility during the most critical window in digital hospitality marketing since the advent of online travel agencies. Properties that wait for booking tools to arrive may find themselves optimizing for a system that has already moved past them.

The source material for this analysis comes from Adriana Lee’s reporting in Skift, titled “Google Earnings: AI Search Is Changing Travel Discovery, Even Before Booking Tools Arrive,” published February 4, 2026.

What Is Changing: The Rise of the Answer Spiral

The traditional model of travel research involved a traveler typing a short query into a search bar, reviewing a list of blue links, clicking through to various websites, and repeating this process dozens of times before making a decision. Each search was discrete. Each click was trackable. Each visit to a hotel website represented a measurable moment of engagement.

That model is dissolving.

Google reported during the earnings call that queries in AI mode run three times longer than traditional searches. A significant portion of those queries now produce follow-up questions rather than clicks. Nearly one in six AI mode queries use voice or images rather than text. Google’s Circle to Search feature now reaches 580 million Android devices.

These numbers tell a story about behavior, and the story has a shape. Think of it as the Answer Spiral.

Here is how the Answer Spiral works. A traveler begins with a broad, often visual prompt. Maybe they circle a rooftop pool in an Instagram video using Circle to Search. Google’s AI responds with context about the property. The traveler follows up with a question about room rates. Then another about nearby restaurants. Then another about airport distance. Each question narrows the scope. Each answer tightens the focus.

The traveler may leave the spiral temporarily, checking a review on TikTok, glancing at a friend’s recommendation, or scanning a travel blog. Then they return to the conversation. The spiral continues, pulling the traveler closer to a decision with every exchange.

This differs fundamentally from the old click-based research model. In the Answer Spiral, the traveler stays inside a single conversational thread for longer. The thread absorbs what used to be five, ten, or fifteen separate search sessions. Google confirmed this dynamic when it described AI mode sessions as complex, multi-turn interactions.

The implications for hospitality visibility are immediate. In the old model, a hotel had multiple chances to capture a traveler’s attention across separate searches. In the Answer Spiral, a hotel either appears inside the conversation early or may never appear at all.

Why This Matters: The Traffic Equation Is Changing

Google’s chief business officer Philipp Schindler shared a revealing insight during the call. He noted that Gemini’s understanding of intent has improved Google’s ability to deliver advertising on longer, more complex searches that were previously difficult to monetize. Travel searches often contain layered preferences for dates, destinations, accommodations, and activities. These are exactly the types of queries Google says it can now monetize more effectively.

The company reported that the combination of its Gemini app and traditional search has created an expansionary moment with no cannibalization between the two products. More queries, more engagement, and more time on platform.

For Google, expansion means growth. For hospitality brands, expansion carries a different meaning.

When a single conversational session absorbs what previously required multiple separate searches, Google answers more of the traveler’s questions inside one thread. Fewer questions leave the conversation. Fewer questions generate clicks to hotel websites. The total number of queries grows, but the total number of clicks to external properties may shrink.

This is the central tension. AI travel discovery drives more engagement with travel content overall. It also consolidates that engagement inside fewer, longer sessions. Hotels that appear within those sessions earn attention. Hotels that depend on traditional click-through traffic from individual search results face a fundamentally different competitive environment.

What This Means for Work: New Responsibilities Across Hospitality Teams

The shift from click-based search to conversational AI travel discovery changes the day-to-day responsibilities of people throughout a hospitality organization. This goes well beyond the marketing department.

Revenue Management

Revenue teams have long relied on search volume data to forecast demand. When queries were short and discrete, tracking search volume for specific destinations and property types provided reliable signals. The Answer Spiral changes this. A single long conversation may contain more buying intent than a dozen individual searches, but it generates fewer trackable data points in traditional analytics tools.

Revenue managers need to understand how conversational AI changes the demand signals available to them. This requires new literacy, new measurement frameworks, and a willingness to acknowledge that some of the old indicators may lose predictive power.

Digital Marketing

Marketing teams optimized for clicks. They measured success by cost-per-click, click-through rate, and website visit volume. The Answer Spiral reduces the number of clicks available while potentially increasing the value of each one. A traveler who clicks through from a conversational AI session has already asked and answered multiple questions. They arrive at a hotel website deeper in the decision process than a traveler who clicked through from a simple search result.

This means marketing teams must rethink what they measure. Click volume as the primary metric becomes misleading when fewer, higher-intent clicks replace more numerous, lower-intent ones.

Content and Brand Teams

In the Answer Spiral, AI systems pull from property descriptions, guest reviews, structured data, and published content to generate answers. The quality, clarity, and machine-readability of a hotel’s content determines whether that property enters the conversation.

Content teams move from creating material for human browsers to creating material that both humans and AI systems can interpret and cite. This represents a substantial shift in editorial priorities and skill requirements.

Operations

Front-desk teams, concierge staff, and guest services professionals may see the effects before any digital team does. Guests arriving through AI-mediated discovery often arrive with specific expectations formed by the answers the AI provided. If the AI told a guest the hotel had a rooftop restaurant and the restaurant closed six months ago, the gap between expectation and experience creates friction.

Operations teams need awareness of what AI systems are telling potential guests about their property. This awareness requires new workflows and new forms of cross-departmental communication.

What This Means for Leadership: Designing Capabilities for the Answer Spiral

Google’s agentic travel booking tool, which partners with Booking.com, Expedia, and major hotel groups, remains in development. Google first revealed this initiative in November 2025. Pichai’s recent statement that more capable agents would arrive within twelve months aligns with the timeline for these transactional features.

Alphabet’s planned capital expenditures of $175 billion to $185 billion in 2026 underscore the scale of commitment. This level of investment signals that AI features will continue moving from demonstrations to wide distribution.

For hospitality leadership, the strategic question extends beyond marketing. It becomes a question of capability design.

The organizations best positioned for AI travel discovery will invest in three interconnected capabilities.

AI Literacy Across the Organization. Every team that touches the guest experience or contributes to the property’s digital footprint needs foundational understanding of how AI systems discover, evaluate, and recommend properties. This includes revenue management, operations, content creation, and guest services. Literacy precedes effective action. Without it, teams apply outdated assumptions to a system that operates differently.

Visibility Architecture. Properties need systematic approaches to ensuring their content, data, and digital presence are accessible and interpretable by AI systems. This goes beyond traditional SEO. It includes structured data, machine-readable content, review management, and ongoing monitoring of how AI platforms represent the property. Visibility in AI systems functions as competitive power. Properties that AI can understand and cite earn placement in the Answer Spiral. Properties that remain opaque to AI systems lose placement by default.

Adaptive Measurement. The metrics that defined digital marketing success for two decades require rethinking. Click volume, search ranking position, and traditional conversion funnels were designed for a click-based discovery model. The Answer Spiral demands new measurement approaches that account for conversational engagement, AI citation frequency, and the quality of intent behind fewer, more meaningful interactions.

Why Literacy and Visibility Matter Now

The Window Between Behavior and Infrastructure

The most important strategic insight from Google’s Q4 2025 earnings is the gap between where traveler behavior already exists and where booking infrastructure will eventually arrive.

Traveler behavior has already changed. Queries are longer. Conversations are multi-turn. Visual and voice inputs are growing. The Answer Spiral is happening right now, in millions of travel planning sessions every day.

Booking infrastructure has yet to catch up. Google’s transactional tools, including its protocol for agentic checkout and pilot programs for retailers to surface offers inside AI results, are designed for retail products with fixed prices and standard inventory. Travel’s dynamic pricing and perishable inventory remain incompatible with these systems.

This gap creates a window. Properties that build visibility within AI travel discovery conversations now will have established presence before the transactional layer arrives. When booking tools do launch, the properties already visible inside the Answer Spiral will have a structural advantage over those scrambling to catch up.

Waymo and the Expanding Ecosystem

Google’s autonomous vehicle unit, Waymo, crossed a milestone during the same earnings period. With $16 billion in new funding, Waymo now provides 400,000 paid rides weekly. The service has expanded to airports, with Miami, the United Kingdom, and Japan on the roadmap.

This development connects directly to AI travel discovery. Google is building infrastructure that spans discovery, transactions, and mobility. A traveler who discovers a hotel through AI search, books through an agentic checkout tool, and arrives via Waymo has completed their entire journey within a single company’s products.

The hospitality industry faced a version of this consolidation before, when online travel agencies aggregated discovery and booking into unified platforms. The AI version moves faster and encompasses more of the traveler’s experience.

The Structural Nature of This Shift

AI travel discovery represents a structural transformation in how the hospitality industry connects with potential guests. This distinction matters because structural shifts require structural responses.

Adding an AI chatbot to a hotel website addresses one small piece of the puzzle. Optimizing keywords for a search algorithm that is fundamentally changing addresses another small piece. Neither response matches the scale of what is happening.

The structural response requires organizations to rethink how they create content, how they structure data, how they train teams, how they measure success, and how they position themselves within discovery systems that operate on entirely different principles than the ones that defined digital hospitality for the past two decades.

Literacy comes first because understanding precedes effective action. Visibility follows because presence within AI discovery conversations determines whether a property earns consideration. Together, literacy and visibility form the foundation for every tactical decision that follows.
