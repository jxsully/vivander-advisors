“Vibe Videoing?” Is that even a thing?

Michael J. Goldrich · June 25, 2025

It’s definitely not called that but there is vibe coding and vibe marketing, so with a lack of a “label”, I’ll call it that for now until corrected.

So, I was playing with Google’s Project Mariner to test how it automatically searches the Internet to book a room.

I noticed I also had access to Google’s new Flow with VEO 3 video/audio generation, so I thought, let’s make some videos for the upcoming conferences for HSMAI and Hospitality Financial and Technology Professionals (HFTP®)‘s HITEC in Indianapolis.

I wanted to create videos for Revenue, Sales, Marketing, GMs, and Tech leaders going next week.

What do I know about video generation?



As of two days ago, I didn’t know anything about video/audio generation.

Some might rightfully argue after looking at the below, I still don’t. (NOTE: there are still a lot of misspellings in the videos.)

However, you’ve got to start somewhere, experiment, and learn from your mistakes.

For example, I tried really really hard to make a video of a hotel REVMAX meeting with the the words “Missed Monthly Budget” on the white board and sales, revenue, marketing sitting around a conference table and in the center of the table was a wine bottle with the label BLAME.

Then I proceeded to make way too many failed videos of the GM spinning the bottle and have it point at the sales manager.

When it stopped spinning, the Sales Manager would stand up and say, It’s not my fault, It’s there fault as he pointed across the table.

In theory, funny and too real, but the videos that came out were just awful.

I also tried having a sales manager riding a bull labeled Q4 Budget Target and he is yelling, “I won’t let go, I won’t let go”. That didn’t come out so well either! Funny but bad.

What I learned in my two days of video generation:
You need to provide the following in great detail:

– Character,
– Setting,
– Action,
– Emotion, and
– Dialogue.

You need to plan for just 7-8 seconds.

So it’s not easy, and if you try to have too much going on at once, it just will fail.

But this technology is evolving quickly, and the only way to learn it, is to use it.

Try, fail, get frustrated, try again, fail again, get frustrated, stick with it and try again. That is what learning feels like forever going forward.

Anyhow, as I reflect back a few years, if I wanted to create something like this when I was hotel side, it would be expensive, take a long time, and of course be much better.

Anyhow, for those of you going to Indy, as I say in the video tagline, “See you in Indy!”
