Why is my competitor recommended when I am only mentioned?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Because one of two conversions is failing, and they have opposite fixes. If AI names you and never offers you as an option, the assistant does not understand what you are for. That is an entity problem, and the fix is clarity about what you sell and to whom. If AI offers you and then recommends the competitor, you are on the shortlist and losing it. That is a differentiation problem, and the fix is third-party evidence that you are the better answer to that specific question. At Vivander Advisors I measure both conversions in every audit, because more visibility makes the second problem worse.

More visibility, more public losses. That surprises people.

The resort that made every shortlist and won none

A beachfront resort was offered in 60 percent of the family prompts I ran. Recommended in 4 percent.

Every losing row cited a competitor's page that named its kids' club hours and a suite rate. The resort had a kids' club with hours. It had suite rates. And it had published neither. The assistant chose the property it could describe.

Two paragraphs fixed it. Paragraphs the resort had never written because nobody had asked the question in a heading.

The two conversions

I read every AI answer through a funnel. Named: you appear. Offered: you are put forward as an option for the person asking. Recommended: the response presents you as an answer to their question.

Two ratios fall out.

Named-to-offered is the landmark problem. The platforms know you exist and use you as furniture in an answer about something else. Directions. A comparison foil. Or a historical aside. A low ratio means the assistant has no clear idea what you are. The fix is entity work: category pages that say what you sell and for whom, alias resolution, the same facts on every source. Publishing more content does not fix this. More content about an entity the assistant cannot place is more furniture.

Offered-to-recommended is the shortlist problem. Present, considered, passed over. The fix is differentiation evidence and corroboration for the question you are losing. Raise visibility without fixing the evidence and you raise the count of times you lose in front of a customer.

Both ratios low is a corroboration problem, and the off-site layer of the audit will already be saying so.

What Ghost Rankings tell you

A Ghost Ranking is a row where you were named or cited and a competitor was recommended in the same response. I count them because they are the most actionable rows in an audit. You were in the answer. The assistant had your page or your profile in front of it. It chose the other one, and the sources it cited on that row show why. A stated price. Some specific claim, a review theme, a local list. Or a fact you never published.

Why the competitor wins the same prompt

Across hotels and restaurants, the same handful of reasons.

They state a price and you do not. A missing price was a unanimous gate across six models in a 252,000-trial study by Vishwakarma and colleagues in 2026.

They answer the question in a heading. "Is the hotel family friendly" is an H2 on their site and a sentence in paragraph four on yours.

They are corroborated on the source that platform cites. An OpenTable page with a tasting-menu price, a Business Profile whose services match the website, a local editorial list.

Their description is specific and yours hedges. Ahrefs' fake-brand experiment found detailed third-party pages beating a hedged official one.

Does being mentioned more help?

On its own, no. Mentions build familiarity. Recommendations move revenue. A brand named ten times and recommended zero times has not been recommended. The cited-when-mentioned rate tells you whether the assistant is describing you from your own page or from someone else's, and that decides whether the fix lives on your site or off it.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Pull every row where you were named and sort it: landmark, foil, offered, recommended. The distribution tells you which conversion to fix, and the two fixes do not overlap.

For your role. Do not buy more visibility until the shortlist conversion improves. That is a hard thing to say to a marketing budget, and it is the thing the data says.

For your customers. They compared you. Then they chose. They never knew the hours and the rate existed, because the page that had them was not the page the engine read.

What I do not know

How often Ghost Rankings occur across a book of clients. The metric is new and one engagement is not a base rate. I report the count per client and do not quote a rate.

Where this is going

Shortlists are getting shorter. The AI local pack surfaces roughly a third of the businesses the classic three-pack did. Agents will collapse the shortlist to one, and then the second conversion is the only one that pays.

You do not have a visibility problem. What you have is a losing problem, and it is fixable, and it starts with reading the row where you lost.
