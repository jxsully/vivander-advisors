PHG AI Literacy Mindset Program

Michael J. Goldrich · July 21, 2025

Executive Summary

The AI Literacy Mindset Program represents a strategic and highly successful investment in Pacific Hospitality Group’s (PHG) future, designed to cultivate a workforce proficient in leveraging Artificial Intelligence. Through a comprehensive evaluation of pre- and post-program surveys and daily task tracker data, we have observed statistically significant and substantial enhancements in participants’ AI understanding, confidence, and particularly their practical engagement with AI tools.


Key findings underscore the program’s profound impact: participants are reporting measurable time savings (average 36.4 minutes per AI-assisted task, over 64 minutes saved daily overall), indicating a direct uplift in productivity and efficiency. User sentiment remains overwhelmingly positive (nearly 75.8% positive overall) and strengthens over time, reflecting high engagement and satisfaction with the program and the AI tools provided, like ChatGPT Teams. While core AI competencies have seen transformative improvement, the modest but significant 10% rise in ‘Overall AI Readiness’ shows broader confidence is building, though paired data suggest some teams are still calibrating their expectations. This program is instrumental in building a future-ready, adaptive, and innovative culture, positioning PHG ahead of most peer hotel groups.

Background: The Foundation for AI Adoption (One Year Prior)

Approximately one year before the launch of the current AI Literacy Mindset Program provided by Vivander Advisors, an initial 60-minute discussion was held to introduce high-level AI literacy concepts within the Pacific Hospitality Group (PHG) organization. While there was an early recognition of AI’s potential, widespread AI adoption did not take hold at that time. This can be attributed to a gap in addressing what we refer to as the “4 T’s” crucial for successful technological integration:

  • Tone from the Top: During that initial period, AI was not yet a clearly articulated top priority from leadership. Without a strong, consistent directive, broader adoption proved challenging. For the current AI Literacy Mindset Program, Garrett Busch explicitly made this a clear organizational priority, setting a vital tone from the top.
  • Tools: Employees lacked direct access to specific AI tools, hindering hands-on experimentation and practical application.
  • Training: There was no structured, comprehensive training program to guide employees systematically through AI concepts and usage.
  • Time: Dedicated time for employees to experiment with and learn new AI tools within their daily workflows was not explicitly provided or encouraged.

Recognizing these foundational needs, the AI Literacy Mindset Program was conceived and launched exactly one year after these initial high-level discussions, strategically designed to address each of these “4 T’s” head-on and foster an environment where AI adoption could genuinely flourish.

The Strategic Imperative: Cultivating AI Fluency

Prior to the AI Literacy Mindset Program, internal assessments identified an opportunity to enhance our employees’ AI proficiency. Many expressed only a basic understanding of AI concepts and limited practical experience with AI tools. This gap posed a risk to our ability to fully capitalize on AI-driven opportunities, potentially hindering productivity gains, informed decision-making, and organizational agility. The AI Literacy Mindset Program was strategically launched to address this critical need, empowering our employees with the essential knowledge and skills to effectively leverage AI, including tools like ChatGPT Teams (which replaced the originally proposed Microsoft Copilot). The program aims to foster an AI-first mindset across the organization, encouraging innovative problem-solving and establishing AI as a practical tool for augmenting human capabilities rather than replacing them.

Program Design & Rigorous Evaluation

The AI Literacy Mindset Program was structured as a comprehensive, all-hands AI education and adoption initiative, designed to deliver foundational AI literacy and practical application skills. Its effectiveness was rigorously measured through a standardized pre- and post-program survey administered to all participants, complemented by a daily AI task tracker during the program’s active phase.

Program Overview

  • Core Objective: To foster an AI-first mindset across PHG that encourages innovative problem-solving, generate quick wins demonstrating tangible business value, and build internal capacity for AI innovation through designated champions and power users.
  • Initial Target Audience: An initial cohort with capacity for up to 120 participants, reflecting an all-hands approach that included executives (President, CEO, Finance, Legal, Marketing), as well as leaders and team members from Revenue, Sales, Food & Beverage (F&B), Operations, General Managers (GMs), Front Desk, Administrators, and the Technology team. Participation spanned both property and corporate levels, encompassing both branded hotels and boutique independent hotels, ensuring broad organizational impact and a truly comprehensive reach.
  • Program Structure: The program was designed as an 8-week training initiative, combining intensive foundational learning with practical application, focusing on AI tools like ChatGPT Teams.
    • Foundational Workshop: Developing AI Literacy: The program commenced with an intensive four-hour workshop, offered in both in-person and virtual formats for maximum accessibility. This workshop established the foundation for AI literacy and mindset development through interactive elements and knowledge checks. It addressed why this AI initiative matters to PHG, focusing on how AI tools can serve as skills multipliers across the organization. The session emphasized AI as a practical tool for enhancing work rather than a theoretical concept, augmenting human capabilities. It reviewed pre-course survey results to address concerns and covered the draft AI governance framework to establish clear guidelines for responsible AI usage (bias/drift). Practical applications of current AI trends relevant to hospitality were explored, focusing on achievable, near-term uses.
    • Weekly Training Sessions (GEAR Methodology): The foundational workshop served as a springboard for six subsequent weekly interactive sessions, leveraging the GEAR Methodology (Gauge, Examine, Acquire, Reinforce) to ensure a dynamic and actionable learning experience. Due to scheduling challenges, these training sessions were repeated three times live for an hour each week for anyone who could attend virtually, ensuring maximum accessibility and flexibility. The sessions were designed to be friendly and introduced a new topic each week, progressively building skills and confidence.
      • Week 1: Identifying Use Cases: Focused on introducing participants to the concept of AI use cases. Through discussions, hands-on exercises, and real-world examples, participants learned to identify opportunities where AI can address repetitive, predictive, and analytical tasks, including a framework for evaluating AI opportunities.
      • Week 2: Prompting Frameworks: Introduced participants to the art of prompting – how to communicate effectively with AI tools to achieve desired results. This session covered foundational and advanced prompting concepts.
      • Week 3: Embedding ChatGPT Teams in Workflows: This session taught participants how to use ChatGPT Teams effectively in their workflows through demonstrations and live practice, exploring its capabilities in Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook to streamline data analysis, content generation, and task management.
      • Week 4: Exploring AI Solutions: Expanded participants’ understanding of the broader AI ecosystem by introducing alternative tools like Google Gemini, Claude, and Meta’s offerings. Participants learned to evaluate these tools based on their specific use cases.
      • Week 5: Content Creation with AI: Dedicated to leveraging AI tools for creating high-quality content, from marketing copy and image generation.
      • Week 6: Automation and AI Agents: The final instructional session introduced participants to automation platforms and AI agents, explaining how tools can automate workflows and exploring the potential of AI agents to perform tasks with minimal human input.
    • Wrap-Up Session: Concluded the program with a two-hour wrap-up session offered to ensure all participants could attend. The final session reflected on the collective journey, examined tangible outcomes, and highlighted specific success stories. It also engaged in open dialogue about challenges encountered and administered a comprehensive post-program survey to measure progress against benchmarks.

Evaluation Methodology

Our assessment utilized two key datasets: the combine-pre-post-surveys.xlsx – AI Survey.csv for overall program impact, and the Your AI Daily Task Tracker.xlsx – Sheet.csv for insights during the program.

  • Aggregate Analysis (Pre-Post Survey): This approach involved comparing the mean scores and distributions of all ‘BEFORE PROGRAM’ responses (n=108 unique entries) against all ‘AFTER PROGRAM’ responses (n=71 unique entries), treating them as independent groups. This provided a broad measure of the program’s overall reach and impact across the entire participant pool. Independent samples t-tests were used to determine statistical significance.
  • Paired Analysis (Pre-Post Survey): This focused exclusively on the cohort of 51 unique individuals who completed both the ‘BEFORE PROGRAM’ and ‘AFTER PROGRAM’ surveys. This longitudinal approach offered a direct, person-by-person assessment of improvement and stronger causal inferences regarding the program’s effect. Paired t-tests were utilized for statistical significance.
  • Daily Activity Tracker Analysis: The Your AI Daily Task Tracker.xlsx – Sheet.csv provided granular data on daily AI usage, time savings, perceived AI quality, speed, effectiveness, and user sentiment during the program. This dataset allowed for tracking progression over time in a more immediate and practical context, complementing the overarching pre-post survey results.

Numerical Mapping of Survey Responses

To ensure robust quantitative analysis of categorical survey responses, a precise numerical mapping was applied:

  • For 5-point Likert scales (e.g., ‘Current Understanding’, ‘AI Tool Confidence’), responses like ‘1 (No understanding)’ to ‘5 (Comprehensive understanding)’ were directly mapped to numerical values 1 through 5.
  • For the “Work Done with AI” metric (which presented options like ‘1-3 tasks’, ‘4-7 tasks’, ‘8-10 tasks’, ‘More than 10 tasks’), a granular numerical translation was used to represent the approximate mid-to-upper value of each range (e.g., ‘1-3 tasks’ mapped to 3, ‘4-7 tasks’ to 7, ‘8-10 tasks’ to 10, ‘More than 10 tasks’ to 12). This mapping provides a more accurate and sensitive measure of increased practical engagement compared to a simplified linear scale.
  • For time-saving questions in the daily tracker (e.g., “1-15 minutes”), midpoints of the ranges were used (e.g., 8 minutes).
  • Responses of ‘More than 120 minutes were converted to 150 minutes for midpoint estimation.
  • Metrics: All quality, speed, and effectiveness ratings in the daily tracker were converted to a 5-point numerical scale before averaging: Excellent = 5, Good = 4, Acceptable = 3, Poor = 1, Rows marked “N/A” or left blank were omitted from the calculation.
  • Rows reporting ‘0 tasks’ were excluded from Work-Done means and t-tests.
  • Time-saved midpoint mapping: 1–15 = 8 min; 16–30 = 23 min; 31–45 = 38 min; 46–60 = 53 min; 61–90 = 75 min; 91–120 = 105 min; > 120 = 150 min (upper bound capped at 180 min).
  • For sentiment, word coding:
    • Positive
      [Accomplished, Appreciative, Confident, Curious, Delighted, Efficient, Empowered, Encouraged, Engaged, Excited, Happy, Hopeful, Inspired, Motivated, Optimistic, Productive, Proud, Satisfied]
    • Neutral / Mixed
      [Interested, Neutral, Observing, Open, Uncertain]
    • Negative           
      [Anxious, Confused, Frustrated, Overwhelmed, Stuck, Unsure]

Data Limitations: While the daily tracker provides daily data, a direct integration of all participants’ pre-post scores with their specific daily activity could not be done in a fully linked manner if daily tracker participants were not fully identifiable and matched to the pre-post survey participants. Our insights from the daily tracker are based on the aggregate trends observed within that dataset.

Transformative Impact: Key Findings & Business Benefits

The AI Literacy Mindset Program delivered compelling evidence of its positive impact, translating directly into enhanced human capital and strategic business advantages. The consistency of these findings across both the pre-post surveys and the daily tracker reinforces the program’s effectiveness in driving real change.

• Overall Average Time Saved per Task Using AI: 36.4 minutes

• Overall Average Anticipated Time Savings Per Day: 64.08 minutes

• Overall Average AI Output Quality Score (1-5): 4.28 (Excellent)

• Overall Average AI Speed Score (1-5): 4.46 (Excellent)

• Overall Average AI Effectiveness Score (1-5): 4.25 (Excellent)

Business Benefits and Strategic Implications

Elevated Human Capital & AI Acumen: The statistically significant improvements in “Current Understanding” (e.g., 36.7% increase in paired data, p= 6.3 e-10) and “AI Tool Confidence” (20.9% increase in paired data, p= 4.9 e-05) are profound. They demonstrate that the program is effectively shifting our workforce from basic familiarity to a solid grasp of AI concepts and a high comfort level with AI tools like ChatGPT Teams. This means employees are becoming active, confident users, ready to explore and integrate AI into their roles. This foundational knowledge empowers more informed discussions, faster adoption of new AI initiatives, and reduced resistance to technological change.


Tangible Productivity & Efficiency Gains (Daily Confirmed & Compounding): The extraordinary increase in “Work Done with AI” (a remarkable 51.7% increase in the paired group, with p= 3.06 e-06) is the most compelling evidence of direct business value. This isn’t just about knowledge; it’s about action. Our daily tracker data further validates this, showing an average time saving of nearly 36.4 minutes per AI-assisted task. More impressively, participants are reporting an anticipated average daily total time savings of over 64 minutes per person. This indicates that AI is becoming a significant driver of efficiency across multiple tasks throughout the day. This compounding effect of daily time savings across multiple tasks translates directly into freeing up valuable employee time for higher-value, strategic work. For the 120 employees in this program, conservatively estimating 1 hour saved per employee per day (derived from the observed increase in “Anticipated Time Savings Per Day “), with an assumed salary of $20 per hour, five days per week, over 50 working weeks per year, is 250 hours saved per person per year. This extrapolates to a minimum annual efficiency gains valued at approximately $600,000. The continuous nature of these savings, demonstrated through daily tracking, reinforces the immediate and sustained value of the program.


Consistently High Perceived AI Quality & Speed (Reliable Tool Adoption): The daily tracker reveals consistently high ratings for AI output quality (average 4.28/5, where 5 is Excellent), AI speed (average 4.46/5), and AI effectiveness (average 4.25/5). These high scores, maintained throughout the program’s duration, are critical. They demonstrate that participants are finding its application to be highly satisfactory in terms of output, efficiency, and utility. This high user satisfaction is imperative for sustained and widespread adoption of AI tools, building trust in the technology and ensuring that AI is seen as a reliable partner in daily operations. This indicates that the tools provided (like ChatGPT Teams) are indeed meeting user expectations for performance and utility in real-world scenarios.


Overwhelmingly Positive User Sentiment (Driving Engagement & Advocacy): The daily tracker reveals an overwhelmingly positive overall sentiment among participants (75.8% expressing positive feelings, with only 6.54% negative). More importantly, the proportion of positive sentiment generally trends upwards over the program weeks, with some minor fluctuations, reaching high points in later stages. This sustained enthusiasm, reflected in terms like “Empowered,” “Accomplished,” and “Delighted,” indicates deep engagement with the program and a strong willingness to continue leveraging AI tools. This positive sentiment fosters internal advocacy for AI, encourages peer-to-peer learning, and contributes to a dynamic, forward-thinking organizational culture where AI is embraced as a valuable asset rather than a daunting new technology.


Building Confidence Over Time (Reinforcing Program Effectiveness): The daily tracker’s ConfidenceImprovementPercent metric provides granular insight into participants’ self-reported progress during the program. The clear upward trend, with average confidence improvement generally increasing from around 56% in Week 1 to over 75% by Week 7, demonstrates that the structured learning path and hands-on challenges are continuously building user confidence and ability. This reinforces the program’s effectiveness in progressively developing AI literacy over its duration, ensuring that learning is absorbed and applied incrementally. It validates the program’s methodology of balancing theoretical knowledge with practical application and dedicated experimentation time.


Fostering an AI-Adaptive & Collaborative Culture: Improvements in “Effective AI Communication” (26.26% aggregate, 26.4% paired) and “Consider AI Solutions” (15.6% aggregate, 13.3% paired) signify a more fluid and confident dialogue around AI within the organization. This fosters a collaborative environment where AI solutions are proactively considered, shared, and implemented, accelerating overall organizational adaptability to technological shifts. Employees are becoming more adept at articulating AI-related needs and opportunities, bridging potential communication gaps between technical and non-technical teams.

Expected ROI: Quantifying Opportunity Costs

While the direct cost of the program (facilitation fees + license costs) can be calculated, a truly comprehensive understanding of its ROI requires evaluating the opportunity cost. The value of the benefits we would have lost or delayed by not implementing this program.

What is Opportunity Cost?

In simple terms, opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that you didn’t choose when you made a decision. It’s the benefits you could have received by taking an alternative action, but chose to forgo. When considering the Literacy AI Mindset Program, the opportunity cost represents the substantial benefits that would have been lost or significantly delayed if the organization had chosen not to implement this program, or if its rollout were to be postponed.

Based on the compelling data, here are insights into the significant opportunity costs that would have been incurred without the AI Mindset Program:

  1. Lost Productivity and Foregone Efficiency Gains:
    • Direct & Compounding Impact: The program delivered an average time saving of nearly 36.4 minutes per AI-assisted task and an anticipated average daily total time savings of over 64 minutes per person. If we had not implemented this program, or delayed it, we would be forgoing these daily, compounding time savings across 120 employees. This equates to over 30,000 hours of potential productivity lost annually across the targeted workforce (one hour/day saved per employee * five days a week * 50 weeks/year * 120 employees). These hours represent valuable time that could have been reinvested in higher-value, strategic initiatives, innovation projects, customer service enhancements, or core operational tasks, directly impacting our bottom line. The conservative estimated annual efficiency gain using a $20 hour wage, is approximately $600,000. This is a direct quantification of the financial opportunity cost of not realizing these efficiencies.
  2. Missed Innovation and Delayed Competitive Advantage:
    • Reduced AI Opportunity Identification: The program significantly boosted participants’ ability to “Identify AI Opportunities” (by over 24.8% in paired data). Without this program, our employees would be less equipped to spot and capitalize on emerging AI-driven solutions within their departments and workflows. This translates to missed chances for internal innovation, slower adoption of cutting-edge practices, and a delayed competitive edge as other organizations advance their AI capabilities.
    • Slower AI Solution Integration: Similarly, the increase in employees’ tendency to “Consider AI Solutions” (by over 13.3% in paired data) means a more proactive, AI-integrated workforce. The opportunity cost of not having this program is a slower, more reactive pace of AI solution adoption, potentially leading to being outpaced by competitors who are more agile in leveraging AI.
  3. Compromised Quality and Less Informed Decision-Making:
    • Risk of Suboptimal AI Outputs: The program significantly improved employees’ ability to “Evaluate Quality AI Content” (by over 32.3% in paired data). Without this training, employees would be at a higher risk of misinterpreting or misusing AI-generated information, potentially leading to flawed business decisions, wasted resources on inaccurate outputs, or even reputational damage. The opportunity cost is the perpetuation of these risks and the inability to fully trust and integrate AI outputs into critical processes.
    • Persistent Inefficiencies: The high ratings for AI’s quality, speed, and effectiveness (averaging mid-4s: 4.28 to 4.63) confirm that AI tools are powerful accelerators. The opportunity cost of delaying this program is the continued reliance on slower, more manual processes where AI could provide significant advantages, leaving efficiency gains unrealized across various functions.
  4. Lower Employee Engagement and Morale:
    • Missed Skill Development: The program led to statistically significant improvements in foundational AI understanding and confidence, and participants reported continuous confidence building over time. The opportunity cost is a workforce that feels unprepared for the future, potentially leading to lower job satisfaction, higher attrition rates among digitally savvy talent, and a reduced capacity for innovation if employees lack the skills to engage with cutting-edge tools.
    • Unrealized Cultural Transformation: The program fostered overwhelmingly positive user sentiment. Without such an initiative, the organization would miss the opportunity to cultivate an “AI-first mindset” and a collaborative culture around AI. This could lead to resistance to new technologies, slower internal adoption, and a workforce less aligned with future strategic directions.

In essence, the opportunity cost of not running the AI Mindset Program is substantial. It represents hundreds of thousands of dollars in foregone productivity annually, lost innovation opportunities, a slower pace of strategic AI adoption, and a workforce less prepared and engaged for the future. The program’s success unequivocally demonstrates that the investment is not merely an expense, but a strategic imperative to capitalize on critical opportunities that would otherwise remain unrealized.

Strategic Nuance: Understanding “Overall AI Readiness”

While the program’s success in specific AI competencies is evident, the metric for “Overall AI Readiness” presents a crucial nuance:

  • Aggregate scores rose 10% (p = 0.008), while the matched cohort showed a smaller, non-significant 6.6% uptick (p = 0.08). The mixed signal suggests some teams are newly aware of integration hurdles even as overall confidence improves
  • Interpretation: This finding is not indicative of a program’s failure but rather a sophisticated outcome. As participants deepened their specific AI knowledge and skills, their understanding of what “true” or “overall” AI readiness entails likely matured. Initial perceptions of readiness might have been simplistic; after gaining more insight into AI’s complexities, ethical considerations, and the organizational infrastructure required for seamless integration, participants may be offering a more realistic self-assessment of their overall preparedness. This signals a heightened awareness of the broader AI ecosystem and the journey still ahead for the organization, underscoring the need for parallel investments in governance, tooling, and change management. It is a valuable insight that helps us fine-tune future strategic AI initiatives.

Associate Feedback on the Program

To complement the quantitative improvements, participant feedback across roles revealed how the AI Literacy Mindset Program is driving a tangible cultural and operational shift. Leaders in marketing, sales, operations, and human resources consistently described a transformation in how they view and use AI tools in daily workflows. A Director of Marketing emphasized that AI is now “an extension of my responsibilities, expanding the range of tasks I can take on” from restructuring email communications to enhancing strategy decks and brand-building tasks that previously consumed hours. From the operational side, a General Manager reflected, “When I started, I wasn’t confident in using AI and wasn’t sure how relevant it was to our business. Now, I use it daily, and it’s greatly helpful in many of my tasks.” Food & Beverage leadership described hands-on benefits as “key takeaways included a deeper understanding of its core functionality, strategies for crafting effective prompts, and the ability to create customized GPTs tailored to our operations.” A VP of Strategic Innovation noted, “Overall, I think the training was helpful… but I’d love to understand how we are going to keep up with the adaptations.”

Sales leaders, often more skeptical at the outset, expressed clear shifts in mindset. A Director of Sales shared, “I went into the training a bit of a skeptic… but throughout the training I learned that there are so many more ways to use AI than just tailoring emails, and I’m trying to adopt them into my day to day.” Another Area Sales Manager admitted, “Before the training I was skeptical… afraid of sounding fake. After the training, I learned you can personalize AI to help you get tasks done faster, it doesn’t have to sound robotic.” Several sales managers and GMs confirmed the time-saving impact in practice: “I regularly use AI to interpret data, shape strategy, enhance client engagement, and even draft correspondence.” A VP of Field Operations summarized the strategic shift concisely: “With any AI initiative, we’re all looking for the same thing, something that can help us be more efficient in our jobs and give us time back in the day.”

The program also surfaced valuable insights for refinement. HR and People & Culture leaders observed that some sessions “squeezed a lot of information into the hour, which caused [them] to go fast, which delayed some people.” A VP of People & Culture noted that smaller, departmental sessions “could’ve helped apply the tactics to the actual work we are doing in our individual functional spaces.” Similarly, a General Manager commented, “I do think there would have been more impact from the classes if there weren’t as many people on each one.” Several participants recommended adding practical “homework” to encourage hands-on application immediately after each session, ensuring that concepts moved from theory to action more effectively.

Despite these logistical challenges, the underlying sentiment across roles remained highly positive. Executives appreciated the grounding in real-world applications, with one VP noting, “[Vivander Advisors] provided a lot of baseline understanding, but unless you use it every day, you’ll quickly fall behind.” Encouraging daily use of AI, along with creating GPTs for operational use cases, became common themes among senior leaders. In fact, several VPs highlighted that the program didn’t just demystify AI, it catalyzed self-initiated exploration. As one VP succinctly put it, “This course served as a strong foundation for the more advanced AI training we’ve pursued since.”

Case Study Wrap-Up

Bringing both quantitative results and qualitative experiences together, it is clear that the AI Literacy Mindset Program is not only enhancing technical proficiency but reshaping organizational culture. From skeptics becoming advocates, to leaders embedding AI into their strategic workflows, PHG’s workforce is now seeing AI as a practical partner. Measurable time savings, high perceived quality and effectiveness of outputs, and widespread engagement signal a meaningful shift. While feedback points to opportunities to fine-tune delivery through more tailored sessions and ongoing learning, the outcome is unequivocal: PHG has cultivated a workforce that is both ready and motivated to drive AI-powered productivity. This foundational shift positions PHG as a leader in AI adoption in hospitality transforming potential into everyday operational advantage.

Ten Key Takeaways from the AI Mindset Program

  1. Significant Boost in Core AI Competencies: The program led to a statistically significant increase in participants’ fundamental understanding of AI concepts, their confidence in using AI tools, and their ability to identify AI opportunities. This means employees are now better equipped to engage with AI.
  2. Dramatic Increase in Hands-On AI Application: A standout finding is the substantial rise in the actual “Work Done with AI,” indicating a successful shift from theoretical knowledge to practical, daily application of AI tools like ChatGPT Teams. This suggests employees are actively integrating AI into their workflows.
  3. Measurable Time Savings in Daily Tasks: Participants reported significant time efficiencies, with an average saving of nearly 36.4 minutes per AI-assisted task and an anticipated daily total savings of over 64 minutes per person. This translates directly into increased productivity and capacity for higher-value work.
  4. High Perceived Quality and Effectiveness of AI Tools: Throughout the program, participants consistently rated AI output quality, speed, and overall effectiveness very highly (all averaging mid-4s (e.g., 4.28 – 4.63) indicating ‘Excellent’ performance). This validates the utility and reliability of the AI tools provided, fostering trust and continued adoption.
  5. Overwhelmingly Positive User Sentiment and Engagement: The program cultivated a predominantly positive user sentiment (nearly 75.8% positive overall), which generally strengthened over the course of the program. This high level of enthusiasm signifies strong employee engagement and a willingness to embrace AI.
  6. Progressive Confidence Building: Daily tracking revealed a continuous increase in participants’ self-reported confidence and ability to use generative AI, consistently growing from around 56% improvement in Week 1 to over 75% by Week 7. This indicates effective progressive learning.
  7. Enhanced AI Communication and Solution-Oriented Mindset: Employees showed statistically significant improvements in effectively communicating AI ideas and more frequently considering AI as a solution for work challenges. This fosters a more collaborative and forward-thinking culture regarding AI.
  8. Strategic Nuance in “Overall AI Readiness”: While specific skills improved, “Overall AI Readiness” rose 10% at the company level (significant) and 6.6% in the matched cohort (not significant), indicating growing confidence but also recognition of remaining integration work.
  9. Effective Program Design with Accessibility in Mind: The program’s structure, including an initial 4-hour workshop (in-person and virtual), 8 weeks of training, and weekly sessions repeated three times for accessibility, along with dedicated time for experimentation, effectively addressed barriers to previous AI adoption.
  10. Foundational for Future AI Initiatives: The program successfully built fundamental AI literacy and fostered an “AI-first mindset”, creating a critical baseline of understanding and practical skills. This sets the stage for more advanced AI initiatives, deeper integration, and sustained innovation across the organization.
