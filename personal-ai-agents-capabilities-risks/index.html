AI Agents Can Act on Behalf of People. How Much Trust Have They Earned?

Michael J. Goldrich · October 23, 2026

Part 1 of 3. Research reviewed October 25, 2026. Based on published documentation, not hands-on comparative testing. Product access and features can change. Featured image: AI-generated editorial illustration.

A chatbot can suggest a follow-up email. An AI agent may find the relevant conversation, prepare the reply, and, with the right authorization, send it. That last step changes the conversation about trust.

Claude Duet, ChatGPT dots, Meta Muse, Instinct, and Google’s agent offerings represent a shift toward software that carries work forward across tools and time. The opportunity is less coordination. The risk is giving an imperfect interpretation the ability to become a real action.

What is a personal AI agent?

A personal AI agent uses an AI model, tools, and authorized information to pursue a goal through multiple steps. It may remember context, maintain ongoing responsibilities, and continue work in the background. Its actual reach depends on connections, permissions, product restrictions, and human approvals.

AI agents vs. AI assistants: What changes?

The labels overlap. Some products called assistants already perform actions. The practical distinction is how far the system can carry a task without someone directing every step.

Compare behavior rather than product labels
Dimension Chat assistant Personal agent
Assignment Chat assistantAnswer a question or prepare a draft Personal agentPursue a goal through a workflow
Tools Chat assistantMay search, analyze, or use integrations Personal agentMay coordinate actions across tools
Continuity Chat assistantOften centered on a conversation Personal agentMay retain task state and responsibilities
Oversight Chat assistantReview an answer Personal agentReview information, permissions, actions, and results

An agent’s ability to act is separate from its authority to act. Access to an inbox should not automatically mean permission to send every message it drafts.

Claude Duet, ChatGPT dots, Meta Muse, Instinct, and Google compared

This comparison describes published product designs, rather than a hands-on performance ranking. Availability and features can change during previews.

Claude Duet: Proactive professional work

Anthropic describes Duet as a cloud-based agent for ongoing work. It can prepare morning briefs, research, files, and invoice follow-ups using connected apps. Its research preview is available through a waitlist for US Pro and Max subscribers, with background work consuming plan limits. Microsoft 365 is not yet available, and users cannot watch or take over Duet’s browser. It checks accounts at intervals, so urgent information can arrive between checks. Source: Anthropic’s Duet documentation.

ChatGPT dots: Continuity across tools and projects

A dot has its own cloud computer and browser and can coordinate research, analysis, documents, and software work. Optional local-computer access is a separate connection, with local work requiring an online computer and the ChatGPT app. Access is rolling out to eligible accounts. A dot’s conversation and work it delegates can have different usage treatment. Source: OpenAI’s dots documentation.

Meta Muse: Everyday tasks and broader goals

Meta positions Muse as a personal agent for work across websites and connected apps, including travel, shopping, reservations, and documents. Meta describes a dedicated virtual machine, protected credential handling, and permission choices. Its security design separates the main agent from a permission authority called Sentinel. This matters because controls outside the main agent can limit what it is allowed to do. Meta still acknowledges mistakes and attacks through content the agent reads. Source: Meta’s security architecture.

Instinct: Assistance through familiar communication

Instinct emphasizes an assistant people can text or call, capable of using a phone and computer for tasks such as groceries, travel, and bookings. Its terms describe action authorization and warn that some actions may be irreversible. Confirm current approval controls before assuming they work like another platform’s. Source: Instinct; action terms.

Google: Personal Gemini workflows and enterprise agents

Google introduced Gemini Spark as a personal agent. Its current English help page describes tasks and workflows in Gemini, including inbox management, research, price monitoring, and file creation. That consumer feature has subscription, account, activity, and regional requirements. Google’s separate Gemini agent for work emphasizes enterprise context, integrations, and organizational controls. Consumer access does not establish enterprise suitability. Source: Gemini tasks; Google’s enterprise announcement.

What do the CEOs expect their AI agents to change?

The common ambition is to move from answering questions to carrying work forward. Their emphasis differs: continuity across tools, personal context, delegated action, and dependable execution. The interpretations below are Vivander’s analysis of the cited statements, not a ranking or a promise that every feature already works.

OpenAI and ChatGPT dots: Work that follows you across tools

“It can just follow along with your work wherever it happens.”

Sam Altman, OpenAI CEO. Speaking about dots at DevDay on September 29, 2026, in Yahoo Finance’s recorded keynote excerpt and transcript. He also emphasized that “you stay in control”.

What is different: The vision is an ongoing helper connected to the places where work happens. The user delegates a responsibility and defines boundaries, rather than issuing a new prompt for every step. OpenAI’s dots documentation explains the product’s ongoing responsibilities and controls. For a hotel sales team, the practical question is whether follow-up work can continue across approved systems with enough visibility for the team to supervise it.

Anthropic and Claude Duet: Useful execution requires context and judgment

“There’s a big gap between an AI model that works in a demo and one that works in a regulated industry”

Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO and co-founder. From Anthropic’s February 17, 2026 announcement with Infosys. This is broader context about enterprise agents, not a statement about Claude Duet. No Duet-specific CEO quote was verified in the sources reviewed.

What is different: Amodei’s point is that useful agents require knowledge of the work and its constraints. Applied to evaluating Duet, the question is whether proactive help can correctly interpret context and urgency. Duet’s own October 21 launch article describes a trusted working partner that moves tasks forward and seeks approval for consequential actions. That product positioning comes from Anthropic, rather than a Duet quote attributed to its CEO.

Meta and Muse: Personalized help at broad scale

“toward delivering personal superintelligence for everyone”

Mark Zuckerberg, Meta founder and CEO. From Meta’s October 21, 2026 earnings statement, which specifically includes Muse among its milestones.

What is different: The ambition is a personal agent that understands someone’s context and helps pursue goals over time. Meta’s Muse launch announcement describes connections to personal information and Meta services. For hospitality, that could help an agent shortlist venues around preferences. This is a direction of travel, not evidence that Muse can complete every booking or safely infer every preference.

Instinct: Handling the nuances of everyday life

“We’re building Instinct to be the best personal agent that can handle the deeply personal nuances of everyday life.”

Noah Shinn, Instinct founder and CEO. From a September 28, 2026 company statement reported by Investing.com.

What is different: The emphasis is completing practical errands with personal context through familiar communication. Shinn’s September 28 Colossus interview explores reservations, travel, trust, and coordination. A diner might ask for a suitable restaurant and help arranging the evening, rather than just a list of suggestions. The user still needs to verify important details, permissions, and completed reservations.

Google and Gemini Spark: A personal agent that acts under your direction

“taking action on your behalf and under your direction.”

Sundar Pichai, CEO of Google and Alphabet. Describing Gemini Spark in his May 19, 2026 I/O keynote.

What is different: The intended shift is from receiving an answer to directing action within a personal digital workflow. Google places Spark inside the Gemini app, with connected information and tools shaping what it can do. For a guest, the value would be help moving from research toward a plan or transaction. It still depends on supported services, access, and approval boundaries.

What should hospitality leaders take from these visions? Evaluate the gap between a compelling promise and a completed task. Ask what information the agent can access, what it may do without asking, how errors are caught, and how the business verifies the outcome in its booking, sales, or operations system.

How do personal AI agents work?

The chat interface sits above an execution system. A model interprets the request; tools retrieve information or perform actions; an execution environment runs the work; memory preserves useful context; schedules or events trigger follow-up. Permission checks determine which actions can proceed.

Some connections use application programming interfaces, or APIs. Others involve a browser or computer interface. A cloud browser may have different signed-in accounts and network access from an employee’s laptop. Connecting one service does not connect every system in the organization.

A natural-language instruction is useful, but a technical restriction creates a stronger boundary. Asking an agent to avoid sending confidential files is different from limiting its tools so that those files cannot leave an approved environment.

What can AI agents do well?

Clear assignments with checkable results are promising starting points: assemble a meeting brief, prepare a recurring project report, research a topic with sources, compare approved records, or draft follow-up messages.

Every Friday, review this project folder and prepare a status report. Identify missing updates and link the supporting files. Save the report for review before sharing.

This hypothetical instruction names the source, timing, output, and approval boundary. It gives the agent a useful responsibility without treating every related action as authorized.

What can’t AI agents reliably do?

  • Guarantee correct interpretation. A system can confuse dates, customers, versions, or priorities.
  • Access every tool. Integrations, account permissions, plan limits, and organizational policies constrain execution.
  • Complete every task unattended. Authentication, missing information, approvals, and usage limits can interrupt work.
  • Undo every consequence. Stopping a task does not recall an email or reverse a completed transaction.
  • Replace accountable judgment. A polished explanation does not make a consequential business decision sound.

OpenAI explicitly distinguishes pausing a dot, stopping delegated work, and cancelling schedules. Stopping work does not undo completed actions. Source: dot controls.

What are the biggest risks?

Prompt injection: Untrusted content tries to redirect the agent

A webpage or email can contain instructions designed to manipulate the agent reading it. For example, an attacker might present a request to upload a private document as a necessary verification step. That content is information to evaluate, rather than authorization from the owner. Anthropic warns that defenses are imperfect. Restrict data access and require review before external disclosure. Source: Duet safety guidance.

Privacy: Training, storage, and deletion are different questions

Instinct’s policy illustrates why details matter: it describes model-development uses with opt-out provisions and exceptions, while separately excluding information received directly through Google Workspace APIs from model training and improvement. Disconnecting an integration does not automatically delete indexed information. Review the policy for each source and service. Source: Instinct’s privacy policy.

Standing permission and unattended consumption

Ongoing authorization can reduce interruptions while increasing the consequences of vague instructions. Define recipients, data sources, spending boundaries, and expiry dates. Review resource usage as well as outputs: repeated checks and delegated tasks can consume allowances even when the owner is away.

How should organizations start?

  1. Choose one contained workflow with a clear owner.
  2. Connect only the information it needs.
  3. Define what requires approval before sending, spending, sharing, or deleting.
  4. Inspect completed work in the actual destination system.
  5. Measure accuracy, corrections, time saved, and usage.
  6. Expand scope only after consistent results.

Frequently asked questions

Are personal AI agents the same as chatbots?

They overlap, but agents can carry a goal through multiple steps using tools and permissions. Some maintain responsibilities and run in the background.

Can an AI agent work while a computer is off?

Cloud work may continue independently. Work that relies on a connected local computer can require that computer and its app to remain running.

Do AI agents always ask before acting?

No universal rule applies. Approval depends on the product, action, permissions, prior authorization, and organizational controls.

Which personal AI agent is best for a business?

Evaluate the specific workflow, integrations, data handling, approval controls, activity records, and supervision effort. This article does not establish a tested overall winner.
