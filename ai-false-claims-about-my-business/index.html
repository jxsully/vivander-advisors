Can AI say false things about my business, and how do I correct them?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Yes, and more often than most owners know. AI assistants assemble answers from whatever sources they retrieve. When those sources are stale, thin or wrong, so is the answer. In Ahrefs' 2025 experiment with a fabricated brand, Gemini and Perplexity repeated the fabrications in 37 to 39 percent of answers. The correction is not a complaint to the platform, because there is no desk to complain to. It is a specific, dated, corroborated statement of the current fact, placed on the sources that platform actually reads. Every Vivander Advisors audit carries a fact ledger: every claim AI makes about a client, checked against the truth, routed to the source that produced it.

The assistant is not lying to your guests. It is repeating someone else who was.

The resort that was adults-only

Two platforms described a luxury resort as adults-only. It had a kids' club and family suites. Eleven pages on its site mentioned families. Not one said it in a heading. The most-cited third-party page about the property was a 2019 travel piece about a couples' package.

The assistant did what assistants do. It found the most specific, most retrievable source and believed it.

Why AI gets facts about businesses wrong

Three causes account for almost everything I find.

The fact changed and the sources did not. You renovated in 2024. The 2021 review describing the old rooms is still the most-cited page about you. AI-cited pages average about 1,064 days old, per Ahrefs. A three-year-old description is not an outlier. It is the median.

The fact was never published in a form a machine can read. Rates in a booking widget. The pet policy in a PDF. Private dining capacity behind the word "inquire." The assistant fills the gap from a third party, or from nothing.

The name collides. A restaurant called Ruka shares its name with a Finnish ski resort and a pop musician. Without an address on every source, the engine blends them into one strange entity.

Does the platform prefer your official page?

Not reliably. In the Xarumei experiment ChatGPT cited the official FAQ in 84 percent of answers and stayed under 7 percent contamination. Five other products preferred the fabricated third-party pages. Ahrefs' own summary: AI preferred specific lies to vague truths.

An official page that hedges loses to a third party that states. That is the whole correction strategy in one sentence.

Can you get the platform to fix it?

No. OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and Perplexity publish no process for a business to dispute a claim in an answer. Google's Ask Maps, which replaced the public Q&A on Business Profiles from December 2025, synthesizes its answer in the moment from the profile, the reviews, the photos and the linked website. And the company's entire official guidance on the subject is to keep the Business Profile and Merchant Center current.

The lever is the sources. It is always the sources.

Does old negative coverage count?

Only if it is no longer true, and I separate three cases before I touch anything.

A false claim is a hallucination finding. It routes to the corroboration gap that produced it, and that is inside the audit.

A claim that was true and has since been resolved is the stale-accurate case. The counter is a dated statement of the current fact on the surfaces the engines read.

A claim that is true and current gets stated, and I stop there. I do not help clients obscure accurate current facts. That gets said early, because it is what makes the rest of the assessment worth trusting.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Publish the current fact on your own site in plain text, dated, specific, under a heading that asks the question. Then make it match on every source the platform cites.

For your role. Somebody has to read what AI says about the property every quarter and trace each wrong claim to its source. That is a new job. It is not in your reputation vendor's dashboard, because their dashboard tracks rank and this has nothing to do with rank.

For your guests. They will believe the answer. There is no way for them to know the kids' club exists if the only page that says so is one the engine never read.

What I do not know

How often AI-cited wrong or negative material sits outside Google's top ten, where no rank tracker can see it. The mechanism is documented. Its base rate across an industry is not. I report the count per client and refuse to guess the average.

Where this is going

The resort fixed it with one heading rewrite and one dated editorial mention. Both platforms corrected within the 30-day re-test.

Agents will soon act on these facts, not only repeat them. An agent that believes you are adults-only will not book the family. It will not tell you it skipped you either. Correcting the record used to be a reputation task. It is becoming an inventory task.

The assistant was never wrong about you. It was right about the wrong page.
