Can participants build an AI agent system without writing code?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 25, 2026

Sessions · Article

Agent Builder Sessions guide participants through a working system for job signals, sales leads or local stories, using numbered copy-and-paste steps.

By Michael J. Goldrich, Vivander Advisors.

Yes. Agent Builder Sessions guide participants through building an AI system on your own laptop with numbered copy-and-paste steps. Choose a track for job opportunities, sales leads or local stories. The live build runs up to two hours, and the system is verified before you log off.

The starting point is a recurring piece of work. You need to read a market, identify something worth acting on, check the facts and prepare a useful draft. Doing that once in a chat is helpful. Giving it a repeatable structure makes it easier to run again.

What do participants build?

The system has five plain-text files. Memory stores your background, targets and voice rules. Specialists give separate agents distinct jobs. Recipes set out the steps each follows. A conductor starts the work and passes results between specialists. A ledger records findings and activity so the system can avoid repeating work.

You can read and edit those files. That matters when your territory changes, your job search narrows or your editorial priorities move. Your instructions remain something you can inspect.

Which session track fits the task?

The job track looks for public signals such as new funding, leadership changes and expansion that may indicate an opportunity to approach a company. The sales track looks for buying signals relevant to what you sell. The story track reads public local sources and prepares angles and drafts for your organization’s blog.

Each track gives the system a specific job. A signal is a reason to investigate. You still decide whether it supports a useful conversation or a publishable story.

What should participants bring?

Bring a laptop, the desktop tool for your chosen edition, and a clear description of your target. The sessions offer Claude and ChatGPT editions. Check the current requirements for your edition before choosing a format, including the paid plan it needs.

The handout supplies numbered blocks. You paste them in order, with an explanation of each piece. The system interviews you to establish your background, targets and voice.

What happens the next morning?

The system reads the sources on the schedule you set and prepares findings and drafts. You check them. Nothing is sent or published until you decide. That review is part of the workflow, particularly for names, addresses, dates and inferred opportunities.

You can choose a recorded Build-Along, a small live Cohort or a private Working Session. Current formats are waitlist only while dates are set. Read the format details and pick the amount of help you want while building.
