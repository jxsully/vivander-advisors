Before interpreting an AI visibility score, open the answers behind it.

Michael J. Goldrich · September 29, 2026

Visibility · Article

A visibility score becomes useful when your organization’s team can inspect the prompts, recommendations and sources that produced it.

By Michael J. Goldrich, Vivander Advisors.

Open the underlying answers before deciding what an AI visibility score means for your organization. Vivander Visibility keeps that evidence available: what was asked, what the engine said, where your organization appeared, and which sources it cited.

A number can help leadership see a trend quickly. Your editor needs more detail. A hotel mentioned in an answer about parking and a hotel recommended for a family stay create different opportunities for action.

Which distinction matters when reviewing an AI answer?

Ask whether the business was offered as an option, whether its own website was cited at the mention, and whether it was put forward as a pick for the buyer. The platform uses a defined rubric to assign those tiers. It also records sentiment and position so the team can inspect more than the presence of a name.

In Vivander’s published hospitality audit, the property was named in 831 of 1,689 measurable runs (49.2%) and recommended in 613 (36.3%). Being named and being recommended were separate measures. The historical study used six platforms; the current audit package covers seven. These figures are a baseline, not evidence of a later improvement. Read the anonymized hospitality case and its evidence.

What does the score measure?

The platform separates the audit’s Visibility Stack Score from the Live Visibility Score used in monitoring. The audit score is diagnostic, covering technical foundations, content, outside sources and AI answers. The live score follows seven weighted components over the last four completed cycles.

Those components include visibility, share of voice, sentiment, platform consistency, prominence, description quality and source authority. The published methodology explains the rules. Reading it helps your organization’s team understand the instrument before setting a target.

What should your organization inspect when a score rises?

Find the questions and engines contributing to the change. Check whether the business was recommended more often, cited more often, or described differently. Review the sources and the recorded changes to the cohort or method. A rise has more practical value when someone can explain the answers behind it.

The platform reports a noise range and records changes that affect comparisons. Avoid assigning a business meaning to every small movement. A stable reading across completed cycles is more informative than a favorable answer selected from one afternoon.

Which question should leadership ask next?

Ask what the team will do with the finding. If an accurate policy is missing from a frequently cited listing, assign the correction. If a buyer question has no clear answer on your organization’s site, brief the editor. Then record the action and revisit the relevant answers.

The score helps organize attention. The saved answers help decide the work. Together, they make the review useful to the people responsible for changing the customer experience.
