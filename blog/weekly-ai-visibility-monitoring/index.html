Did website changes improve your organization’s AI visibility?

Michael J. Goldrich · October 3, 2026

Visibility · Article

Weekly AI visibility monitoring gives your organization’s team a record of buyer questions, saved answers and changes over time.

By Michael J. Goldrich, Vivander Advisors.

Repeat the same buyer questions, preserve the answers, and compare results over time. That is how you begin to assess whether the answers customers see changed after your organization’s website work. Vivander Visibility asks AI tools your organization’s buyers’ questions every week and keeps the answers behind its numbers.

A new parking page may be accurate and useful. Publishing it establishes what your organization’s site says. Whether an AI assistant uses it when recommending a hotel is another observation. Your organization’s team needs both.

Why keep the questions fixed?

A trend needs a consistent basis. If one week asks about family stays and the next asks about business travel, a different result may reflect the question set. Vivander Visibility uses a locked, versioned core of 24 buyer prompts for its headline score. A change to that core is recorded and marks a break in the trend.

Additional prompts can be watched alongside the core. They help explore new offers and customer needs without silently changing what the main score measures. Branded questions are reported separately because including your organization’s name changes the task the AI is answering.

Why ask more than once?

An AI answer can vary between tries. The platform asks each core prompt three times per cycle on every enabled engine in the plan, up to six. It records the full text, tier, sentiment, position and cited sources. Your organization’s team can inspect the answer that produced a reading.

Imagine a fictional inn added a clear parking policy. One answer uses it and another relies on an older directory description. Saving both tells the team more than a screenshot of the favorable result.

What should a weekly review cover?

Start with the relevant questions. Where did you become a stronger option? Where did a competitor appear without you? Did an answer repeat an incorrect fact? Which pages were cited? Then compare those observations with the dates of your edits.

The platform’s methodology explains how the readings and scores are calculated. The live score uses the last four completed cycles. Small movement can sit within the reported noise range, so every change should be read in context.

Which decisions can monitoring support?

It gives the team evidence for the next action. A new citation may support continuing a content approach. Persistent wrong information may call for a source correction. A rival’s rise may warrant examining the sources behind its answers.

Visibility is a leading indicator. Pair it with traffic, inquiries and booking records to understand business outcomes. A score alone cannot tell you what revenue an answer produced.
