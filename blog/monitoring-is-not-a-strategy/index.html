Monitoring is not a strategy. Here is what a hotel does with the number.

Profound says monitoring isn’t a strategy. We agree. The four-step system a hotel runs around the number, a 90-day walk-through week by week, and who owns it.

By Michael Goldrich, Vivander Advisors. Published September 27, 2026.

Profound’s comparison of its platform against Peec AI makes a point we agree with in full: “monitoring isn’t a strategy” (tryprofound.com, “Profound vs Peec AI,” 16 April 2026). A dashboard that tells a hotel it is put forward in 31 percent of the answers a guest gets has told it a fact. It has not told it what to do on Monday. The vendor that says so raised a $96 million Series C at a $1 billion valuation in February 2026 (checkthat.ai, checked 2026-09-25), and the sentence deserves to be taken further than a comparison page takes it.

A number is the last step of a system, and a hotel that buys the last step first will look at it for a quarter and cancel. This piece lays out the four steps, what the diagnostic finds that no dashboard can, what the plan looks like, a 90-day walk-through week by week, and who inside a hotel should own the whole thing. Every Vivander fact below comes from the platform’s own methodology (Observed: framework v3.53 to v3.65). The 90-day example is illustrative.

The number is the last step of four

Four steps, in order.

  • Assess. The Vivander Visibility Audit, a paid diagnostic. It builds a Verified Fact Block about the property, tests the locked prompt cohort across five platforms with multi-run sampling, reads the website, the crawler access, the structured data, the Google Business Profile, the directories, the booking flow and the off-site corpus, and sets the baseline: a Visibility Stack Score across four layers and a Live Visibility Score across seven components.
  • Strategize. A prioritized plan. The Executive Summary states five defining findings, and the Full Assessment holds the evidence. Findings are ranked by what they cost the property and what fixing them costs.
  • Implement. A step-by-step Implementation Plan the client’s team or agency executes, or Vivander executes. Every action carries a finding code, so the web developer, the marketing lead and the PR agency work from the same line items.
  • Track. The monitoring platform, which re-measures the locked prompt cohort weekly and scores it over four completed cycles, with an interval, so the hotel can see whether the plan worked.

Monitoring on its own is step four with steps one through three missing. It will report a movement without telling you whether the movement was your work, the platform’s model, or noise, and it cannot tell you what to do next, because it never learned why the number was low. Vivander sells monitoring on its own when a property asks for it. The baseline still comes from the diagnostic, because a number with no reason behind it is a number the hotel cannot act on.

What the diagnostic finds that a dashboard cannot

A dashboard reads AI answers. The diagnostic reads the property. Five findings show the difference; each is a class the audit’s method is built to catch.

  • A fact the site never states. At intake the client confirms a fact: the private room seats 30, the hotel has a rooftop bar open to non-guests, the resort is 12 minutes from the airport. Every verified fact is then checked three ways: AI against the fact, platform against platform, AI against the client’s own site. The highest-yield row is unsourced-but-true, a confirmed fact that the website never states. No dashboard can find it, because no dashboard knows the fact. The fix is publishing.
  • A directory that lists a rival. The audit runs a directory census by name for the vertical. A dining directory with a private-dining filter that lists the competitor and has no entry for the client is a row in the census. Monitoring sees the rival win the prompt; the census sees why.
  • A booking flow that breaks after one click. The booking handoff journey test is scripted to a declared boundary: it follows the path a guest follows from the AI answer to the reservation, and it records where the path stops. A property that is put forward and then loses the guest at the second click has a visibility problem no answer-monitor can see.
  • Unanswered owner replies on the Google Business Profile. The audit reads the profile in full, signed out, owner replies included. Owner replies are public, and in the audit they are captured with the rest of the profile. A profile with 40 unanswered reviews is a corroboration surface left empty.
  • Control scope at a branded property. For a branded, franchise or managed property, the intake asks one question, the ownership model, and every recommendation is tagged property, brand or none. A hotel under a flag does not control its brand-domain page. The plan says so, and spends the property’s effort on the surfaces it owns.

Each of these is a reason, and a reason is what turns a number into work.

The plan: every action tagged

The Implementation Plan is a list of actions, each with a finding code that traces to the evidence in the Full Assessment. Those codes belong in the deliverable rather than in this article; the idea is simple: a marketing lead can point at any line in the plan and open the page in the assessment that proves the problem exists.

For an independent property, the plan is sequenced by yield and cost. Publishing an unsourced-but-true fact costs an afternoon. Earning a directory listing costs a form and a wait. Pitching a trade feature costs a month. The plan puts them in that order.

For a branded, franchise or managed property, every action also carries a Control tag, and the rendered plan has three sections: what is within the property’s reach now, what is submitted to the brand with the request path named, and what is recorded and not actionable at the property. Measurement is the same for both kinds of hotel, so the scores stay comparable. The plan is where the difference shows.

Ninety days, week by week (illustrative)

Every detail in this walk-through is invented to show the shape of a quarter. The property is a 180-room independent hotel in a mid-sized US city with a rooftop bar, a 200-person ballroom and a restaurant with a private room. Its general manager owns the number, and its marketing manager owns the plan.

  • Week 1. Intake. The ownership model is independent. A Verified Fact Block is built and dated: room count, ballroom capacity, the rooftop bar’s hours and its policy on non-guests, the private room’s capacity, the airport time. ICP vocabulary is collected from the sales team and the booking engine’s search log. Prompts are drafted in five buyer voices.
  • Week 2. Collection. The 24 core prompts and at least 20 branded prompts run on five platforms with multi-run sampling. Site pages are crawled and captured. The Google Business Profile is read in full. A directory census runs against the named directories for hotels, meetings and dining in the market. The booking flow is tested from the AI answer to the reservation screen.
  • Week 3. Findings and plan. Five defining findings land: the ballroom capacity is stated nowhere on the site; the rooftop bar is on no dining directory while a rival’s bar is on three; the booking flow drops mobile users at the rate selection step; 61 reviews on the Google Business Profile carry no owner reply; the Live Visibility Score baseline is 34, band Reactive. The plan is delivered with every action coded. Version 1 of the cohort is locked, and the first weekly cycle runs.
  • Weeks 4 to 5. Publishing. The marketing manager writes the meetings page with the ballroom capacity, the setup styles and the AV, and the rooftop bar page with hours and the non-guest policy. Her web developer fixes the mobile rate step. Cycles two and three land, and the platform shows partial windows and says so.
  • Weeks 6 to 7. Listings and replies. Submissions go to the three dining directories and the two meetings directories the census named, with capacity and hours copied from the site so the surfaces agree. The general manager and the restaurant manager split the 61 owner replies and clear them in two weeks. Cycle four lands. The first full four-cycle window is computed: 12 runs per cell, an interval of about plus or minus 2.5 points on the Visibility Rate (Observed: monitoring review, 2026-09-05).
  • Weeks 8 to 9. Earned media. The PR agency pitches the regional business journal on the ballroom and the trade title on the rooftop program. Review requests go out after every group event, asking guests to mention the room by name. Two directory entries go live.
  • Weeks 10 to 11. First movement. The Visibility Rate on the meetings prompts moves from 21 percent to 38 percent, outside the interval. Rooftop prompts move within their interval, because the directory entries went live in week nine and the platforms have not retrieved them yet. The marketing manager opens the answers feed and reads the citations to confirm which pages the platforms used.
  • Weeks 12 to 13. Review and next quarter. The Live Visibility Score reads 44, band Structured, labeled provisional because it sits within 3 points of the Reactive and Structured boundary. Beside it: “This score sits within sampling variance of the Reactive/Structured boundary. Treat the band assignment as provisional; the direction of movement across re-tests is the reliable signal.” The general manager reports the direction, the interval and the two findings closed. Next quarter’s plan starts with the business journal feature, which ran in week twelve and which Source Authority already counts from the corpus.

Thirteen weeks, two findings closed, two in motion, one waiting on a third party. The number moved because the hotel moved, and the hotel can show the answers that prove it.

How the platform keeps the plan honest

Tracking is not the strategy. It is what keeps the strategy from drifting into hope. Three of its rules do most of the work.

  • First defensible delta after four cycles. The live score is computed over the last four completed cycles, 12 runs per prompt-and-platform cell, against the published recommended minimum of 7 to 8 repetitions (Schulte et al., 2026). Before the fourth cycle the platform shows partial windows and labels them. A property that wants to celebrate in week two is told to wait, in writing.
  • Provisional bands. Any band within 3 points of a boundary is labeled provisional, with the sentence quoted above. A hotel at 41 does not get to tell ownership it has crossed into Structured. It gets to tell ownership the direction of movement, which is the reliable signal.
  • The answers feed. Every number clicks through to the prompts and the AI answers behind it, with the tier, the sentiment, the citations and the competitors for every run. When the number moves, the marketing manager reads why before the general manager reports it. If it does not move, the same feed shows which source the answers still leaned on, and the plan is revised from evidence.

Add the rules from the comparability side: a model change is a recorded break, a cohort change with no bridge prints “not comparable” and no number, a quarterly control run publishes instrument drift beside the score, and a daily fleet canary keeps a capture failure from reading as a fall. A plan measured this way cannot claim credit it did not earn, and it cannot be blamed for a fall it did not cause.

Who should own this inside a hotel

The general manager owns the number. It goes in the monthly ownership report next to RevPAR, with the interval and the band, and the general manager is the one who says “provisional” out loud.

The marketing lead owns the plan. It is a list of coded actions, and the marketing lead assigns each line to a web developer, a PR agency or a colleague and reports which lines closed. In the walk-through above, four hours a week covers it.

The director of sales owns the group and dining surfaces, because those are the prompts a planner asks, and the directories and trade titles that answer them are the ones sales already knows.

Booking flow belongs to the revenue manager, because the journey test ends at the reservation screen, and the reservation screen is theirs.

At a branded or managed property, one more person: whoever holds the relationship with the brand’s digital team owns the actions tagged brand, and the request path in the Control scope table is their to-do list.

Nobody on this list is an SEO specialist. The system was built so that none is needed.

What to do with this

Write the four steps on a whiteboard and mark which ones your property has done. If step four is the only one marked, in the form of a screenshot or a free trial, the number on the screenshot is real and you have no way to move it. Start with the diagnostic.
