AI says your organization offers something it does not. Where does the correction begin?

Michael J. Goldrich · October 1, 2026

Audit · Article

Trace an incorrect AI answer to the facts and sources behind it, then give the correction an owner and a retest.

By Michael J. Goldrich, Vivander Advisors.

Begin by saving the answer and checking its claims against facts your organization has confirmed. Then investigate the sources that carry the wrong information. The Vivander Visibility Audit includes that work because being visible with an incorrect policy creates a different problem from being absent.

A guest who is told cats are welcome may book a stay at a dogs-only property. The disagreement reaches the front desk after the decision has already been made. Your organization’s team needs to understand what was said, where the information appears, and which correction it controls.

Which facts deserve attention first?

Start with facts that could change a purchase: eligibility, access, opening hours, prices, policies, capacity and location. Record the exact claim. “AI describes us badly” is too broad to act on. “The answer says our restaurant has a private room for 40; the confirmed capacity is 24” gives the team a testable finding.

Use an internal source of truth that someone responsible has checked. A policy that changed last month needs a date. A capacity figure needs agreement about layout and use. The correction should be accurate before it is distributed.

Why examine sources beyond your organization’s website?

AI answers may draw on directories, travel listings, reviews or articles. An accurate policy on your organization’s site can sit beside an outdated policy elsewhere. The audit examines what AI says against the facts you confirm and investigates the sources behind errors.

In the published resort example, incorrect pet information was traced to travel-agency listings. That is a result from one audit, with its own circumstances. It demonstrates the value of identifying a source and an action rather than assuming every error needs more website copy.

Who makes the correction?

Assign each surface to the person who can change it. Your editor may update the policy page today. A distribution partner may need a correction request. A brand office may control another page. Keep the request, submission date and confirmed update together.

In Vivander’s published hospitality audit, two booking sources said dogs and cats, while the verified brand overview said dogs only and the property FAQ did not specify species. Five of six platforms repeated the broader policy. The recommended action was to correct the affected sources and publish the verified policy clearly. Follow-up results had not yet been reported. Read the anonymized hospitality case and its evidence.

How should your organization assess whether corrections worked?

Check the source after the edit, then repeat the relevant AI questions and save the answers. A corrected listing and a changed AI answer are separate observations. The audit’s follow-up retests help assess that distinction. Your organization’s goal is an accurate buying decision, with a record your organization’s team can inspect.
