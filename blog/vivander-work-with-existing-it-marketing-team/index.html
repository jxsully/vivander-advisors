Can Vivander work with our existing IT team, agency and vendors?

Michael J. Goldrich · August 28, 2026

Vivander Answers · 7 of 10

By Michael J. Goldrich, Founder and Chief Advisor, Vivander Advisors.

Yes. I can work within your existing team and alongside IT specialists, agencies and vendors. We should clarify who owns the business goal, approves tools and data use, implements changes and reviews results. Vivander’s role is scoped around the leadership, advisory or execution support your organization needs.

Why involve the existing team early?

Your team understands customers and operating constraints. Vendors may control systems or content the initiative depends on. Leaving them out can create recommendations nobody is able or authorized to implement.

I want to understand current work before proposing changes. An agency may already have a content process while IT has approved tools and security requirements. A practical strategy uses that information to identify dependencies and decisions.

How would responsibilities be divided?

Name a business sponsor, workflow owner, technical owner and people responsible for review. Vivander can support prioritization, coordination, learning and progress review within the agreed scope. Vendors can continue delivering their contracted work.

A responsibility map should describe decisions as well as tasks. Who approves a tool? Who updates the website? Who signs off on customer content? Who decides to expand a pilot? Those questions keep work from stalling between functions.

What if the project involves visibility?

An audit might identify website changes, directory corrections and new buyer-answer content. Your agency could implement edits while an internal owner verifies service facts. Another person may need to claim an external listing.

The visibility audit can provide priorities and evidence. We agree which recommendations Vivander helps implement and which belong to your partners. A report alone does not assign access or authority.

What if the project involves company rollout?

Rollout may need managers to define workflows, IT to review access and employees to practice new habits. Fractional leadership can connect these efforts and maintain a record of decisions. Your business still needs an accountable sponsor.

For a fictional operations team, we might pilot an AI-assisted brief. The team supplies approved sources, IT confirms tool constraints, managers define quality standards and participants test the workflow. Responsibilities remain visible throughout.

What should the first coordination meeting settle?

Bring the sponsor, workflow owner and relevant partners together around one business objective. Confirm current commitments, available capacity, access dependencies and the decisions that need specialist review. Agree where actions are recorded and how disagreements are resolved. If two vendors depend on each other’s work, make that dependency explicit. This prevents an apparently simple recommendation from turning into an unowned request that moves from inbox to inbox without a decision.

How do you keep the work sustainable?

Agree where documentation lives, how decisions are reviewed and who maintains the workflow afterward. Handover can include the roadmap, reviewed examples, unresolved issues and criteria for future changes, depending on scope.

Bring your team structure and what partners already do. We can discuss fractional leadership, training or a narrower engagement. Clear ownership is part of the work from the beginning.

Related questions

Must we replace our marketing agency?

No. Vivander can collaborate with your agency, with implementation responsibilities agreed for the engagement.

Will Vivander approve our security policies?

Your authorized owners and relevant specialists retain those decisions. Vivander can help coordinate requirements within its scope.

Can you hand the work back to our team?

Yes. Handover, documentation and ownership expectations should be defined at the start.
