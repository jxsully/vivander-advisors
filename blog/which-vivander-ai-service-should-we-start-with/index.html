Which Vivander AI service should your organization start with?

Michael J. Goldrich · October 9, 2026

Vivander Answers · 1 of 10

By Michael J. Goldrich, Founder and Chief Advisor, Vivander Advisors.

Vivander starts with the business problem. If buyers cannot find or accurately understand your organization through AI, Vivander examines visibility. If your organization’s team needs a company AI strategy and help putting it into practice, Vivander discusses fractional leadership. If employees need better skills and judgment, Vivander discusses AI fluency training.

What does Vivander Advisors actually do?

Vivander Advisors connects AI strategy, visibility and fluency to practical business work. Vivander provides advisory services, training, speaking, research and writing. We also offer visibility products and are developing personal agent products. These are different ways of helping a business, and the right starting point depends on the decision in front of you.

I want to understand what is getting in the way: missing customer inquiries, uncertain AI answers, disconnected experiments, repetitive work or employees who do not know when to trust an output. A request to “do something with AI” needs to become a business problem with an owner.

When should Vivander starts with a visibility audit?

An audit fits when your organization needs to understand what AI tells potential buyers about your organization. Vivander examines relevant buyer questions, mentions, recommendations, citations, factual accuracy and sources. The outcome should be a prioritized set of improvements tied to evidence.

For example, Vivander’s published hospitality audit found a recognized property named in 831 of 1,689 measurable runs but recommended in 613. It also found inaccurate pet-policy answers and gaps in the public source record. That evidence supports a prioritized correction plan without claiming that an omission proves a lost booking. Explore the Vivander Visibility Audit for the current defined offer. Read the anonymized hospitality case and its evidence.

When does monitoring make more sense?

Monitoring fits when your organization needs a recurring record across a consistent set of buyer questions. It helps distinguish a single favorable answer from a pattern. You can compare answers and sources over time, then decide which changes deserve attention.

An audit asks what to address now; monitoring asks what changed after you acted. The measurement scope matters. Review Visibility Monitoring rather than assuming a score represents every possible customer conversation.

When should your organization choose fractional leadership or training?

Fractional leadership fits when priorities, decisions and rollout need ongoing coordination inside your organization’s existing team. Training fits when the main need is employee capability. They can work together: a rollout may reveal that managers need evaluation skills while employees need practice with a specific task.

You do not need to replace the team that knows your organization. Vivander can work alongside it to connect strategy, pilots, responsibilities and learning. Compare fractional AI leadership with AI fluency training.

A quick way to prepare an engagement brief

Write down the buyer or employee problem in one sentence. Add the decision you need to make, the current evidence and the person who will own the next step. For example: “We need to understand whether AI describes our service accurately before rewriting the site.” That brief points toward visibility work. “We have three pilots and nobody owns company rollout” points toward leadership. Sharing the decision is more useful than choosing a service by its name alone.

What should your organization bring to the first conversation?

Bring one business priority, an example of work you want to improve, the people involved and any tools or constraints. For visibility, bring questions customers ask before buying. For adoption, bring a workflow and its current quality or effort problem.

Vivander can then discuss a defined first engagement and how your organization’s team will use the outputs. Starting small is useful when it produces evidence for the next decision. A broad wish list without ownership usually makes the scope harder to manage.

Related questions

No. Vivander starts with the business problem and agree a scope that fits it. An audit, training or fractional leadership can be a separate engagement.

Yes. Vivander can work with existing team members and vendors, with responsibilities and decision rights agreed in the scope.
