Can Vivander help us put personal AI agents to useful work?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 4, 2026

Vivander Answers · 6 of 10

By Michael J. Goldrich, Founder and Chief Advisor, Vivander Advisors.

I can help identify a useful agent workflow, define the information it needs and decide where human review belongs. The advisory scope should match your tools and constraints. Sessions and MyTeam are under development and testing, so I would not plan a business rollout assuming every proposed product capability is already available.

What makes a task suitable for an agent?

Look for repeatable preparation or coordination with a clear purpose and a person who can review the result. The task needs defined inputs, an expected output and a way to recognize mistakes. “Run my business” leaves too many decisions unresolved.

A fictional manager might want a briefing assembled from approved notes and a task list. We would specify available sources, which details belong in the briefing and what the manager must confirm. That is a narrower, testable starting point.

How is an agent different from an occasional chat?

An agent workflow can involve a repeatable sequence, retained context or permitted tool connections. Behavior depends on implementation. Calling something an agent does not prove it is reliable, integrated or authorized to act for a user.

I ask what happens at each step, what information it accesses and what it does when a source is missing. The useful question is how work improves, not whether the demonstration uses a fashionable label.

Where do Sessions and MyTeam fit?

Sessions is Vivander’s agent-builder product direction. MyTeam explores personal agents supporting preparation, commitments and review. Both are under development and testing. Their pages describe current direction and status.

Review Sessions and MyTeam, then confirm availability and scope directly. A consultation about an agent workflow is not a promise of access to a finished subscription or every integration.

What should require human approval?

Approval boundaries depend on the task. A briefing may require review before use. Sending customer messages, changing records or committing money needs a clearly authorized process. Your company’s requirements determine the boundaries.

Define permitted actions, access, exceptions and escalation. If a workflow cannot distinguish instructions from untrusted source material, address that design issue before widening its authority. Relevant specialists should review their areas of responsibility.

What should an agent brief contain?

Describe the task, approved inputs, output format, source freshness requirements and review owner. Name the actions it may take and those that require approval. Include examples of a good result and an unacceptable one. Specify what should happen when information is missing or contradictory. This brief does not replace technical design, but it makes the business intent reviewable before anyone connects tools or grants access. It also gives a pilot something concrete to test.

How should we assess a first pilot?

Measure the whole task, including checking and correction. Compare outputs with the current way of working and record failures such as missing evidence, stale context or incorrect commitments. An agent should make work easier to manage instead of moving effort into cleanup.

The personal AI agents guide can help you choose a workflow. Bring one task, its approved information and the actions that must remain under human control. We can discuss a scoped advisory engagement.

Related questions

Are Sessions and MyTeam fully launched?

They are under development and testing. Confirm availability and supported capabilities with Vivander before planning around them.

Can an agent send messages without review?

Action authority must be explicitly designed and authorized. Approval boundaries depend on the task and your organization’s requirements.

Are all integrations included in advisory work?

No. Connections and technical implementation must be specified in scope, with the responsible internal team or vendor identified.
