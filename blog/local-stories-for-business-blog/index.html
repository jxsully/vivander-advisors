What should your organization’s blog say when the local news changes?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 19, 2026

Sessions · Article

Build a system that reads public local sources, checks practical details and prepares original story drafts for your review.

By Michael J. Goldrich, Vivander Advisors.

Your business blog should explain a local development your customers care about and connect it honestly to what you do. The Find Your Next Local News Story track in Agent Builder Sessions builds a system that reads public sources, checks details and prepares a draft for you to review.

A restaurant may have something useful to say about a new theater opening nearby. A hotel may help travelers understand a festival’s transport changes. The relevance comes from serving the reader’s decision, with enough local knowledge to add something useful.

Where does the story come from?

The system reads free public news pages, event calendars, listings and notices. It watches openings, closings, practical changes and seasonal moments. A recurring event can receive a write-by date so the draft arrives while the information is still useful.

That schedule addresses a familiar problem: the event is in two days and the business has only now remembered to write about it. Planning around the reader’s decision gives the editor time to verify, revise and publish.

What makes a local angle worth writing?

The angle needs a connection your organization can support. Imagine a fictional hotel near a new weekend market. A useful post could explain walking time, public transport, opening hours and how to plan a visit. Each practical claim needs verification. The hotel’s own experience can add a reason for a particular route or time of day.

The story track includes an angle agent that looks for that local perspective. An item without an honest angle returns to the calendar rather than becoming filler for the blog.

Which facts need checking?

Dates, times, venues, addresses and prices should come from the source page. The system’s fact checker verifies those details and checks that the day agrees with the date. Your review should also consider last-minute changes and any facts supplied from your own experience.

The system reads public information and prepares original writing. It does not go behind paywalls or copy an article. Linking to the source helps readers check the event information for themselves.

Who decides what gets published?

You do. The system prepares a draft in your voice and keeps an editorial calendar. You assess the angle, inspect the sources and shape the final post. Nothing publishes until you decide.

The session teaches the same five-file structure used in the other tracks: memory, specialists, recipes, conductor and ledger. The result is a recurring preparation process you can inspect and adjust. See the Local News Story track for current formats and availability.
