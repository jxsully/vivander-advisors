What does it cost to work with Vivander Advisors, and how do we start?

Michael J. Goldrich · August 7, 2026

Vivander Answers · 10 of 10

By Michael J. Goldrich, Founder and Chief Advisor, Vivander Advisors.

Cost depends on the service and agreed scope. I first clarify the problem, deliverables, team involvement and timing, then discuss an engagement and fee. Vivander does not publish a universal advisory rate for every situation. We should agree what is included before work begins.

Why is there no single price for everything?

A visibility audit, training program, research project and ongoing fractional leadership involve different work. Audiences, workflows, sources, review rounds and implementation responsibilities affect scope. A single rate would not explain those differences.

Distinguish a defined project from ongoing involvement. A report with agreed deliverables differs from leadership inside your team over time. The proposal should make the commercial model understandable.

What information helps scope the work?

Bring the goal, issue, people involved and outcome you want. Include existing tools, constraints, decision makers, timing and what your team can implement. You can state a budget range if you have one.

For visibility, bring buyer questions and examples of missing or inaccurate information. For training, bring the audience and tasks. For fractional leadership, bring priorities, experiments and team structure. Each helps define useful work.

What should the proposal specify?

It should identify work, deliverables, timing, responsibilities and fees. Where relevant, explain assumptions, dependencies, reviews and what requires a scope change. Your company should know who makes decisions and how outputs will be used.

An advisory plan should not leave you guessing whether website implementation or integration is included. A training proposal should identify audiences and formats. Research needs a defined method and review process. We discuss details that matter to the chosen service.

Can we begin with a smaller project?

A defined first project can produce evidence for the next decision. It could be an audit, workshop or advisory scope around a workflow. We discuss whether that structure fits the problem.

Starting smaller does not mean ignoring prerequisites. A pilot still needs appropriate access, ownership and review. Narrow work is valuable when boundaries and outputs are clear; it should not be presented as a complete company rollout.

What is a useful first message?

Describe the problem in a few sentences, name the service you are considering if you have one, and include the timing and decision owner. You do not need to prepare a complete strategy before contacting us. A concrete example of work or a buyer question is enough to make the discussion more productive. Avoid sending confidential source material before the information-sharing arrangements are clear. We can identify what is needed after defining the likely scope.

What happens after we proceed?

We confirm scope and practical arrangements, identify participants and establish the information needed to begin. Progress reviews and handover should fit the engagement. Ongoing support is agreed rather than assumed.

Use the contact page to describe the problem, or review services and products. I would rather start with a clear question than ask you to choose a broad package without understanding the need.

Related questions

Is there one published hourly rate for all services?

No universal advisory rate is published for every engagement. Scope, deliverables, responsibilities and timing inform the fee discussion.

Do we need a long-term engagement to start?

Not necessarily. A defined project or workshop may fit the need. We discuss structure before agreeing an engagement.

Is implementation automatically included?

Implementation, technical work and ongoing support must be specified in the agreed scope.
