An AI visibility audit should give your organization a clear action plan.

Michael J. Goldrich · October 5, 2026

Audit · Article

A practical audit connects each finding to an action, a responsible owner and a way to check the result.

By Michael J. Goldrich, Vivander Advisors.

A useful AI visibility audit leaves your organization’s team with a prioritized work list: what to change, why it matters, who can make the change, and how to check the result. The Vivander Visibility Audit is built around that handoff. The findings should survive the presentation and become work someone can finish.

The problem often appears after a report lands. Leadership sees a score. Marketing sees a request for more content. The website agency sees another technical brief. Nobody knows which action comes first or what would count as completion.

What makes a finding actionable?

A finding should name the buyer question and the evidence behind the proposed change. In Vivander’s published hospitality audit, five of six platforms repeated a pet policy that conflicted with the verified brand overview. The practical assignment was to confirm the dogs-only policy, state it clearly in the property FAQ and reconcile conflicting booking-source information. The case records a recommended correction, not a measured post-edit improvement. Read the anonymized hospitality case and its evidence.

That assignment still needs context. Is the fact missing from listings as well? Can AI retrieve the page? Does a brand team control the copy? The sequence follows the diagnosed constraint.

How should a property and brand divide the work?

Separate actions by control. A property may manage its Google listing, local photography and policy details. A brand may manage the booking path and the main website. A third-party directory requires a request. Each action needs the owner who can move it.

The audit’s after-the-audit guidance explains this approach. An action tagged for the property can begin immediately. A brand request should include the finding, the proposed edit and the supporting evidence so it can be evaluated.

What belongs in the first working meeting?

Pick the highest-priority actions the team can execute. Agree on the exact change, the responsible person and a completion date. Preserve the original page or listing so the team can establish what changed. Keep questions about broader strategy visible without letting them stall a straightforward factual correction.

For each completed action, record the date and the affected surface. That record becomes essential when later answers change. Several edits made at once may make individual effects difficult to separate.

What happens after the edits?

Retests at 60 and 90 days are available only if the client signing up for the audit wants them. They are optional, not automatic. Ongoing Vivander Visibility monitoring can track buyer questions between those checkpoints. Measurement shows how answers changed; it does not automatically establish why they changed.

The Monday test is simple: can each owner explain the next action without reopening the presentation? If so, the audit has become something the business can use.
