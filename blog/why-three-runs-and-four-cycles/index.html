Why we ask every question three times and score over four weeks

AI answers change from run to run. The statistics behind three runs a week, twelve runs a window and the Wilson interval, in plain language for hotel operators.

By Michael Goldrich, Vivander Advisors. Published September 27, 2026.

Ask ChatGPT for a hotel near the convention center with a rooftop bar. An hour later, ask again. The lists will overlap and they will not match. Tomorrow, ask a third time, and a hotel that appeared twice may be gone.

Nothing is broken. That is what a probabilistic system does, and any measurement of it has to be built around the fact. This piece explains, in plain language, why the Vivander platform runs every question three times a week and scores over four weeks, what the printed interval means, and what the approach costs in patience.

The system is probabilistic, and the figures are published

Three findings from the research the Vivander Master Framework cites set the terms.

For the same prompt on the same platform, the overlap between the sources cited on one day and the next runs 0.34 to 0.42 (Schulte et al., 2026, as cited in the Vivander Master Framework). On a scale where 1.0 means identical sources, that is well under half.

Between 9 and 28 percent of decisions change from one run to the next even at temperature zero, the setting that is supposed to make a model repeat itself (Kirsten et al., as cited in the framework). Consumer products do not run at temperature zero.

The same research puts the recommended minimum at 7 to 8 repetitions before a reading of a single prompt is stable enough to report (Schulte et al., 2026, as cited in the framework).

One more, from a vendor rather than a paper: Profound reports from its own research that 40 to 60 percent of cited domains change monthly across answer engines (Profound, as published on its site, checked 2026-09-25).

Together those say one thing. A single run of a single prompt is one draw from a distribution, and a serious reading of that prompt needs enough draws for the distribution’s center to show through the noise.

What one run tells you

Think of a Tuesday dinner service. One Tuesday’s covers tell you something about Tuesdays. They do not tell you the Tuesday average, and a restaurateur who changed the menu because one Tuesday was slow would be reacting to noise. Eight Tuesdays start to tell you the average. Twelve tell you the average and how much it moves.

One AI run is one Tuesday. A daily dashboard that runs each prompt once and plots the result is plotting individual Tuesdays and drawing a trend line through them.

Why three runs, and why in separate passes

The Vivander platform runs every core prompt on every platform three times per weekly cycle (Observed: platform methodology, framework v3.53 to v3.65). Three is the smallest number that yields a stability reading: two or three appearances out of three reads as stable presence for that question on that platform, and one out of three reads as intermittent.

The three runs are taken in three separate passes rather than back to back. In our operation of the platform, a prompt run three times within seconds can return three readings of one cached state, which is one sample counted three times (Observed: Vivander platform operation). Separate passes make the three samples three samples.

Across 24 unbranded prompts and five platforms, three runs is 360 scored runs per cycle. Three branded prompts ride along, recorded and never scored, for 405 runs in all.

Why four cycles

Three runs per cell per week sits below the published minimum of 7 to 8. Four completed cycles bring each prompt-and-platform cell to 12 runs, above the minimum with margin (Observed: platform methodology). The live score is computed over the last four completed cycles. When a new cycle lands, the oldest one leaves the window, and the score moves. Between cycles it does not move at all, because nothing has been measured.

A score that moves only when a cycle lands is a score that can be explained. Every movement has a cycle behind it, and every cycle has 360 answers behind it.

The interval, in plain words

Every rate the platform prints carries a 95 percent Wilson interval computed on its own denominator: Visibility Rate, Share of Voice and Platform Consistency each get their own (Observed: platform methodology). The interval is the honest width of the number. A Visibility Rate of 41 with an interval of plus or minus 2.5 says: the true rate on this cohort, this window, is very likely between 38.5 and 43.5.

The width depends on the number of runs. At 12 runs per cell the interval on the Visibility Rate is about plus or minus 2.5 points. With 3 runs per cell it is about plus or minus 5 (Observed: Vivander monitoring review, 2026-09-05). A single run per cell has an interval wide enough that most of what a daily dashboard calls movement sits inside it.

The platform uses Wilson rather than the simpler textbook formula because Wilson behaves properly near 0 and 100 percent, which is exactly where a property that is nearly invisible or nearly always recommended will sit.

The floor of ten

A component measured on fewer than 10 qualifying cells prints “insufficient data” with its n, and the composite reweights across the components that did compute (Observed: platform methodology). Six cells would give an interval wider than the number itself. The platform declines to print it. A client reads “insufficient data, n = 6” and knows why, rather than reading a number that will be quoted in a board deck.

The provisional band

Scores fall into five bands: Invisible 0 to 19, Reactive 20 to 39, Structured 40 to 59, Integrated 60 to 79, Optimized 80 to 100 (Observed: platform methodology). A band within 3 points of a boundary is labeled provisional, and the platform prints this sentence beside it:

“This score sits within sampling variance of the [band]/[band] boundary. Treat the band assignment as provisional; the direction of movement across re-tests is the reliable signal.”

Read the last clause twice. A score of 41 is Structured. One of 39 is Reactive. Between those two lies less than the interval, so the label means less than the movement. A property that reads 39, then 41, then 43 across three windows is moving up, and that is the finding. The band it happens to occupy on any given window is a description rather than a verdict.

A worked example (illustrative)

A Boston restaurant group’s flagship, a 90-seat room in the Seaport with a 20-seat private dining room. Every figure here is illustrative.

Take one cell: the prompt “private dining room for 20 in the Seaport” on Perplexity. Over four cycles the restaurant is offered in 6 of 12 runs. The cell’s rate is 50 percent.

A daily tool reporting each run on its own would have printed 0 percent and 100 percent on alternate days for this cell. Reporting each cycle on its own, a weekly tool would have printed 33, 67, 67, 33, and a GM would have asked what went wrong in cycle 4. The window reading is 50, with a width, and it says the restaurant is at the edge of Perplexity’s shortlist for that question: present when the platform draws on one set of sources, absent when it draws on another.

That is a corroboration finding. The diagnostic’s influence ledger shows which sources Perplexity leaned on in the runs where a rival was offered instead, and the plan says where the restaurant needs to state its private-room capacity so the fact reads as established rather than claimed.

Comparability, recorded and never smoothed

Two more rules protect the number. A change of AI model on any platform is recorded as a break and flagged inside the window, because a model change can move a rate with no change at the property. Any cohort change with no computed bridge produces a cell that reads “not comparable: cohort v1 to v2” and carries no number (Observed: platform methodology). That second rule was written after a cohort change was reported to a client as a 19-point decline. A quarterly control run re-measures every client’s cohort against a pinned reference model and publishes instrument drift beside the score, never inside it.

What this costs in patience

The first defensible delta prints after the first four cycles. Not after the first week, and not the morning after a fix goes live. A GM who publishes the private-room capacity in week three will see that fact enter the window as the cycles land, and will see the cell’s rate move with a stated width by the end of the first window.

That is slower than a daily dashboard. It is also the honest trade. A daily number that swings gives a GM something to look at every morning and nothing to act on. The four-cycle number with an interval gives the GM one thing to act on a month, and the confidence that acting on it is not chasing noise.

What to do with this

Ask any vendor how many runs per prompt per week, and whether the three are taken in separate passes.

Then ask whether the number prints an interval, and how wide it is at the vendor’s run count.

Find out what the tool prints when the cohort changes or a platform swaps its model. If the answer is “the number keeps going,” the number is being smoothed.

Set the expectation inside your own team as well. The first window closes after four cycles. Read the direction across windows. Treat the band as provisional whenever the platform says it is.
