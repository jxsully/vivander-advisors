HSMAI NYC Data Driving Decisions: Jan Freitag of Costar on NYC

Michael J. Goldrich · June 3, 2025

Hospitality’s Resilience and Emerging Challenges

The Peninsula New York hosted a capacity audience of hospitality leaders for an HSMAI NYC breakfast, where Jan D. Freitag, National Director of Hospitality Market Analytics for CoStar Group, dissected the current state of the U.S. and New York City hotel markets. His presentation, following the NYU Hotel Investment Conference, outlined a U.S. landscape characterized by “bumpy” conditions and a New York City market demonstrating notable resilience alongside specific challenges. The Peninsula New York was thanked for its excellent arrangements.

Freitag conveyed that while CoStar does not predict a recession, a “market slowdown” is evident, influencing both consumer and corporate decisions.

Top 5 U.S. Hospitality Insights from Jan Freitag:

  1. Macro Environment: “Bumpy” with Consumer Caution: Freitag described the overall U.S. economic climate for hospitality as “bumpy.” He pointed to Tourism Economics/Oxford Economics GDP growth forecasts that suggest continued, albeit slower, growth. A key factor is consumer sentiment, illustrated by the University of Michigan survey, which shows a disconnect: current feelings might be adequate, but future anxiety is high. This, Freitag explained, “crushed” booking windows, especially for leisure. “I was presenting in Nashville last week and I asked her what’s the summer and they’re like June’s good, July’s okay… honestly when did June get good? Well, two weeks ago.” This necessitates agile strategies from hoteliers.
  2. National Performance: Slow Growth, ADR Lagging Inflation: The CoStar forecast for the U.S. hotel industry suggests supply and demand are largely in equilibrium, with new construction financing proving “extremely impossible to get done right now.” Overall Average Daily Rate (ADR) growth is projected by CoStar and other analysts like CBRE around 1.3%. Freitag emphasized this is “well below the rate of inflation,” which hovers around 2.3-2.5%. “We’re not growing the top line as much as expenses grow, and that obviously has an implication on the bottom line,” he stated. Consequently, U.S. RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Room) is expected to grow by approximately 1%.
  3. Airline Commentary: Domestic Softness, Premium Strength: Insights from major airline earnings calls (United, Delta, American, Southwest) revealed a consistent theme: domestic leisure, or the “back of the bus” demand, is soft. Airlines cited factors like the resumption of student loan payments and general cost-of-living increases impacting discretionary spending. “The American leisure traveler is not as active on their flights and that means that they’re not as active in our hotels,” Freitag summarized. Conversely, premium cabin demand remains robust, with airlines like American stating “premium bookings are terrific,” indicating strength at the higher end of the travel market.
  4. Forward-Looking Occupancy: A Potential “False Negative”: Addressing forward-looking data, specifically occupancy on the books, Freitag cautioned against immediate pessimism. While data 90 days out may look weak, he suggested, “I’m honestly think that’s a false negative because of that blue line [consumer confidence in the future becoming the present].” As travel decisions are made closer to the date, these figures are expected to improve.
  5. International Inbound: Modest Overall Share, Notable Declines: Freitag highlighted that international inbound travelers constitute about 5% of total U.S. room nights sold (based on 60 million arrivals versus 1.3 billion room nights sold annually). While a smaller segment overall, he pointed to “murky news” and “deterioration,” citing Department of Commerce I-94 data and concerns from economists like Adam Sacks of Tourism Economics. Specific call-outs included a 33% drop in Canadian arrivals in a recent month, impacting markets reliant on drive-to demand.

Top 5 New York City Hospitality Insights from Jan Freitag:

  1. Strong Occupancy & Resilient RevPAR: “Life’s pretty good on the occupancy side” in New York City, Freitag declared, with levels frequently reaching 80-90%, and last week hitting over 90%. This outpaces many other U.S. markets. While ADR growth is “below the level of inflation,” it’s often stronger than the U.S. average, leading to a projected RevPAR growth of around 2% for the city. He noted the March/April data was “super funky” due to the Easter shift, which elongated the spring break cycle to nine weeks.
  2. Short-Term Rental Ban: A Boon for Hotels: The city’s crackdown on short-term rentals has significantly benefited hotels. Using data from AirDNA, Freitag illustrated a sharp decline in short-term rental nights sold in Manhattan. “Those rooms, if people are still coming and they can’t stay in a short-term rental, turns out that is hotel demand… I think this is absolutely part of what drives pricing power in this market,” he asserted, emphasizing its positive impact on both occupancy and, more significantly, ADR.
  3. Luxury Segment Thriving: The luxury hotel segment in New York is performing exceptionally well. “The upper end, the leisure, the luxury corporate transient traveler, the incentive traveler, they’re like ‘I want it and I want it now’… I’m happy to pay for it,” Freitag explained. This translates to robust ADRs, with four-digit room rates becoming increasingly common. “The experience that you get in return is the issue,” he added, noting these high rates come with high expectations. This demand is supported by strong occupancy in luxury properties.
  4. Group vs. Transient Dynamics: An NYC Anomaly: While transient demand (bookings of 1-9 rooms) in NYC has largely recovered to pre-pandemic levels, group business (10+ rooms) still has “a hole to fill,” often tied to the convention calendar at Javits Center. A peculiar NYC trend Freitag highlighted is that group ADR is often higher than transient ADR. “That is not true in New York City… I don’t understand this… This is not normal for how a lot of other markets price their rooms,” he stated, suggesting it warrants closer examination by local hoteliers.
  5. International Headwinds & Airline Adjustments: Despite overall strength, NYC faces international headwinds. Referencing I-94 data for JFK, LaGuardia, and Newark airports, Freitag showed international inbound down 10% versus 2019, and down 4% year-over-year for the recent period. Outbound international travel from these airports, however, is up. He also shared concerning news from European airlines: Lufthansa (JFK frequency reduction), British Airways (weak leisure bookings), Air France (weaker demand), and Scandinavian (Newark route canceled) are all seeing or anticipating reduced demand for U.S. travel, citing “decline in US interest.”

During the event, Freitag gave a warm send-off to Donna Keren, the NYC&CO head of research, a long-time HSMAI supporter, who is retiring. He also introduced CoStar’s new East Coast analyst.

Generative AI: The “Sharp Left Turn” Impacting Travel

Freitag dedicated a segment to the burgeoning influence of Generative AI on the travel industry, calling it a “sharp left turn” that demands attention. He cited a Deloitte Summer Travel Survey indicating that nearly half of travelers (48% for destinations, 46% for accommodations) are already using GenAI for research. “Customers are using it to figure out, ‘I’m going to New York, what should I do?'” he said. He envisioned a near future where AI not only provides recommendations but also handles bookings directly: “We are I think only this far away from ‘Oh, and now book me that.’ That is absolutely 100% coming.”

While acknowledging CoStar’s current internal restrictions on using public GenAI tools with proprietary data, Freitag stressed that hotels will inevitably integrate AI. “Revenue management is absolutely a clear use case because there’s so much data coming your way you can’t parse it yourself,” he predicted. However, he advised a cautious approach to current vendor offerings: “I think it’s early days, so just hold off on that purchase please, please, please.” The key message was that customer use of AI is present and growing, fundamentally changing how travel is researched and will be booked.

Key Takeaways for Hotel Commercial Teams:

  1. Master Agility for Short Booking Windows: With U.S. leisure travelers delaying decisions, marketing campaigns and revenue management must be highly flexible to capture near-term demand. Continuously monitor short-term pickup and adjust pricing and promotional strategies accordingly.
  2. Leverage NYC’s Unique Market Strengths: Capitalize on consistently high occupancy and the pricing power afforded by the short-term rental ban. For NYC hotels, particularly in the luxury tier, this means confidently managing rates while ensuring service delivery matches premium price points. The unusual group vs. transient ADR dynamic requires careful analysis to optimize revenue across all segments.
  3. Strategize Around International & Domestic Demand Shifts: Acknowledge the current softness in some international inbound markets, particularly from Canada and parts of Europe, as indicated by airline adjustments. Focus on strong domestic segments and resilient premium international travelers. Monitor airline premium cabin performance as an indicator of high-value demand.
  4. Address the Group Segment Deliberately: For NYC, recognize the existing gap in group demand compared to pre-pandemic levels. Proactively target group business, understanding the influence of the city’s convention calendar. Investigate the anomaly of higher group ADR to ensure optimal pricing across transient and group bookings.
  5. Embrace and Prepare for Generative AI: Understand that customers are increasingly using GenAI for travel planning. Commercial teams must consider how their properties are represented and discovered via these new platforms. While widespread operational AI tools for hotels may still be evolving, the impact on customer search and booking behavior is current and accelerating. Begin strategizing for AI’s role in marketing, guest communication, and future revenue management.
