How AI Is Quietly Reshaping Travel Marketing and What Hotels Must Do Next

Michael J. Goldrich · July 22, 2025

Vivander Advisors helps businesses understand how AI describes and recommends them. This guide connects AI discoverability with clear business facts, buyer questions and evidence you can inspect.

At Phocuswright’s recent New Age(nts) Trend Series (video link at the bottom of this post), the future of travel marketing was declared. Panelists representing research leaders, digital marketing strategists, and hotel industry veterans laid out the blunt reality: AI is dismantling the foundations of how hotels have historically driven visibility, engagement, and bookings.

Search as we know it is fading. Google’s AI Overviews, answer engines, and conversational agents are replacing traditional discovery. Hotel websites, which were once the bedrock of direct bookings, are becoming secondary to structured data pipelines and machine-to-machine communication.

Panelists included:

  • Mike Coletta, Senior Manager of Research and Innovation, Phocuswright
  • John Lyotier, CEO, TravelAI
  • Brennen Bliss, CEO, Propellic
  • Michael Goldrich, Founder of Vivander Advisors and President of HSMAI NYC

What followed was a warning shot for every hotelier and travel marketer who assumes that optimizing for Google’s blue links will still matter in 2026.

AI Is Not the Future of Search. It’s the Replacement.

Search engines were once the starting point for travel planning. Coletta opened the panel by naming the “elephant in the room”: Google’s search monopoly isn’t what it used to be. Guests now ask AI assistants like Google’s AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity to decide where they should go, stay, and book.

These assistants do not provide ranked lists. They provide single answers. If your hotel isn’t in the answer, your website doesn’t matter.

Answer engines have replaced search engines. That shift is not pending. It is already underway.

AI Overviews Are Quietly Rewriting Visibility

Google’s AI Overviews now control the top of the search experience. Hotel listings and organic content are buried. Guests scanning AI summaries do not see your website, your blog, or your paid ads.

Hotels that optimize for traditional SEO are preparing for a game that is no longer being played. Visibility today depends on being structured, machine-readable, and ready for AI-driven presentation.

Marketers: Stop Thinking Like Campaign Builders. You’re Now Agent Managers.

Goldrich introduced a critical reframe. In this AI-first environment, marketing roles change fundamentally. Hotels will no longer rely on human-led teams to manually craft social posts, emails, or campaigns. AI agents will handle execution.

The hotel marketer becomes an “agent boss.” This role is not about production. It is about orchestration:

  • Directing AI models to generate content.
  • Managing automated workflows.
  • Overseeing output and outcomes across digital touchpoints.

The role shifts from doing to directing. From creating to coordinating.

Personalization Won’t Be Handcrafted Anymore

Lyotier and Bliss reinforced this shift. AI systems can generate ads, offers, and dynamic content in real-time, personalized to the individual traveler. Not a segment. Not a persona. A person.

Hotels that resist automation will lose not because their creative is inferior, but because their response time is too slow to matter.

The Direct Channel Is at Risk

Perhaps the most concerning shift is how AI intermediaries threaten direct bookings.

Historically, hotels worked hard to own their direct channel by optimizing websites, running paid media, and capturing bookings via controlled touchpoints. Now, AI assistants filter the guest’s decision-making before they ever reach your website.

If your hotel isn’t integrated into AI ecosystems, your direct channel will shrink. Visibility happens inside the assistant. Discovery and booking may happen without your website ever being seen.

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is now the frontline for protecting direct business.

Metrics Need to Change Immediately

Marketers who track clicks, impressions, and conversions via the website are missing the point. In this new environment, success looks different:

  • How often does your property appear in AI-generated answers?
  • How many recommendations or mentions are delivered by assistants?
  • What share of agent-mediated interactions convert to bookings?

If your team can’t measure AI-driven visibility, you are flying blind.

Digital Identity Is Survival.

What AI knows about your hotel depends entirely on your structured digital presence:

  • Room descriptions.
  • Amenities.
  • Images.
  • Rates.
  • Location data.

If your data isn’t structured and accessible to AI systems, you are invisible. Digital identity management shifts from a branding task to a technical imperative.

Hotels must manage their digital presence as a product, not as a website. Structured data, APIs, and metadata are now your most important marketing assets.

Machine-to-Machine Communication Will Define the Next Wave

Beyond guest interactions, AI agents will soon communicate with each other to manage inventory, optimize rates, and exchange data across platforms.

Hotels must prepare for machine-to-machine (M2M) interactions where AI systems trade your hotel’s data on your behalf. Without structured data pipelines and integrations, you’ll be locked out of these exchanges.

The Divide Between Leaders and Laggards Is Forming Now

Bliss warned of a stark, immediate divide. Organizations embracing continuous learning, experimentation, and AI-first thinking will win. Those waiting for certainty, best practices, or guidance from vendors will fall behind quietly and permanently.

AI rewards curiosity and speed. Hotels waiting for confidence will become invisible.

Your Guest Has Moved On. Are You Still Optimizing for 2019?

Travel marketing’s fundamentals are being rewritten. Guests ask AI assistants for recommendations. Answer engines, not search engines, mediate discovery. Your hotel website is no longer the starting point. In many cases, it is not part of the journey at all.

The Phocuswright panel made this clear. AI didn’t evolve search. It replaced it.

Hotels that adapt can dominate this new landscape. Those that wait will simply disappear from the conversation and from the booking path.

10 Actionable Takeaways for Hotels and Travel Marketers:

  1. Guests no longer search. They ask AI.
  2. Being named in AI-generated answers matters more than ranking in Google.
  3. Google’s AI Overviews are displacing traditional organic content.
  4. Protecting direct bookings requires integrating into AI ecosystems.
  5. Your marketing team should manage AI agents, not run manual campaigns.
  6. Personalization is real-time, automated, and unavoidable.
  7. Click-based metrics are obsolete. Track AI-driven visibility instead.
  8. Your hotel’s digital identity must be structured for machines.
  9. Machine-to-machine communication will drive future visibility.
  10. Experimentation and AI literacy are now non-negotiable survival strategies.

This isn’t about future-proofing. It’s about present-proofing. Hotels not addressing this shift today will be invisible tomorrow.
