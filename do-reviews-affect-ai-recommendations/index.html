Do reviews change whether AI recommends my business?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

They can, when the platform can reach them, and the evidence is thinner than the review industry would like you to believe. Review text is exactly the kind of specific fact assistants prefer: who a place is for, what it is like at night, whether the family suite is actually quiet. Assistants read reviews from the sources they can retrieve, which is not the same list as the sources you spend your time on. Yelp blocks most AI crawlers and is cited through a licensing deal. Google Maps reviews feed Google's surfaces and ChatGPT both. In every Vivander Advisors audit I sweep every review surface for the client and five named competitors, record what I find, and keep it out of the visibility score.

Eight hundred reviews on the wrong surface is eight hundred reviews the engine never read.

The steakhouse with 815 reviews and no recommendations

A Boston steakhouse. 815 Google reviews. 4.5 stars. Recommended in none of its occasion prompts.

Its competitor had a third as many reviews and was recommended in most, cited from OpenTable and a local editorial list. Review count was not the variable. Where the reviews lived, and what else sat on the cited page, was.

Which review sites can AI actually read?

The ones that let crawlers in, and the ones that signed contracts.

Yelp blocks GPTBot, ClaudeBot and CCBot, blocks its business pages outright, and is cited by ChatGPT anyway because Yelp licensed its content to OpenAI. Amazon blocks everything. TripAdvisor and OpenTable pages are retrievable and show up in my hotel and restaurant citation data. G2 and Capterra let AI crawlers in, which is why G2 is the only review platform in ChatGPT's top ten cited domains. Google Maps is retrievable, and ChatGPT cites it at roughly 188 citations per 1,000 best-of queries per Muck Rack's May 2026 data.

On a review platform the lever is access, not optimization. Time spent on a surface the assistant cannot read is time spent on a surface the assistant cannot read. I keep saying that sentence because owners keep not believing it.

Does review sentiment change the answer?

Probably. Nobody has measured it in the wild.

The best study placed synthetic review attributes directly into the prompt and found the model's recommendations followed them. That proves review data dominates when it reaches the context window. It proves nothing about whether any engine retrieves your reviews live for a given question. I record sentiment as an input to source authority and brand sentiment, and I do not claim a causal link I cannot show.

Does volume or recency matter?

Recency, for the same reason old timestamps gate pages: assistants prefer current material. Volume relative to competitors, because the assistant is choosing between you and them, not grading you alone.

My competitive review benchmark reads volume, rating, recency of the newest review and owner response rate on Google, Yelp, TripAdvisor and OpenTable for the client and five named competitors. Every figure read off the live surface and dated. Recorded, never scored. A score would reward suppressing bad reviews instead of fixing the service.

Do owner responses matter?

For the guest, yes. To the assistant, unmeasured.

Here is why I recommend them anyway. A response that states a current fact, "the rooftop reopened in May 2026," is a dated, specific, corroborating statement on a surface the platform reads. That is the exact shape of every correction that works. Not for a ranking effect nobody has demonstrated. For the record.

Does Google's Ask Maps use reviews?

Yes. Ask Maps replaced the public Q&A on Business Profiles from December 2025 and synthesizes its answer in the moment from attributes, services, photos, reviews and the linked website. A review that says there is no parking and a website that says there is produce a credibility conflict Google resolves against you.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Find the two review surfaces your platforms actually cite for your category. Put your effort there. For most hotels that is Google and an OTA. Most restaurants find it is Google and OpenTable or TripAdvisor.

For your role. Benchmark against the five competitors AI recommends instead of you, not the five you think of at the revenue meeting. They are rarely the same list.

For your guests. They will read the assistant's summary of your reviews, not the reviews. The summary was built from whichever surface the engine could reach.

What I do not know

Whether review sentiment reaches the context window live for a given prompt on a given platform. The mechanism is demonstrated only with reviews injected into the prompt. I say so in every report that touches reviews.

Where this is going

AI systems are already reading twenty thousand reviews to extract who a hotel is actually for. Google's travel lead said as much at Skift Megatrends: the engine is learning to interpret images, scan reviews and infer emotional context. Your guest called the property "quiet" four hundred times. The engine noticed before your marketing team did.

Reviews were reputation. They are becoming inventory data, and the engine is the one taking inventory.
