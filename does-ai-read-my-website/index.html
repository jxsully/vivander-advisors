Does AI read my website at all?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Possibly not, and nothing will tell you. Three things stop AI systems from reading a business website. A firewall or CDN rule that blocks their crawlers. Or a robots.txt that disallows them. And pages whose facts exist only after JavaScript runs. Any one of them makes you invisible to the retrieval step every AI answer starts with, and none of them throws an error. URL accessibility is the single best-evidenced factor in AI visibility, graded 9.5 on a 30-source analysis, ahead of anything about content. It is the first check I run in every Vivander Advisors audit, because a page nothing retrieves drives no content recommendation.

Nothing was broken. Nobody looked. That is the whole story of most invisible websites.

Eighteen months behind one toggle

A hotel's host enabled a setting called Block AI Bots in 2024. Merely a security default. Sensible-sounding.

The property held its rankings. Its site loaded fine. It was invisible on ChatGPT and Perplexity for eighteen months while its marketing team optimized pages no assistant could read.

One toggle. The 30-day re-test after it was switched off showed the hotel cited on both platforms for the first time.

Which crawlers matter?

More than most people think, and they come in categories that mean different things.

OpenAI runs GPTBot for training, OAI-SearchBot for its search index, and ChatGPT-User for live requests. Anthropic documents exactly three: ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User. Perplexity runs PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User. Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode ride Googlebot's normal index, and Google added Google-Agent in March 2026 for agents that navigate and act. Bingbot fronts Copilot. Applebot fronts Siri and Apple Intelligence. Meta-WebIndexer gates citation in Meta AI.

Two corrections to what circulates. Google-Extended is not a crawler. It is a robots.txt token that opts out of Gemini training, and blocking it does nothing to Search or AI Overviews. And xAI, ByteDance and DeepSeek publish no crawler documentation at all, so every token you see for them is unverifiable.

Can a CDN block AI without you knowing?

Yes. And since 15 September 2026 it can block Google too.

Cloudflare's own announcement says it plainly: from that date, multi-purpose crawlers such as Googlebot, Applebot and Bingbot will be blocked for customers who selected Block Training, including through the legacy Block AI Bots service. Enable that toggle once, ever, and you start blocking Google on that date unless you opt out. No error, no warning. Out of Google, Bing, Copilot, AI Overviews and Apple Intelligence in one move.

The default itself is narrow. New Cloudflare domains, training and agent categories, ad-bearing pages. Existing customers are unaffected unless they opted in. This is not a blanket block. It is an old training decision quietly becoming a search decision.

Ten minutes in the Cloudflare console. The highest-severity item on any audit I run.

Do AI crawlers read JavaScript?

Some do. Most do not.

Googlebot, Applebot and Bingbot execute JavaScript, and that set feeds AI Overviews, AI Mode, Siri and Copilot. GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and their user-triggered siblings do not render. They read the initial HTML response and stop. The measurement behind that comes from Vercel and MERJ in December 2024, and no vendor except Apple has confirmed or denied it since.

The practical rule does not need the debate settled. Anything that matters goes in the initial HTML. The rate, the room count, the menu, the hours, the policies, the schema block. JSON-LD injected by a tag manager is invisible to every non-rendering crawler. A rate that appears when a widget loads does not exist.

Does blocking training crawlers erase me?

No. Blocking retrieval crawlers does.

cloro studied 1,058 domains in July 2026. The median site blocking GPTBot earned 0.003 ChatGPT citations per Google ranking. Sites allowing it: 0.417. Those blocking PerplexityBot earned a median of zero on Perplexity.

OpenAI's split between GPTBot and OAI-SearchBot is the cleanest available. Say no to training and yes to being found. Publishers already draw that line: 13.9 percent block GPTBot, 3.4 percent block OAI-SearchBot.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Open the Cloudflare console before you read another word about content. Then fetch your rooms page with JavaScript off and read what comes back.

For your role. This check sits between IT and marketing, and in most hotel companies that gap has no owner. It costs ten minutes and it has been the entire problem more than once.

For your guests. They asked. The assistant went to your door. It was locked, and it walked to the next one without telling anybody.

What I do not know

Whether the no-JavaScript finding still holds for every crawler in 2026. The measurement is nineteen months old and the vendors have not updated it. I test the initial HTML directly rather than trust either side.

Where this is going

Agents are arriving with less patience than crawlers. A crawler that cannot read your page moves on. An agent that cannot read your page books somewhere else and confirms it to the guest. The access layer used to be plumbing. It is becoming the front desk.

The site was fine. Its door was shut. Nobody had checked the door.
