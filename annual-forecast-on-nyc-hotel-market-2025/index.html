Annual Forecast on NYC Hotel Market 2025

Michael J. Goldrich · November 22, 2024

Insights from Sean Hennessey – NYU Professor

At the recent HSMAI NYC Chapter meeting held at the Yard House in Times Square, Sean Hennessey delivered his highly anticipated Annual Forecast on the New York City hotel market for 2025. Drawing from global and local economic trends, Hennessey painted a complex picture of the travel and hospitality landscape, emphasizing both the resilience of the industry and the challenges it faces. Here’s an in-depth look at the key takeaways from his presentation.

The Global Perspective: Uneven Recovery and Stabilizing Growth

Hennessey began his presentation by discussing global travel trends, where tourism arrivals have nearly reached pre-pandemic levels, standing at 97–98% of 2019 figures. Yet, recovery has been uneven across regions. While markets such as Greece have soared beyond 2019 levels, with Athens reporting a remarkable 60% growth in RevPAR, China’s recovery remains sluggish. This disparity underscores the regional complexities within the global travel industry.

He also noted that the surge in post-pandemic growth is giving way to more normalized patterns. The initial bounce-back, driven by pent-up demand, has waned, and many markets are now returning to traditional growth trajectories. This shift signals a stabilization that industry stakeholders should embrace while preparing for sustained, incremental development.

U.S. Travel Trends: Economic Shifts and Consumer Behavior

Turning to the U.S., Hennessey highlighted how economic factors are shaping the travel landscape. Inflation has cooled from 8% in 2022 to a projected 3% for 2024, contributing to greater consumer confidence. However, the strong U.S. dollar has created a mixed dynamic: outbound travel by Americans has surpassed pre-pandemic levels, with 72 million citizens traveling abroad in 2023 compared to 60 million in 2019. Conversely, inbound international travel to the U.S. remains below 2019 levels, with numbers dropping from 65 million to 59 million. This trend reflects the impact of a strong dollar making U.S. travel costlier for international visitors.

NYC Hotel Market: A Bright Yet Complex Picture

Hennessey devoted much of his forecast to the New York City hotel market, where the industry has made significant strides. Visitor numbers are forecasted to reach 65 million in 2024, representing 97% of pre-pandemic levels. However, the city’s recovery has been shaped by unique challenges, including economic disruptions and regulatory constraints.

  • Hotel Supply Constraints: Since 2019, stricter regulations have made hotel development in NYC more challenging. With only 52 new hotels and 8,000 rooms under construction, the pipeline for new properties remains limited. This has led to a supply slowdown, contrasting sharply with the pre-pandemic era when NYC led the nation in hotel construction.
  • Luxury Hotels and New Entrants: The market is seeing an influx of luxury properties, including the Four Seasons and the long-delayed Waldorf Astoria reopening. These developments are poised to enhance New York’s premium hospitality segment, catering to high-spending international travelers and affluent domestic guests.
  • Asylum Seeker Impact: One unexpected challenge has been the housing of asylum seekers, which currently accounts for 11–12% of hotel rooms. This dynamic imposes financial strain on the city, with projections estimating $5–6 billion in associated costs over the next fiscal year.

Economic Factors Shaping NYC’s Tourism Future

Hennessey also touched on broader economic trends impacting the NYC hotel market:

  • Inflation and ADR Growth: Average Daily Rates (ADR) in NYC have rebounded strongly, but inflationary pressures may temper future rate increases. Nonetheless, the city’s reputation as a high-value market ensures continued demand for its premium offerings.
  • Office Market Evolution: NYC’s return-to-office rates exceed national averages, signaling positive momentum for business travel. However, the decline of economically viable Class B and C office buildings presents long-term questions about urban real estate.
  • Tourism Infrastructure: Broadway’s reopening and packed airports highlight the resilience of NYC’s tourism infrastructure. International travel recovery, while slower, offers upside potential as these visitors typically spend more and stay longer.

Opportunities and Challenges Ahead

As Hennessey noted, the NYC hotel market operates within a unique set of constraints and opportunities. Supply limitations, coupled with robust demand, present a favorable environment for pricing power. However, the city must navigate headwinds like regulatory challenges, economic uncertainties, and geopolitical risks.

Emerging opportunities include the potential for casino developments and the continued rise of experiential luxury travel, with NYC positioned to attract high-value travelers seeking bespoke experiences. Conversely, the decline in short-term rental options post-regulation resets the competitive landscape, providing hotels with an edge in reclaiming market share.

A Steady Path Forward

Hennessey’s forecast struck a tone of cautious optimism. While the NYC hotel market faces structural and economic challenges, it remains a resilient and adaptive force. Stakeholders can expect steady growth driven by sustained demand, enhanced by the city’s unparalleled appeal and robust infrastructure.

For NYC hoteliers and industry professionals, the road ahead requires balancing strategic investments with agility in navigating external pressures. As Hennessey aptly summarized, “New York City’s hospitality market remains one of the most dynamic and resilient in the world. While we face headwinds, the opportunities for growth and innovation have never been greater.”
