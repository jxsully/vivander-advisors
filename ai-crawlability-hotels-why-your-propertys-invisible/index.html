AI Crawlability Hotels: A Reason Why Your Property May Be Invisible

Michael J. Goldrich · January 23, 2026

Description: Hotels blocking AI crawlers are becoming invisible to the next generation of travelers. Learn why AI crawlability determines direct booking success and how hospitality leaders can reclaim visibility in the age of agentic search.

The way travelers discover hotels is undergoing its most significant transformation since the rise of search engines. A recent Bain study found that 80% of consumers rely on zero-click results at least 40% of the time. This means potential guests are finding their answers within AI-generated summaries rather than clicking through to hotel websites.

For hospitality leaders, this shift demands attention. Properties that remain invisible to AI systems will struggle to capture direct bookings in the emerging discovery landscape.

This article examines what is changing, why it matters, and how hospitality organizations can position themselves for the visibility economy that AI is creating.

The Structural Shift in Travel Discovery

Traditional search engine optimization focused on helping Google’s crawlers index hotel websites for search results. That model is evolving. Large language models now power discovery tools like ChatGPT, Google’s Gemini, and Perplexity. These systems crawl websites differently, process information differently, and serve results to users in entirely new formats.

As technology advisor Brennen Bliss of Propellic has noted, LLMs are changing how the web is indexed, processed, and served to users. His recommendation: serve a crawler-friendly version of your site.

AI crawlers follow links, read publicly accessible content, store information for analysis, and use what they find to train models and generate responses. This differs fundamentally from traditional search indexing. When someone asks ChatGPT about a hotel, the model draws from content it has been able to access and process.

The problem for many hotel groups is straightforward. According to hospitality consultant Ira Vouk of Hospitality 2.0 Consulting, analysis of major hotel chain websites reveals that large groups are blocking most AI bots in their robots.txt files. This makes these properties practically invisible on major AI platforms.

Online travel agencies have recognized this opportunity. Expedia and Booking.com have opened their doors wider to AI crawlers. When a potential guest asks an AI assistant about accommodations, these intermediaries often surface before brand.com properties.

Pedro Colaco, president and CEO of GuestCentric, summarized the situation clearly: hotels are really showing up in the conversation. That is very bizarre, that they’re leaving this to third parties.

Why Visibility Now Equals Competitive Power

The robots.txt file on any website tells AI crawlers what they can and cannot access. View any hotel’s file by adding robots.txt to the end of their domain. What you find reveals their strategic posture toward AI-driven discovery.

Choice Hotels has taken a different approach. An international spokesperson confirmed the company is working with its content distribution network provider to allow LLM agents to search its sites. Choice Hotels also works directly with LLM providers to ensure visibility and searchability across AI platforms.

The contrast matters because visibility compounds. Properties that AI systems can access and understand become part of the training data and response generation for millions of user queries. Properties that block access become invisible by default.

This creates a strategic asymmetry. Third-party intermediaries capturing AI traffic can route bookings through their own platforms, adding margin extraction to every transaction. Hotels that reclaim visibility through direct AI accessibility reduce intermediary dependence.

The dynamics mirror what happened with traditional search engines. Early adopters of search optimization captured organic traffic. Late adopters paid more for the same visibility through advertising. AI discoverability follows similar patterns, but the window for establishing position may close faster.

Rethinking Content Strategy for Machine Readers

Opening up to AI crawlers represents only half the equation. Hotels must also consider what content they make available.

Traditional SEO emphasized frequently asked questions. AI discovery favors a different approach. Sanjay Vakil, CEO and co-founder of DirectBooker, suggests creating infrequently asked questions that only the hotel can answer.

Consider the types of queries AI assistants now field: Does the pet policy have a weight limit? What temperature is the Jacuzzi? Can I request a room facing the courtyard? These long-tail queries signal high intent. Someone asking about specific pet weight limits is likely traveling with a pet and actively considering a booking.

Vakil argues that long-tail queries should be recast as high-intent queries. Make that information easily available within an AI, and visitors will be more likely to book.

This represents a fundamental content philosophy shift. Traditional websites optimized for human scanning and search engine keywords. AI-accessible websites must optimize for machine comprehension and conversational response generation.

The opportunity extends beyond answering practical questions. GuestCentric’s Colaco advises hotels to return to storytelling basics. If hotels are telling their story well on their website, everybody that talks about them, whether it’s the media or user reviews, if they can surface what the experience is at the hotel, then they’ll show up.

He cited one client, Memmo Alfama in Lisbon, which saw decreased website visits but increased conversion and direct bookings. The strategy that worked? Going back to the original brand story about the neighborhood and its artisans. People want to know: what am I going to experience? Travel starts with the discovery piece.

Navigating Security and Compliance Realities

Large organizations have legitimate reasons to restrict AI crawler access. Hospitality technology expert Olivier Delaunoy, former CTO at Bloc Hotels, outlined several concerns:

Unauthorized price scraping allows competitors and automated systems to monitor pricing strategies in real-time.

Content copying enables AI systems to reproduce proprietary descriptions, photography captions, and marketing language.

Competitive intelligence gathering becomes automated when crawlers can access detailed property information systematically.

Delaunoy noted that robots.txt files represent only the visible surface of bot management. Large hotel brands typically run multiple layers of protection behind the scenes. A robots.txt file may look simple, but operational security drives the rest.

The challenge grows more complex as AI systems become more sophisticated. Reports indicate that ChatGPT can now pass “I am robot” verification tests. Amazon recently sued Perplexity over concerns about its agentic shopping tool disguising automated activity as human browsing.

Delaunoy recommends a nuanced approach: tailor the hotel bot strategy to block harmful scrapers and allow beneficial AI crawlers and engage with friendly and approved data-sharing programs, where the hotelier can control accuracy, pricing, and brand image.

Additional complications exist. Internet security provider Cloudflare recently defaulted to blocking AI crawlers for new domains. Felix Shpilman, CEO of Emerging Travel Group, noted that many hoteliers are likely unaware of this default setting. Properties may be invisible to AI systems without any intentional decision to block access.

The Organizational Readiness Gap

Technical decisions about crawler access reflect deeper organizational questions about AI strategy.

Hotels blocking AI crawlers often do so through default settings established years ago, before anyone understood the implications. IT departments made reasonable security decisions. Marketing departments were never consulted. Revenue management continued focusing on traditional distribution channels.

This organizational fragmentation mirrors patterns seen across enterprise AI adoption. Technology decisions happen in silos. Strategic implications span the entire organization. Coordination mechanisms lag behind the pace of change.

The hospitality companies that will thrive in AI-driven discovery are those that align technical infrastructure with commercial strategy. This requires:

Cross-functional awareness of how technical decisions affect marketing visibility

Clear ownership of AI-related technology decisions that span traditional departmental boundaries

Ongoing monitoring of emerging AI platforms and their content access patterns

Strategic frameworks for evaluating which AI systems deserve access and under what conditions

What This Means for Hospitality Leadership

The shift toward AI-driven discovery is happening whether individual properties participate or not. Leadership teams face strategic choices with lasting implications.

Assess current visibility. Check your robots.txt file. Audit your technical infrastructure for default settings that may block beneficial AI access. Understand what AI assistants currently say about your properties when users ask.

Evaluate content readiness. Does your website contain the detailed, specific information that AI systems need to answer high-intent queries? Can machines understand your brand story, or only parse your keyword density?

Align organizational capabilities. Bring technical and commercial teams into conversation about AI discovery strategy. Establish clear ownership for decisions that span traditional boundaries.

Monitor the evolving landscape. New AI discovery platforms emerge regularly. Each has different crawling behaviors, different content preferences, and different implications for visibility. Static strategies become obsolete quickly.

The travel industry is entering a period that GuestCentric’s Colaco compared to the early days of SEO, when people were writing keywords in transparent fonts at the end of pages. Current tactics will evolve. The fundamental importance of AI visibility will only increase.

Barry Diller, chairman of IAC and Expedia Group, captured the broader trajectory at The Phocuswright Conference: now that AI has a chance, that search monopoly experience of Google is going to change. The implications for hospitality visibility are profound.

The Path Forward

AI crawlability is becoming table stakes for hospitality visibility. Properties that embrace this reality can capture direct booking opportunities that others forfeit to intermediaries.

The technical mechanics of crawler access are relatively straightforward to address. The harder work involves building organizational capabilities to navigate ongoing AI evolution. This includes developing literacy about how AI systems work, understanding what visibility means in conversational discovery contexts, and designing leadership structures that can coordinate responses across traditional functional silos.

Hotels have always competed on guest experience. The next frontier extends that competition into the discovery experience itself. Properties that AI systems can access, understand, and recommend will capture attention. Properties that remain invisible will lose ground steadily to intermediaries willing to play the visibility game.

The structural shift is underway. The question for hospitality leaders is whether their organizations will shape that shift or be shaped by it.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is AI crawlability and why does it matter for hotels?

Short Answer: AI crawlability refers to whether AI systems can access and process your website content to include your property in AI-generated responses.

Elaboration: When travelers ask AI assistants like ChatGPT about hotels, those systems can only recommend properties whose information they have been able to crawl and understand. Hotels blocking AI crawlers through robots.txt files or other technical settings become invisible in these conversations. With 80% of consumers relying on zero-click AI results at least 40% of the time, invisibility in AI discovery translates directly to lost booking opportunities.

2. How can I check if my hotel is blocking AI crawlers?

Short Answer: Add /robots.txt to the end of your website domain to see what your site tells AI crawlers they can access.

Elaboration: The robots.txt file is publicly accessible on every website. For example, visiting yourdomain.com/robots.txt reveals instructions your site gives to various crawlers. Look for entries related to common AI bots like GPTBot, Claude, or Anthropic. If these are listed under “Disallow” rules, your property may be invisible to those AI systems. Technical teams can modify these settings, though security considerations should factor into any changes.

3. Are online travel agencies better positioned for AI discovery than hotels?

Short Answer: Currently, yes. OTAs like Expedia and Booking.com have been more receptive to AI crawlers.

Elaboration: Analysis of major hotel chain websites shows that many brand.com properties block AI bots more aggressively than OTA platforms. When AI assistants answer traveler questions, they often pull information from whichever source they can access. If OTAs are accessible and brand.com properties are blocked, the OTA becomes the default recommendation source. This perpetuates intermediary dependence and margin extraction that direct booking strategies aim to reduce.

4. What are the security risks of opening up to AI crawlers?

Short Answer: Legitimate concerns include price scraping, content copying, and competitive intelligence gathering.

Elaboration: AI crawlers can systematically collect pricing data, allowing competitors to monitor rate strategies in real-time. They can also copy proprietary content and gather detailed competitive intelligence. Large hotel brands typically run multiple layers of bot management beyond what robots.txt files reveal. The recommended approach involves blocking harmful scrapers while allowing beneficial AI crawlers through approved data-sharing programs that let hoteliers control accuracy and brand presentation.

5. What type of content should hotels prioritize for AI discovery?

Short Answer: Detailed, specific information that answers high-intent questions only your property can address.

Elaboration: Traditional FAQs covered common questions that many websites answer. AI discovery rewards unique content. Consider infrequently asked questions like pet policy weight limits, pool temperatures, room view options, and neighborhood experiences. These long-tail queries signal booking intent. Someone asking about specific pet requirements is likely traveling with a pet and actively considering a reservation. Make this information easily accessible in formats AI systems can parse.

6. How does AI discovery differ from traditional SEO?

Short Answer: Traditional SEO optimized for search engine keywords and human scanning. AI discovery requires machine comprehension and conversational response generation.

Elaboration: Google’s search index helped users find websites to visit. AI assistants generate complete answers that may never require clicking through to a website. This zero-click environment means content must be structured for AI systems to extract, understand, and present in conversational format. Hotels need both traditional SEO for search visibility and AI optimization for emerging discovery channels.

7. What should hotels do about Cloudflare’s default AI blocking?

Short Answer: Check your Cloudflare settings and consider adjusting defaults to allow beneficial AI crawlers.

Elaboration: Cloudflare recently began blocking AI crawlers by default for new domains. Many properties may be invisible to AI systems without any intentional decision to block access. IT teams should audit current settings across all security and CDN providers. Decisions about AI crawler access should involve both technical and commercial stakeholders to ensure security measures align with visibility strategy.

8. How can hotels measure their AI visibility?

Short Answer: Test what AI assistants say about your property and monitor referral patterns from AI-driven sources.

Elaboration: Start by asking ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and other AI assistants about your property. Note what information they provide, what sources they cite, and whether they recommend your direct website or intermediaries. Track whether any website traffic comes from AI-related referrers. The industry is still developing standardized visibility metrics for AI discovery, but active monitoring reveals your current position.

9. Should all hotels open completely to AI crawlers?

Short Answer: No. The optimal approach involves selective access that balances visibility with legitimate security concerns.

Elaboration: Blanket opening to all crawlers exposes properties to malicious scraping, content theft, and competitive surveillance. The recommended approach involves identifying beneficial AI platforms and allowing them access while maintaining protection against harmful actors. This requires ongoing monitoring as AI systems evolve and new players enter the market. Detailed filtering is a skill, and each organization must determine appropriate boundaries.

10. How quickly is AI discovery changing the hospitality landscape?

Short Answer: Rapidly enough that waiting to act creates competitive disadvantage, but slowly enough that thoughtful strategy matters more than panic.

Elaboration: The comparison to early SEO is instructive. Organizations that recognized search engine optimization early captured organic visibility advantages that took years for competitors to match. AI discovery is following similar patterns but potentially at faster speed. Properties establishing AI visibility now build advantages that compound over time. Those delaying decisions may find themselves paying more for the same visibility later through intermediaries or advertising.
