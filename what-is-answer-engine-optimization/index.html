What is answer engine optimization (AEO)?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Answer engine optimization is the work of structuring a page so an AI assistant or answer engine can lift a fragment of it as the direct answer to a question. SEO asks whether a page can be found and ranked. AEO asks whether the page answers a question in a form a machine can quote: a heading that asks it, an answer that opens the section, facts stated in extractable units, nothing load-bearing hidden behind a click. At Vivander Advisors I score AEO as the content architecture layer of the AI Visibility Audit, and the largest single component of that score is whether the buyer's questions appear as headings anywhere on the site.

Traditional SEO earns the crawl. AEO earns the quote. I said that in 2025 and it is the shortest true sentence I know about this field.

Why AEO exists

Answer engines do not rank pages. They dismantle them. Microsoft describes assistants breaking content into smaller structured pieces evaluated for authority and relevance. Google's grounding output, read across 883,262 snippets, shows extraction at roughly fifteen-word chunks with a heavy lead bias and a plateau near 540 words.

A page can rank first and never be cited because its answer sits in paragraph six. That is the common case. It is why "we rank well" is no answer to "why are we not in the AI response."

What does AEO involve?

Six structural choices. A descriptive heading hierarchy, one idea per heading. Question-and-answer pairing, which Microsoft says assistants can often lift word for word. Lists and tables that extract whole. Answers front-loaded, context after. Sentences that stand alone without the paragraph around them. And visible HTML, with prices, specifications and policies in the initial response rather than in tabs, PDFs or widgets.

Answer-first ordering is one of only two levers a 45-study review grades "strong." Hedged language and a missing price were unanimous gates across six models in 252,000 trials. And there is no word count for the opening answer. The "40 to 60 words" figure came from featured-snippet research, a search feature, and no sourced word band exists for AI citation.

How does AEO differ from SEO and GEO?

SEO is findability: crawlability, indexation, speed, links, rank. AEO is extractability: does the page answer the question in a liftable form. GEO, generative engine optimization, is selection: is the brand chosen when the answer is assembled from many sources, which depends on corroboration and entity clarity off the site.

A business can pass SEO and fail AEO completely. Most do.

How does Vivander measure AEO?

Seven sub-modules, one score from 0 to 10, reported beside the visibility score and never folded into it.

Heading inventory: does the template have enough addressable units. Heading-to-question mapping: of the buyer questions captured, how many does a heading anywhere on the site ask, weighted at 40 percent. Answer-first structure. Sentence self-containment. Entity declaration on the lead page.

And two that read in the other direction. What question does each page think it answers, read blind to the prompt list. And where do the two sets fail to meet. That reverse read is what surfaces drift, a page answering a question nobody asks in internal vocabulary, and collision, three pages claiming the same question. A coverage percentage never finds either.

What are the cheapest AEO fixes?

Publish facts already on the site under question-shaped headings. Reorder partial answers so the answer precedes the context. Un-hide anything load-bearing. Rewrite drift pages in the customer's vocabulary. Resolve collisions. Only then commission new content.

The first three are an editor's work. They are also the ones a prompt-first audit never surfaces cleanly, which is why I run the page-first read too.

What AEO does not include

FAQ schema as a lever. Google dropped FAQ rich results in May 2026, and the visible text is what gets extracted. Word-count targets. Any technique that hides a fact in markup.

Where this is going

Content teams now write for machines and humans simultaneously. That is a new craft, and the people who have it mostly learned it this year, on the job, by reading what the engines lifted and what they skipped.

Structure signals trust. Flowery copy, buried text and hidden tabs get skipped. Crisp, consensus-backed facts get lifted. The engine is a very literal reader, and it is reading more of your site than any human ever did.
