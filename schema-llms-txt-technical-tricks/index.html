Does schema markup, llms.txt or any technical trick get me recommended?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

No. Nothing you add to the markup layer has been shown to cause an AI citation, and the two most-sold tricks have been tested and failed. Ahrefs compared 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD schema against 4,000 matched controls in May 2026 and found no significant lift on ChatGPT or AI Mode, and a significant decrease on Google AI Overviews. Google states that llms.txt files are not needed for Search and have no effect on visibility. Schema is worth doing for rich results and entity grounding. The facts have to be in visible text first, because a fact that exists only in markup does not exist to a crawler reading the page. At Vivander Advisors I recommend markup for what it does and refuse to sell it for what it does not.

I told hotels in 2025 that rich schema was the weapon. At the time I was reading the correlation. Then the experiment ran.

The schema overhaul that described an empty page

A hotel paid an agency for a full schema overhaul and a llms.txt file. Perfect markup. Every attribute typed.

Its rate was still inside a booking widget. Six months later the property was cited from Booking.com in every prompt where it appeared. The markup was flawless and described a page with nothing on it.

What the schema evidence shows

Three independent methods agree, and together they outrank the correlation everyone quotes.

Causal. Ahrefs' difference-in-differences: 1,885 treated pages, 4,000 matched controls, four convergent tests. AI Mode plus 2.4 percent, not significant. ChatGPT plus 2.2 percent, not significant. Google AI Overviews minus 4.6 percent, significant. Ahrefs had earlier published the correlation that cited pages are about three times likelier to carry schema. Then they refuted its causal reading with their own experiment. Schema rides on sites that do everything else well.

Mechanism. searchVIU ran eight controlled tests across five AI systems in December 2025. A price placed only in JSON-LD was extracted by zero of five on direct fetch. Hidden microdata and RDFa ignored too. Only visible HTML was read.

Official. Google's documentation on AI features, updated December 2025: there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and no special structured data you need to add.

The scope limits are fair to state. Every page in the Ahrefs study already had over 100 AI Overview citations before treatment, so the null covers already-cited pages. searchVIU tested direct fetch; schema is likely still read at indexing time, which is the path into Google's and Copilot's surfaces. Ahrefs itself keeps that path open: good reasons remain for JSON-LD, for rich results, voice assistants, knowledge graphs and entity recognition. That is the justification I use. The only one.

Is FAQ schema worth adding?

Not as a visibility play. Google dropped FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026, removed the reporting in June, and removes the Search Console API support in August. FAQPage remains a valid type and Google says it still uses it to understand pages, so publishing it is harmless. Recommending it as a lever is not supportable.

The questions and answers in visible HTML are what gets extracted. Put them there.

Does llms.txt do anything?

Not for visibility, and Google settled it. The Search Central changelog of June 2026: the files are not needed for Google Search and will not positively or negatively affect visibility.

Ahrefs looked at 137,210 domains. 28 percent publish one. 97 percent of those received zero requests in May 2026. Among the 3 percent with any traffic, AI bots were 19.5 percent, behind SEO audit tools at 21.7 percent. Zero AI bot requests to files that did not exist. Nothing goes looking for it.

It has a second, narrower life as an agent-context surface. Chrome's Lighthouse audits it under Agentic Browsing, unscored, beside agent accessibility. If you expose developer tools or agents, publish one. A hotel or a restaurant should treat it as an optional experiment, never strategy. It cannot allow, forbid or compel anything. A client treating it as an access or ranking lever has misread it, and that misreading is itself a finding.

What about vector optimization, Web Bot Auth, WebMCP and the rest?

Vector-embedding optimization: never sold as a deliverable. No public case evidence, and machine-predicted relevance that user behavior does not confirm invites correction.

Web Bot Auth: production-active at Cloudflare and AWS, no ratified standard beyond the signature RFC, incompatible syntax between Cloudflare and Google. Worth knowing about. Not a visibility lever.

WebMCP: a community draft, off by default in Chrome. An early-mover signal for agent interaction, never a traffic driver.

Markdown content negotiation: some agents ask for it, cheap to serve on Cloudflare, thin adoption. A genuine early-mover signal at low cost.

What technical work does move visibility?

Access and delivery. Crawler allow rules. The CDN toggle. Facts in the initial HTML. Server-rendered schema for entity grounding, matched to the visible text. Native controls and labeled forms for agents. A visible booking action an agent can complete. URL accessibility is the top-graded factor in the field at 9.5. That is where the technical budget goes.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Price, specifications and policies in visible text before you touch markup. Then LodgingBusiness or Restaurant schema, server-rendered, matched word for word to the page. Google's one endorsed check is that the structured data matches the visible text.

For your role. Somebody sold you the markup because the correlation looked causal. It was an honest mistake. I made it too. The job now is to stop paying for it.

For your guests. They never see the markup. What they see is whether the assistant knew your rate. It did not, because the rate was in the code and not on the page.

What I do not know

Whether schema read at indexing time contributes to Google's and Copilot's AI surfaces in ways the direct-fetch tests cannot see. The null evidence covers already-cited pages and direct fetch. I recommend schema for grounding on that basis and claim nothing more.

Where this is going

The engines are converging on reading the visible page, the way a person would, and cross-referencing it against the structure underneath. That trick layer is getting thinner every quarter. What is left is the boring thing: say true, specific, current things where anyone can read them.

I moved with the evidence on schema, in public, because the alternative is defending advice the data retired. Ask any vendor when they last did that.
