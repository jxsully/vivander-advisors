Everybody Got Promoted: AI Supervision in Hotels

By Michael J. Goldrich · Founder, Vivander AdvisorsOriginal feature: October 29, 2026 · Summary published November 3, 2026A summary of Everybody Got Promoted, with the main argument and practical implications for hotel leaders.Read the full feature on Hospitality Upgrade →

What is this feature about?

AI supervision in hotels means giving employees the skills and authority to assess work produced by AI systems. In Everybody Got Promoted, I argue that hotels have already handed people this responsibility, often without changing their training, job descriptions or support. Installing the tool is only the beginning.

What changed when AI entered hotel work?

A revenue manager who once built a recommendation may now receive one overnight and decide whether to approve it. The work has moved from production to supervision. Yet experience doing a job does not automatically prepare someone to judge a machine’s version of it. The reviewer needs to know what information the system used, what it could not see and when its answer deserves a second look.The feature connects this shift to an older pattern: technology redistributes work before organizations update their roles. This time, the redistributed work includes delegation, quality control and decisions about what may reach a guest, a rate plan or a financial record.

Why can plausible AI output be harder to supervise?

Obvious mistakes are easy to challenge. A tidy report with an outdated assumption is harder. As systems improve, reviewers may become more confident even though important errors remain. A polished result does not show whether an input was stale, a contract term was missed or a local exception was ignored.Hotels therefore need evidence for approval. What was checked? Against which standard? Who can reject the result? A correction should leave a record the next reviewer can use.

What should an AI workflow include?

The article describes five connected elements: the event that starts the process, the work the system performs, the limits of its authority, the point where a person reviews it and the record of what happens when that person rejects or corrects it. Together, these make accountability visible.Group billing illustrates the point. Software can assemble folios, compare records and surface discrepancies. A coordinator can then spend time on exceptions, while recorded corrections become an operational memory. The value comes from redesigning the handoff, not simply putting an agent into the old process.

How do hotels train people for the new role?

Begin with the actual product: distinguish a responsive assistant, a defined workflow and a goal-directed agent. Test familiar cases, compare results with approved outcomes and repeat the exercise when the system changes. Set permissions gradually, as you would with a new employee.Then protect the learning pipeline. Routine work has long taught junior staff how the property operates. If automation removes that repetition, replace accidental learning with deliberate practice: review exceptions, explain overrides and pair newer employees with experienced operators.

What should hotels do with the time returned?

The feature’s opportunity is practical. Less assembly and reconciliation can leave more room for coaching, inspection and conversations with guests or planners. A group sales coordinator may get time back for the call that reveals what the meeting really needs.That outcome requires a management decision. Faster work alone does not decide whether the benefit becomes better service, stronger staff development or simply more work. The central question is whether people are being prepared for the responsibility the technology has already given them.

Practical takeaways for hotel leaders

  • Identify which employees now approve AI-generated work and what they are accountable for.
  • Define authority, review standards, escalation and a usable correction record.
  • Use real exceptions to train judgment and preserve the development of junior staff.
  • Decide how returned time will improve service, coaching or commercial work.

Questions about AI supervision in hotels

What is AI supervision in hotels? AI supervision is the work of assigning, reviewing, approving, correcting and escalating machine-generated outputs. It requires clear standards, appropriate permissions and a person accountable for the outcome.
Does AI training only mean learning prompts? No. The article emphasizes evaluating output, understanding a system’s limits, managing its authority and redesigning the workflow around it. Prompting is only one part of that responsibility.
