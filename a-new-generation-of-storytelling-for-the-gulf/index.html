A New Generation of Storytelling for the Gulf

Michael J. Goldrich · December 2, 2024

This speech by Ben Baseley-Walker​​​​, the Founder & Managing Partner of Andart Global at the HSMAI Middle East conference identifies the critical challenge of positioning emerging destinations in Saudi Arabia and the UAE as compelling, emotionally resonant experiences rather than just another collection of luxury hotels and amenities. The focus is on crafting narratives that elevate these destinations beyond bricks and mortar, forging deep connections with visitors and ensuring long-term loyalty and success.

Beyond Bricks and Mortar: The Evolution of Destinations

Destinations like NEOM, the Red Sea, and others across Saudi Arabia and the UAE represent a new frontier in travel. They are not simply about sun, sea, and sand, luxuries that many places offer. Instead, they aim to provide unique, transformative experiences. To achieve this, destinations must go beyond traditional property-focused strategies. The task is to craft narratives that speak directly to potential visitors’ imaginations and emotions.

A destination cannot rely on amenities alone, especially when the competition is fierce and offerings are easily replicable. Instead, the story of the destination must differentiate it. Visitors need to see the value in choosing one place over another. This becomes increasingly complex when promoting large-scale developments with multiple properties, attractions, and experiences, as seen in NEOM or the Red Sea project. It is here that storytelling becomes not just a marketing tool but a strategic necessity.

Core Values for Building Resonant Narratives

  1. Believability and Authenticity: Destinations must go beyond glossy marketing renders. Potential visitors need to see the destination as a credible and authentic choice, one that stands apart from generic alternatives. The narrative must convey a sense of place, rooted in the unique culture, history, and natural beauty of the region.
  2. Trust and Safety: For many international travelers, particularly those unfamiliar with the region, there may be lingering perceptions of geopolitical instability. Building trust requires consistency, transparency, and an emphasis on safety and hospitality. Destinations need to demonstrate that they are welcoming and reliable places to visit repeatedly.
  3. Relatability: Travelers must be able to see themselves within the story of the destination. Whether it’s hosting a milestone celebration, creating family memories, or simply escaping for a transformative experience, the narrative must feel personal and accessible. Avoiding extremes of ultra-luxury or overwhelming scale is key to making destinations feel inclusive.
  4. Character and Identity: Destinations need a strong, clear identity, much like a well-defined character in a story. Visitors should immediately understand what a destination represents, how it differentiates itself, and what unique experiences it offers. This clarity of message helps cut through the noise of competing markets.

Lessons from Other Industries

To understand how to build compelling destination narratives, we can look to industries like political campaigns, cinema, and even space exploration:

  • Political Campaigns: The success of a figure like Donald Trump demonstrates the power of a clear, strong narrative. Whether one agrees with his message or not, his ability to present a simple and resonant vision contrasts with more complex or fragmented messaging. Destinations must similarly convey clear, unifying messages that inspire confidence and engagement.
  • Film and Television: Much like New Zealand leveraged its role as Middle-earth in The Lord of the Rings, destinations can create aspirational, immersive worlds. People don’t just visit a place, they buy into a story that connects them emotionally to the destination.
  • Space Exploration: Selling investments in asteroid mining or gas stations in space requires translating futuristic visions into relatable, traditional concepts. Similarly, destinations must align their ambitious developments with narratives that feel tangible and understandable to investors and visitors alike.

Creating Loyalty and Fostering Engagement

The success of these grand destinations lies in making visitors feel like they are part of an evolving story. This approach builds loyalty and encourages repeat visits. For instance, Dubai has thrived by continually offering something new, ensuring that both residents and visitors feel invested in its ongoing development. Similarly, Saudi Arabia’s destinations must create narratives that entice visitors to return year after year, eager to see the next chapter of the story unfold.

Internal Alignment and External Clarity

Crafting a coherent narrative is not just about external messaging. Internally, stakeholders, whether they are government entities, hotel brands, or local communities, must align around the destination’s identity and purpose. For example, properties under the same umbrella, such as Hilton hotels across Saudi Arabia, must tell a unified story that complements the broader narrative of the destination.

Externally, the narrative must be crystal clear. Consumers need to quickly grasp what the destination offers and why it matters. Complexity or vagueness can confuse potential visitors, making them less likely to choose the destination over more established competitors.

From Strategy to Execution

As the region moves into a new phase of development, the focus will shift from visionary strategies to tangible outcomes. This transition requires destinations to have a strong foundation of narrative and identity in place. The groundwork laid today will enable destinations to optimize marketing, attract visitors, and generate sustainable growth as these projects come online.

The Challenge Ahead

The GCC region, particularly Saudi Arabia, has immense potential to become a global leader in tourism. However, the challenge lies in ensuring these destinations are more than just ambitious projects, they must become deeply resonant places where visitors feel a connection that compels them to return. By focusing on believability, trust, relatability, and character, and by learning from the successes and failures of other industries, these destinations can craft narratives that inspire, captivate, and ultimately succeed.

In a competitive global market, it is no longer enough to build stunning hotels and impressive infrastructure. The future of tourism lies in storytelling, the ability to turn destinations into stories that travelers want to be part of. Only then can these projects achieve their full potential and leave a lasting legacy in the world of travel and hospitality.
