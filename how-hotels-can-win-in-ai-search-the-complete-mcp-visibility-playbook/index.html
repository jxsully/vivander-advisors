How Hotels Can Win in AI Search: The Complete MCP Visibility Playbook

Michael J. Goldrich · December 26, 2025

Vivander Advisors helps businesses understand how AI describes and recommends them. This guide connects AI discoverability with clear business facts, buyer questions and evidence you can inspect.

Model Context Protocol is rewriting how travelers discover and book hotels. Here’s why it matters, what it means for your business, and the exact steps to ensure AI agents can find you.

Your website’s search engine ranking is becoming less important by the day.

Not declining gradually. Becoming structurally irrelevant to how travelers will find hotels.

The traveler of 2027 won’t search “hotels near Times Square” and scroll through sponsored results. They’ll tell an AI assistant: “Book me somewhere walkable to Broadway with a rooftop bar and late checkout.” That AI will query hotel systems directly, compare real-time rates, verify specific amenities, and complete the booking. No website visit. No Google search. No clicking through ten blue links.

The technology enabling this shift shipped in November 2024. It’s called Model Context Protocol, and it determines whether AI agents can see your inventory at all. According to Ulrich Pillau, CEO of property management platform Apaleo, the travel industry’s current readiness sits at “about five on a scale of one to ten.”

Half the industry is unprepared. The other half is positioning to capture direct bookings that online travel agencies have dominated for two decades.

This isn’t speculation about a distant future. Turkish Airlines already runs an MCP server giving AI models direct access to live booking data. Amadeus, Sabre, and RateGain have adopted the protocol. The infrastructure for AI-first travel distribution is being built right now.

The question isn’t whether AI will reshape hotel discovery. The question is whether your property will be part of the distribution that AI can see.

What Is Model Context Protocol and Why Should Hotel Leaders Care?

Model Context Protocol solves a problem so fundamental that most business leaders never knew it existed.

Before MCP, connecting an AI model like ChatGPT to a hotel’s booking system required custom engineering for every single connection. Each system spoke a different technical language. Connecting ten AI platforms to one hundred hotel databases meant building one thousand separate integrations. The economics made it impossible for anyone except the largest technology companies.

MCP functions as a universal grammar that any AI agent can speak and any business system can understand. It’s the translation layer that lets artificial intelligence actually interact with the real world of inventory, pricing, and transactions.

Here’s what this means in practice: When a traveler asks an AI assistant for hotel recommendations, that AI can now drill past generic search results into your actual systems. Real-time room availability. Current pricing. Specific amenities like connecting rooms, Central Park views, or guaranteed late checkout. The AI doesn’t scrape your website. It reads your database directly through secure, controlled channels you define.

The hotels that enable this access become visible to AI. The hotels that don’t become invisible to an entire channel of travelers who never visit hotel websites at all.

This represents the most significant shift in travel distribution since online travel agencies transformed the industry in the early 2000s. Except this time, the change is happening faster.

The Strategic Stakes: Who Controls the AI Interface Controls the Booking

Here’s the uncomfortable reality that should concern every hotel operator.

Power in travel distribution is shifting from inventory owners to those who control AI interfaces. MCP doesn’t eliminate gatekeepers. It creates new ones.

Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel describes his vision of the “connected trip,” an AI assistant that acts as a personal digital travel agent. This AI would proactively manage disruptions, automatically rebooking flights after delays, updating car rentals, and shifting restaurant reservations. For travelers, it sounds remarkably convenient.

For hotels, the implications are more complex.

If that AI assistant defaults to recommending certain hotel brands over others, properties not in the AI’s consideration set simply disappear from discovery. The traveler never knows they existed. They were filtered out before human decision-making even began.

Booking.com and Expedia have already integrated with ChatGPT through MCP-based development kits. Users can search real hotel inventory inside the conversation. And here’s the strategic detail worth noting: by design, these integrations currently push final booking back to OTA websites. The AI does the discovery work. The OTA captures the commission.

This isn’t disintermediation. It’s reintermediation through a new technology stack.

The entities controlling AI interfaces, default settings, and conversational pathways will shape traveler decisions at unprecedented scale. If most MCP connections run through OTA platforms, the hotel becomes secondary to whoever controls the AI conversation.

Why AI Actually Favors Direct Hotel Connections

The OTA advantage in AI isn’t permanent. It might not last five years.

Jason Cincotta, cofounder of hotel AI technology company Kismet, articulates why: AI systems inherently favor “differentiated information, differentiated content, and differentiated rates.” Their operational logic rewards unique data over aggregated data.

When a traveler asks for a room with a specific view, connecting rooms for a family trip, or a late checkout guarantee, standardized OTA listings fall short. The AI needs granular information that only the property controls. As Cincotta reasons, the AI’s constant search for better information will naturally lead to direct connections with hotels.

This is the counter-strategy forward-thinking operators should build now.

Through direct MCP integration, hotels can expose information OTAs simply cannot replicate:

Real-time visibility into specific room attributes like view type, floor level, balcony access, and connecting room availability. Exclusive packages available only through direct booking channels. Dynamic information about on-property services, restaurant availability, and spa openings. Guaranteed best rates that undercut commission-inflated OTA pricing.

OTAs aggregate inventory. They cannot match the depth and freshness of data that hotels control at the source. An AI agent optimizing for the traveler’s actual request will increasingly recognize this limitation.

The strategic window is narrow. Hotels that establish direct MCP connections now will train AI agents to query them directly. Hotels that wait will find those agents have already formed habits around OTA integrations.

How Ready Is the Hotel Industry for AI Visibility?

Apaleo’s assessment breaks the hotel industry into three distinct tiers.

The vanguard represents roughly twenty to thirty percent of hotels. These operators are technologically ready and actively experimenting with AI integration. They’ll capture disproportionate direct booking share as AI adoption accelerates.

The constrained majority accounts for approximately fifty percent of properties. They’re interested in AI integration but trapped by legacy property management systems designed before interoperability mattered. Their platforms simply weren’t built for this level of data sharing.

The laggards, roughly twenty percent of hotels, aren’t engaging with AI distribution at all. They’re increasingly invisible to travelers who will never visit their websites.

The barrier for the constrained majority isn’t awareness. It’s architecture. Mapping proprietary data from fragmented legacy systems to MCP’s universal format requires significant technical work. The costs can be prohibitive for independent properties and small chains.

Technology vendors like Apaleo and Kismet are racing to solve this with platforms designed to make AI compatibility more accessible. The question is whether the constrained majority will move fast enough to matter.

Legacy technology has always created competitive disadvantage. In the AI era, it creates existential risk to discoverability itself.

The Five-Step Framework for MCP Readiness

Positioning your hotel for AI visibility requires systematic action across technical infrastructure, content strategy, and partnership management. Each step builds on the previous one.

Step One: Audit Your Machine Readability

The first question every hotel operator must answer: Can AI agents access your inventory directly?

If your current property management system can’t expose structured data to external AI through standardized protocols, you’re already losing visibility in a channel that will only grow. Your audit should assess several dimensions.

What to do: Evaluate whether your PMS, booking engine, and channel manager can share real-time data through APIs. Document which information is currently accessible and which remains siloed. Identify gaps between what AI agents need and what your systems can provide.

Why this matters: AI agents can only recommend hotels they can see. If your inventory lives in closed systems that don’t speak MCP, you’re invisible to the fastest-growing discovery channel in travel.

Common mistakes: Assuming your technology vendors have AI integration on their roadmap. Many legacy platforms weren’t designed for this level of interoperability and won’t adapt quickly. Waiting for your current vendor to catch up may cost you years of visibility.

What good looks like: A clear technical assessment showing which systems can share data through MCP, which require middleware, and which need replacement. A timeline for achieving full machine readability.

Step Two: Develop Your Differentiated Data Strategy

Generic hotel listings get commodity treatment from AI systems. Rich, specific content about your unique offerings gives AI agents reasons to recommend you over alternatives.

What to do: Identify every piece of differentiated information only you control. Room-specific attributes like exact views, connecting room configurations, and floor levels. Exclusive packages not available through intermediaries. Dynamic availability for amenities like spa appointments, restaurant reservations, and late checkout options.

Why this matters: OTAs aggregate standardized inventory data. They cannot replicate the depth of information that originates at the property level. AI systems searching for the best answer to a traveler’s specific request will increasingly recognize this limitation.

Common mistakes: Treating MCP as simply another distribution channel rather than an opportunity to compete on information quality. Exposing the same generic data that OTAs already have provides no competitive advantage.

What good looks like: A comprehensive data inventory identifying every piece of information that could differentiate your property in AI recommendations. A prioritized plan for structuring and exposing that data through MCP.

Step Three: Evaluate Your Integration Options

Direct MCP implementation ranges from building your own server to purchasing turnkey solutions. The right approach depends on your technical resources and strategic priorities.

What to do: Research MCP implementation options appropriate for your property’s size and technical capabilities. Cloud providers offer MCP server hosting. Specialized hospitality technology vendors like Apaleo and Kismet provide platforms designed for faster implementation. Your PMS vendor may have MCP compatibility in development.

Why this matters: The build-versus-buy decision affects both timeline and ongoing maintenance costs. Properties waiting for perfect solutions risk falling further behind early adopters.

Common mistakes: Underestimating the ongoing maintenance required for MCP integrations. Overestimating the technical complexity of initial implementation. Choosing integration partners based on existing vendor relationships rather than AI capability.

What good looks like: A clear implementation roadmap with defined milestones. Vendor contracts that include AI integration capabilities. Technical resources allocated to maintain and optimize your MCP presence.

Step Four: Prioritize Content That AI Can Parse and Cite

Every piece of information about your hotel should be structured so AI systems can easily understand and reference it. Think of this as search engine optimization for artificial intelligence.

What to do: Structure property information in formats AI can readily parse. Use clear, factual statements about amenities, policies, and room attributes. Provide specific data points rather than marketing language. Ensure consistency between what your website says and what your MCP integration exposes.

Why this matters: AI systems are trained to provide accurate, citable answers to user questions. Content structured for machine readability is more likely to surface in AI recommendations than marketing copy designed for human emotional response.

Common mistakes: Assuming existing website content translates directly to AI-readable formats. Using vague marketing language where specific data would serve travelers better. Maintaining inconsistent information across different channels.

What good looks like: Property information formatted in structured data that AI can easily parse. Clear factual statements about amenities and policies. Consistent data across all distribution channels including your MCP integration.

Step Five: Monitor Your OTA Relationships Strategically

Your OTA partners are integrating with AI platforms. Understanding how they represent your property in AI contexts is essential to maintaining control of your distribution strategy.

What to do: Request visibility into how your property appears in OTA AI integrations. Understand what data they’re sharing and how your listing is positioned relative to competitors. Compare the completeness of information available through OTA channels versus what you could provide directly.

Why this matters: OTAs are moving fast to capture AI-driven demand. If they control richer data about your property than your own direct channels expose, they’ll capture bookings that could have come directly to you.

Common mistakes: Assuming OTA integrations will represent your property accurately and completely. Allowing OTAs to maintain information advantages that should belong to the property. Ignoring how commission economics play out in AI-driven distribution.

What good looks like: Clear visibility into your property’s AI presence across OTA platforms. Rate parity and information parity between OTA and direct channels. Direct MCP connections that give AI agents reasons to bypass intermediaries.

How This Plays Out: A Realistic Scenario

Consider a family planning a Broadway trip asking their AI assistant for hotel recommendations. They want connecting rooms, walkable distance to theaters, breakfast included, and the ability to store luggage before their evening show.

In the current model, the AI searches generic hotel data, returns options from OTA feeds, and the booking flows through traditional channels with commission attached.

In the MCP-enabled model, hotels with direct AI integration can expose exactly the information this family needs. Which specific rooms connect on which floors. Walking distances to specific theaters. Breakfast hours and menus. Luggage storage policies with real-time availability.

The hotel offering the most complete, specific answer to this family’s actual request becomes the AI’s recommendation. Not because of paid advertising. Not because of OTA placement algorithms. Because the AI found better information and gave a better answer.

This is where the strategic opportunity lies. Hotels that can provide AI systems with differentiated, accurate, real-time information about their specific offerings will capture bookings that would otherwise flow to properties with less complete data.

The constraint is equally important to acknowledge. Properties with legacy systems that cannot expose this level of detail will struggle to compete on information quality. Their rooms may be perfect for this family’s needs, but if the AI can’t see those details, the recommendation goes elsewhere.

What MCP Does Not Solve

Realistic expectations prevent wasted resources and missed opportunities.

MCP provides the infrastructure for AI visibility but doesn’t guarantee bookings. Properties still need compelling offerings that meet traveler needs. Better AI visibility amplifies existing strengths. It doesn’t create them.

Legacy system migration remains a significant barrier for many properties. MCP readiness for hotels running on outdated property management systems requires substantial technical investment. Acknowledging this reality allows for better planning.

Data quality matters as much as data access. Exposing inaccurate or outdated information through MCP damages AI relationships faster than providing no information at all. AI systems track which data sources provide reliable answers and adjust recommendations accordingly.

Competitive dynamics continue. As more hotels adopt MCP, the baseline for visibility rises. Early advantage comes from moving first. Sustainable advantage comes from providing consistently better information than competitors.

The Payment Layer That Completes the Transaction

MCP handles data access. A complementary protocol handles money.

The Agentic Commerce Protocol, introduced through OpenAI’s partnership with Stripe, enables transactions inside AI conversations. MCP determines what a guest can book. ACP determines how they pay for it.

The convergence points toward a traveler experience that never leaves the conversation. Research, comparison, customization, booking, and payment all within a single AI interaction. No website redirect. No checkout page. No friction point where the booking might fall apart.

Critically, ACP keeps the hotel as merchant of record. The direct financial relationship with the guest remains intact even when the booking happens inside an AI interface.

This isn’t speculative technology. The integrations are live. The trajectory runs toward full automation of the booking journey within conversational interfaces.

Hotels positioned for this paradigm will capture transactions seamlessly. Hotels that aren’t will watch AI agents route bookings through intermediaries equipped to handle the payment flow.

What Success Looks Like in Twelve Months

Hotels that act on this framework now should expect measurable progress within a year.

Technical infrastructure that can expose real-time inventory data to AI agents through MCP or equivalent protocols. Clear visibility into how your property appears in AI-driven discovery across both direct and OTA channels. Differentiated data strategy that gives AI systems reasons to recommend your property over alternatives with less complete information.

The booking patterns may take longer to shift meaningfully. AI adoption among travelers continues growing, but the transition from traditional search to AI-driven discovery happens gradually. The value of early positioning compounds over time as AI agents learn which data sources provide the best answers.

The risk of inaction is clearer. Properties that ignore AI visibility for another twelve months will find themselves further behind an accelerating curve. Catching up becomes progressively more difficult as early adopters establish themselves in AI recommendation patterns.

Three Actions to Take This Week

First, request an AI readiness assessment from your property management system vendor. Ask specifically about MCP compatibility and their roadmap for AI integration. If they can’t provide clear answers, begin evaluating vendors who can.

Second, audit your property information for AI readability. Identify every piece of differentiated data about your rooms, amenities, and services. Assess how that information could be structured for machine consumption. Document the gaps between what AI agents would need and what you currently provide.

Third, investigate your property’s presence in OTA AI integrations. Contact your OTA partners and ask how your hotel appears when travelers use AI assistants to search their platforms. Compare that representation to the information you could provide directly.

These actions require no technical implementation. They establish the baseline understanding necessary for informed decisions about AI visibility investment.

The Five-Year View on Hotel Discovery

We’re watching the first phase of a transformation that will reshape hospitality distribution more fundamentally than the OTA revolution two decades ago.

Today, AI agents help travelers plan. Tomorrow, they’ll book autonomously. Eventually, they’ll manage entire trip logistics without human intervention, handling disruptions, optimizing experiences, and processing transactions.

The hotels visible to those agents will capture demand. The hotels invisible to them will struggle to understand why direct inquiries keep declining while marketing spend keeps rising.

Model Context Protocol is the standard that determines visibility. The technology is ready. Adoption is accelerating. The window for early-mover advantage is closing.

Being open-source and vendor-neutral, MCP has achieved what industry observers describe as unprecedented adoption speed. Anthropic, the company that created the protocol, donated it to the Linux Foundation’s Agentic AI Foundation. No single company controls the standard.

That openness explains why adoption has been so rapid. It also explains why waiting to see how things develop isn’t a strategy. It’s a choice to become invisible to an increasingly significant share of travelers.

Not being machine-readable will be the fastest way to become invisible.

Visibility has always driven distribution. The mechanism is changing. The stakes aren’t.
