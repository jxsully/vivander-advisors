How long does it take to change what AI recommends?

Vivander Advisors helps businesses understand how AI describes and recommends them. This guide connects AI discoverability with clear business facts, buyer questions and evidence you can inspect.

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Days to weeks for anything on your own site, because retrieval is live and a page that changes today is re-read the next time an assistant fetches it. Months for anything that depends on other people writing about you. Longer still for whatever a model learned in training. The two halves move on different clocks, and a report that does not separate them cannot tell you which lever worked. For the current Vivander Advisors audit, clients can choose retests of the same prompt set at 60 and 90 days. These are optional and happen only if the client signing up wants them. Site-side and off-site changes are reported separately.

Three changes in one week. Two platforms moved in thirty days. The third took ninety. Same property.

The family-owned property, in three changes

Rate range in text on the rooms page. The family-friendly answer in a heading. One editorial mention that stated the same facts.

The 30-day re-test showed the property recommended in family prompts on Gemini and AI Mode. ChatGPT moved at 90 days, after the OTA listing was matched to the site.

Nothing about the property changed. What the engines could read about it did.

Why site-side changes show fast

Every AI answer starts with a retrieval step against a live index or a live fetch. Move the rate into text, put the question in a heading, answer it first, and the next fetch reads the new page. In my re-tests, heading and fact fixes have shown in 30-day results on at least one platform in most engagements. Google's surfaces and Perplexity tend to move first because they fetch most often. ChatGPT's search index refreshes on its own schedule.

The exception is a page that was never retrieved. If crawlers were blocked or the facts were invisible, the first change is access, and citation follows only after the page enters the index. That alone can take weeks.

Why off-site changes take months

Corroboration depends on other people publishing. An editorial mention has a pitch cycle. A review platform needs guests. YouTube videos need producing and transcripts need indexing. And the platforms weight sources differently, so a placement that moves Perplexity may do nothing for ChatGPT.

Ahrefs' fake-brand experiment moved five of eight engines within two months of seeding three pages. That is the fastest documented off-site change, and it involved a brand with no competition for its name.

The training half is slower still. Whatever a model learned about your category before its cutoff sits in the weights until the next version. Treat that half as reputation infrastructure and the retrieval half as the tactical lever.

Why the answers move on their own

AI answers drift with nobody touching anything. Kirsten and colleagues tracked 4,706 queries across two months: the pages Google's AI Overviews cited for the same questions overlapped by 18 percent, and 9 to 28 percent of decisions changed at the lowest temperature setting. ChatGPT's Reddit citations fell roughly six-fold in one month in 2025.

A change you observe after your fix may be the platform, not you. That is why I run at least three passes per prompt per platform across two days at every measurement point, and report the stability figure with the result.

What does a realistic timeline look like?

Weeks one and two: access fixes, the Cloudflare check, robots rules, facts into initial HTML. Then weeks two to four: heading and answer-first rewrites on the ten pages that matter, implicit facts published under question headings. At 60 days: an optional first retest if the audit client wants it; review platform-specific changes without assuming improvement. Months two and three: Business Profile aligned to the site, OTA and reservation listings matched, first editorial and video corroboration. Day 90: second re-test, and expect off-site movement to begin. Then quarterly against the same prompt set, because the platforms keep moving.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Fix access and facts first. They move fastest and they gate everything else. Start off-site corroboration in week two, because it takes the longest.

For your role. Keep a ledger of what changed when. Without it, you cannot tell your fix from the platform's drift, and someone will take credit for weather.

For your guests. The window between where their behavior is and where the booking infrastructure will catch up is open now. They are already asking. The transaction layer is still being built.

What I do not know

How long a gained recommendation persists once it appears. My 90-day re-tests are the longest window I have data on. Anyone quoting a durability figure is guessing.

Where this is going

Properties that build presence inside AI conversations now will have structural presence before the transaction layer launches. Those that wait will be optimizing for a system that moved past them.

Speed matters here in a way it did not in search. Models update fast. Waiting for the next budget cycle means playing from behind, against a clock that does not read budget cycles.
