The Cyrano de Bergerac Effect (When Online Genius Becomes an In-Person Dunce)

Michael J. Goldrich · July 9, 2025

We’ve all encountered them: the team members who dominate the remote meeting, their insights razor-sharp, their presentations flawless. Their emails brimming with strategic foresight, and their Slack and Teams messages ooze with brilliance.

But then you meet them in person, and something shifts. The wit feels dulled, the presence muted, the brilliance… borrowed. This growing phenomenon is what I propose we call the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect.

The Cyrano de Bergerac Effect is a carefully constructed online persona that is infused with AI intelligence, making individuals appear far more competent than they might genuinely be in an unassisted, face-to-face setting.

The classic tale of Cyrano de Bergerac: a wordsmith of unmatched “eloquence” who, self-conscious of his prominent nose, ghost-writes passionate love letters for the handsome but tongue-tied Christian. Roxane falls deeply in love, not with Christian’s true self, but with Cyrano’s carefully crafted words.

In our modern age, a similar deception plays out daily, but the “Cyrano” isn’t another person, it’s the digital environment itself augmented by advanced artificial intelligence.

The Coming Wave: AI’s Amplification of the Illusion

While digital communication has long allowed for some degree of performance, the advent of sophisticated AI tools promises to amplify the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect to unprecedented levels.

Picture this: a future where subtle, undetectable AI overlays feed you real-time prompts and answers during a live conversation. This isn’t science fiction. Tools like Cluely are already demonstrating this capability, operating in stealth mode, monitoring your screen and audio, and whispering contextual suggestions directly to you.

Real-time, on-demand ideas, tactical insights, and even emotional cues being fed directly to the user. The “brilliance” becomes a direct download, indistinguishable from genuine thought to the unsuspecting observer.

This technology now enables you to simulate intelligence, creating a carefully constructed online persona infused with AI. You can appear super smart and articulate, even when the underlying knowledge or quick wit might not be fully yours.

 The Digital Stage: Where Everyone’s a Star

Our digital workspaces have become highly curated stages. Email, Teams, Slack, and collaborative documents offer us the luxury of time, time to refine, edit, and perfect every thought before it’s delivered.

This asynchronous advantage allows for a level of polish that’s simply unattainable in spontaneous, face-to-face conversations. We become masters of the “hyperpersonal” advantage, as researchers describe it, carefully crafting every word to project an idealized version of ourselves.

Video calls enhance this controlled performance. The webcam frame allows us to hide notes, secondary screens, and even discreet Google searches just out of frame. This selective self-presentation amplifies perceived credibility, masking any hesitations, stumbles, or genuine real-time processing that might occur.

The stark reality of the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect becomes painfully clear when the digital curtain falls and in-person interaction begins.

Based on the above, we can understand why some online superstars transform into real-life duds:  

  • The Missing Human Connection
    Human interaction thrives on subtle cues: eye contact, body language, shared energy, and true spontaneous dialogue.
    Studies show a significant difference in neural alignment between in-person and online interactions. When we’re physically together, our brains literally synchronize more, fostering deeper rapport and intuitive understanding. 
  • The Loss of Cognitive Safety Nets
    Online, there’s a buffer for reflection, research, and AI assistance. In person, there’s no pause button, no instant Google search, and no whispering AI. The brain is on its own, demanding immediate, unbuffered thinking.

  • The Atrophy of Real Skills
    When AI consistently supplies the answers, the underlying cognitive muscles, critical thinking, creative problem-solving, and witty repartee begin to atrophy.

The Real Stakes. Beyond a Bad First Impression

The implications of the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect stretch far beyond a few awkward social encounters; they have tangible impacts on our businesses, our colleagues, and our culture. 

  • Impact on Our Meetings and Business
    Imagine a sales pitch where the lead seems brilliant remotely, only to falter and lose the deal in a follow-up in-person meeting. Or a strategic planning session where the polished virtual strategist can’t lead a live brainstorming. This translates into tangible financial and operational costs. Research suggests the average cost of a bad hire is around $17,000, with figures potentially soaring much higher for senior roles. The Cyrano de Bergerac Effect directly contributes to this risk by obscuring a candidate’s true in-person capabilities.
  • Impact on Our Colleagues and Culture
    When team members realize that a colleague’s brilliance was, in part, an AI-backed performance, it fundamentally damages psychological safety and trust within teams. This erosion of authenticity fosters suspicion, hindering genuine collaboration, open communication, and shared innovation. It cultivates a “performance culture” over a “trust culture,” where people are more focused on presenting an ideal self than on genuine contribution. 
  • The Echo Chamber of Simulation
    Just as social media platforms have allowed us to curate hyper-perfect selfies and simulate ideal lives, creating a disconnect between our online persona and true happiness, this new wave of AI technology enables the simulation of intelligence. We’ve moved from filtering our appearance to filtering our thoughts, potentially creating a workforce where perceived competence is manufactured, not earned. It’s something profound to consider: if our digital presence can simulate true happenings with perfect selfies, what happens when it can simulate true intelligence with perfect answers?


Navigating the New Reality. Best Practices for an AI-Augmented World

I fully support the usage of generative AI; it is a transformative technology that, when used responsibly, can genuinely augment our existing abilities, streamline workflows, and unlock new levels of creativity. When AI helps us refine our arguments, quickly synthesize information to deepen our understanding, or accelerate creative processes, it is an incredible asset. It should augment existing abilities, not be used to show yourself off as something you are not. It’s perfectly fine to be amplified by AI, to use it as a powerful tool that makes you even better. However, to leverage AI to present yourself as an imposter, to feign intelligence or competence that isn’t genuinely yours, is something we should be deeply concerned about. This deception holds us back, erodes trust, and undermines meritocracy.

Here’s how individuals and organizations can navigate this new reality and foster a culture that embraces AI responsibly, while guarding against the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect:

For Individuals: 

  • Practice Unassisted Presence:

    Regularly engage in conversations, presentations, and problem-solving sessions without any AI assistance. Record yourself, review your performance, and actively seek feedback on your spontaneity and articulation in live settings. 

  • Deliberate Skill Cultivation:

    Dedicate time to honing your raw, unassisted skills. Engage in debates, impromptu speaking, or critical thinking exercises. Think of it as intellectual cross-training to build genuine mental agility. 
  • Embrace Transparency (When Appropriate):

    If using AI to assist in a professional context, consider being transparent about its role where ethical and practical. “I used an AI tool to help me draft this initial report, but the analysis is my own.”

For Leaders & Teams:

  • Balanced Assessment:

    Design interview and performance review processes that blend asynchronous, remote evaluations with mandatory live, interactive, and whenever possible, in-person scenarios. Include spontaneous problem-solving challenges and unscripted discussions to gauge genuine, unassisted abilities. 
  • Clear AI Usage Policies:

    Develop and communicate explicit, ethical guidelines for AI tool usage. Distinguish between AI as an enhancement (e.g., for note-taking, transcription, or generating initial drafts that are then heavily edited by a human) and AI as a substitute for original thought or an enabler of deception (e.g., real-time prompting for answers in live interactions). These policies should be well-known and consistently enforced. 
  • Invest in Human Skills Development:

    Prioritize training and development programs that strengthen core human abilities that AI cannot replicate: critical thinking under pressure, improvisation, active listening, public speaking (without teleprompters), and emotional intelligence. These “soft skills” become even more valuable and differentiating in an AI-augmented world.     
  • Foster a Culture of Authentic Vulnerability:

    Create an environment where it’s safe for individuals to admit when they don’t know something, to ask for help, and to engage in genuine, unfiltered discussions. Celebrate intellectual curiosity and the effort of learning, rather than just polished, potentially artificial, outputs.


Leveling Up. Converting Borrowed Knowledge into True Expertise

If you find yourself exhibiting the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect, relying on external tools to simulate knowledge you don’t fully possess, there’s a powerful way to truly level up. To genuinely prepare for discussions, to articulate your insights with authority, and to speak confidently on what you’ve shared, you need to internalize that knowledge. You need to transform borrowed information into your own deep understanding.

One of the most remarkable tools emerging for this kind of rapid, authentic learning is Google NotebookLM. Unlike generative AI that simply produces text, NotebookLM is designed to help you understand and synthesize information from your own uploaded sources. You can feed it reports, articles, meeting notes, even entire books, and it helps you: 

  • Ask complex questions and get detailed answers derived directly from your materials.
  • Summarize vast amounts of information into digestible insights. 
  • Identify key themes and connections across multiple documents. 
  • Generate new ideas and perspectives based on your uploaded knowledge base.

 It acts as a dynamic study partner, enabling you to become an expert on virtually any topic in a fraction of the time. This is about accelerating genuine comprehension and retention. For those who realize the limitations of the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect and truly want to “level up,” building deep, articulable expertise with tools like Google NotebookLM is the complete solution. It empowers you to not just present information, but to truly own it, allowing your authentic brilliance to shine through, whether online or in person.

 

The Lasting Echo of the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect

From the polished screen to the unfiltered room, the journey through the Cyrano de Bergerac Effect is a reminder of the perceived nature of competence. It begins with the seemingly innocuous convenience of digital polish, graduates to the subtle infusion of AI-driven ‘intelligence,’ and culminates in the unsettling reality of in-person disconnect.

We live in a world where perfect selfies can simulate happiness, and now, technology is poised to help us simulate intelligence. We are at a critical juncture where the line between true amplification and deceptive impersonation blurs. This effect is a profound mirror reflecting who we are becoming. It compels us to ask: in the pursuit of appearing smarter, are we losing the very essence of genuine understanding and trust? It’s something truly significant to think about.
