What is the HFTP AI Collective?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

The HFTP AI Collective is a group of more than thirty hospitality industry AI experts and liaisons from ten industry associations, convened by Hospitality Financial and Technology Professionals to develop AI resources for the industry: thought leadership, an AI certificate and standards. It grew out of the HFTP AI Council and unveiled its strategic roadmap at HITEC 2026. I co-chair it alongside Shannon McCallum, and in 2026 I joined the HFTP Global Board of Directors for the 2026 to 2027 term. Vivander Advisors' audit method is one input to the Collective's work on how AI systems read and represent hospitality businesses.

Something unusual happened in hospitality this year. Ten associations that compete for members, sponsors and attention agreed to collaborate on one question.

Which associations are part of it?

AHLA. AAHOA. BLLA. CHTA. CMAA. HSMAI. HTNG. ICHRIE. iHITA. JHTA. All ten joined HFTP's AI Collective, alongside HFTP itself. That breadth is what makes it different from any single association's technology committee, and it is why I said yes to co-chairing it.

What is the Collective working on?

Three tracks announced at HITEC 2026.

Thought leadership: research and guidance on AI adoption, governance and workforce change in hospitality.

An AI certificate: a credential for hospitality professionals that establishes a shared baseline of AI literacy. Literacy before automation, at industry scale.

Standards: shared definitions and practices for how hospitality organizations evaluate, deploy and govern AI, including how AI systems read and represent hospitality businesses.

HFTP has also launched Workforce 20X: The AI Evolution, a learning lab at HITEC on how AI changes hospitality roles.

Why does it matter for AI visibility?

Because the questions the Collective is working on are the questions that decide whether a hotel appears in an AI answer. How AI systems read hospitality data. The way businesses should govern the crawlers and agents that read them. What a shared literacy baseline looks like.

Standards written by the industry's own associations carry more weight with vendors and platforms than any one firm's method. My retrieval gate, my recommendation-event definition and my evidence register are proposals into that process. I would rather they be argued with by thirty experts than adopted by one client.

What is my role?

Co-chair of the AI Collective and, from 2026, a member of the HFTP Global Board. I have opened the HFTP CIO Summit three years running, spoken at HITEC and the Executive Vendor Summit, and authored the hospitality industry foundation's research series on generative AI in commercial talent management, which the Collective's thought leadership track builds on.

How does a hospitality professional get involved?

Through HFTP membership and the AI certificate when it launches. Via the participating associations' liaisons. And through the sessions at HITEC and the CIO Summit where the Collective reports its work.

Where this is going

The question CIOs should be asking is not which AI tool to use. It is how to redesign the way work gets done. The Collective is the first place I have seen the industry ask that question together instead of vendor by vendor.

Ten associations in one room. That is the signal. The standards are the consequence.
