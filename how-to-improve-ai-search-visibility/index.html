Field guide · AI discoverability

Make your organization easier for AI search to understand.

A practical guide to clearer facts, stronger evidence and answers that help buyers evaluate your organization.

The buyer’s question

“Who can help our managers use AI effectively?”

The short answer: make your organization easy to find, understand and verify. Publish direct answers to buyer questions, keep business facts consistent and measure how different AI tools describe your organization.

AI discoverability means how easily an AI search or assistant can find and represent your organization when someone asks a relevant question. A brand mention, a linked citation and a recommendation are different outcomes.

Your organization can improve the information available to these systems. An organization cannot guarantee inclusion or control every answer.

Make the basics strong

Make a better source, one page at a time.

Think of each page as evidence a buyer could use to decide whether your organization fits. Start with the most important gaps.

01

Collect real buyer questions.

Start with sales calls, inquiry emails and customer conversations. Include category questions, comparisons and practical concerns about fit, cost and getting started.

02

State your organization clearly.

Explain what your organization offers, who it serves, where it is available and what happens next. Keep your organization’s name and contact details consistent across public profiles.

03

Answer on dedicated pages.

Give important services their own useful pages. Lead with a direct answer, then add scope, examples, limitations and a clear route to inquire or buy.

04

Make the evidence specific.

Use substantiated case studies, original research, named authors and current sources. Explain what an example demonstrates and what it does not establish.

05

Check access and accuracy.

Review indexing, readable text, broken links and crawler settings. Structured data should describe the visible page accurately.

06

Establish a baseline.

Run a consistent set of buyer questions across relevant AI tools. Save the answers and sources so your organization can compare changes over time.

A concrete content example

Replace a vague claim with a useful answer.

Hard to evaluate

“We transform your future with innovative solutions.”

Specific and verifiable

“We train managers and employees to use AI in their actual workflows. A workshop includes practical exercises, output review and agreed next steps.”

The stronger version helps a buyer understand the service. Add the scope, intended audience, delivery format and next step. Publish prices when they are established; explain the quoting process when the work is custom.

Keep SEO fundamentals in the plan.

Google’s guidance says the established SEO practices remain relevant to its AI search features. Pages must meet eligibility requirements, and there is no special schema required for these features. Read Google’s guidance on AI features and websites.

Other AI services have their own crawling and retrieval behavior. Review each platform’s official controls before changing access rules. A training crawler and a search crawler may serve different purposes.

Build a first-month improvement list.

  • Write ten important buyer questions.
  • Record how selected AI tools answer them today.
  • Fix unclear or contradictory business facts.
  • Improve three high-priority service or answer pages.
  • Add useful, verifiable evidence and author information.
  • Check page access, internal links and structured data.
  • Record the changes and set a date to measure again.

This is an illustrative working plan. Search systems may take time to discover changes, and results can vary between runs.

Questions people ask

Is AI discoverability the same as SEO?

They overlap. Technical access, useful content and credible evidence matter to both. AI discoverability also examines how conversational tools describe, cite and recommend your organization.

Will schema make AI recommend your organization?

Schema can help describe a page accurately. It does not guarantee an AI mention, citation or recommendation. Keep the markup consistent with what visitors can actually see.

Should your organization publish large volumes of AI-generated articles?

Prioritize useful answers grounded in your organization and expertise. Repetitive pages with little original information can create more maintenance without helping buyers.

How can your organization identify the biggest gaps?
