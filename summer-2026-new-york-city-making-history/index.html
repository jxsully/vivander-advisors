Summer of 2026: New York City Making History

Michael J. Goldrich · January 27, 2026

The Stage is Set

Forget recovery. Forget cautious optimism. New York City is done playing catch-up.

That was the message on January 22nd when hospitality professionals packed the ballroom at Hard Rock Hotel for the first HSMAI Greater New York Chapter luncheon of 2026. This is the largest HSMAI chapter in the world, and the room reflected it. Standing room only. Energy electric. And the news from NYC Tourism and Conventions matched the mood.

This summer, the FIFA World Cup, America’s 250th anniversary, Sailport 250, and a relocated Fleet Week will converge on the five boroughs in a way no city has ever experienced. New York will not be hosting events. New York will be the event.

Four leaders from New York City Tourism and Conventions took the stage to break down what lies ahead. Kelly Curtin, EVP of Membership and Destination Services, set the context for the year. Kelly Jones, VP of Research and Insights, delivered the data. Nancy Mammana, Chief Marketing Officer, outlined how the city is positioning itself to the world. And Jerry Cito, Chief Sales Officer, rounded out the presentation with a perspective on business development. Together, they painted a picture of a city preparing for its biggest summer in decades.

The Data: $81.4 Billion and Climbing

Kelly Jones brought the numbers that silenced any lingering pandemic pessimism. Jones joined NYC Tourism from Hilton Corporate, and she brought the rigor to prove it.

In 2024, New York City welcomed 64.5 million visitors. Those visitors generated $81.4 billion in economic impact and $7.1 billion in local tax revenue. For every $100 spent in this city, $10 goes directly back to New York. Tourism is not an industry here. It is the infrastructure.

The city has reached 97% of pre-pandemic visitor levels. But the real story is in the composition. International visitors represent 20% of arrivals. That 20% drives 50% of all spending. The premium traveler is back, and they are spending.

The top ten international feeder markets have held steady since before the pandemic: the UK, France, Germany, China, and others continue to send their travelers here. Canada dropped 18% in 2025 due to economic and political headwinds. France and Germany also softened. Projections show all three rebounding in 2026. The appetite for New York has not diminished. People want to travel. They want to travel here.

Domestic travel grew 1.7% last year, picking up the slack while international markets recalibrated.

For hoteliers, the metrics are even more encouraging. Overnight visitation now accounts for 61% of all visitors, up from pre-pandemic levels. Day trips have dropped to 39%. That trend line keeps rising. Guests are staying longer. RevPAR and ADR both grew 5% in what Jones described as a price-led market. New York does not have a flood of new inventory hitting the market. What it has is pricing power, and that power is holding firm.

Business travel remains the slower recovery story. International corporate travel dipped again in 2025 amid visa friction and economic uncertainty. Leisure has roared back. Business is following, but cautiously.

Seasonality in New York is more balanced than most destinations. Q4 peaks with fall foliage and holiday tourism. Q1 remains the soft spot. Programs like Winter Outing and Hotel Week, running through February 12th, are designed to fill that gap. Hotel Week participation has grown for four consecutive years.

The Events: A Summer That Will Redefine the City

FIFA World Cup 2026: 39 Days, Eight Matches, One Championship

The most watched sporting event on the planet arrives June 11th. New York hosts its first match June 13th. The championship takes place July 19th at New York-New Jersey Stadium. Eight total matches. Billions of eyes on this city.

New York is the only host city with FanFest activations running all 39 days of the tournament. Liberty State Park anchors the celebration in partnership with Live Nation. The Billie Jean King Tennis Center hosts FanFest June 17th through 28th. Rockefeller Center, sponsored by Telemundo and NBC, takes over from July 4th through the final match.

Visitors will pour in from every competing nation, from neighboring states, from around the corner. They will want matches. They will also want everything else New York offers. The host committee has developed a toolkit for businesses wanting to host watch parties, complete with FIFA guidelines and the “Welcome World” branding available to all.

America 250 and Sailport 250: History Floats Into the Harbor

While the World Cup rolls through its knockout rounds, America’s 250th birthday arrives. Sailport 250 runs July 3rd through 9th, with July 4th delivering a spectacle this city has never seen.

Forty-six tall ships from nations around the world will parade through New York Harbor. The Parade of Ships launches at 9:30 AM from the Verrazano Bridge and finishes at the George Washington Bridge by 2:00 PM. Fifteen miles of waterfront viewing stretches across Brooklyn, Manhattan, Staten Island, and the Bronx.

An international naval review adds 50 more tall ships to the harbor between 7:30 and 9:30 that morning. Then the sky takes over. One hundred twenty aircraft, including the Blue Angels and the Royal Air Force, will conduct an aerial review lasting one hour. The Blue Angels are skipping Jones Beach on Memorial Day. They are saving themselves for this.

Macy’s has announced the largest fireworks display in the history of their July 4th show.

Fleet Week Joins the Party

Fleet Week is moving from Memorial Day to July 4th weekend. In a typical year, around 2,000 service members participate. This year, organizers expect between 15,000 and 20,000 Marines and Navy personnel to flood the city.

World Cup. America 250. Sailport. Fleet Week. The Blue Angels. The largest fireworks show ever. All happening within the same ten-day window. The compression of demand will be unprecedented.

HSMAI Greater New York: Be in the Room Where It Happens

The January luncheon at Hard Rock Hotel is exactly why HSMAI Greater New York exists. This is where hospitality professionals hear the data before anyone else. This is where the dots get connected between tourism trends and operational decisions. This is where relationships form that turn insight into action.

The chapter has a packed calendar for 2026. A World Cup panel will break down what hoteliers need to know before June arrives. A Luxury Travel panel will explore the high-value segments driving disproportionate spend. A discussion on AI will address how technology is reshaping hospitality operations and guest experience. The Berkman Awards will celebrate excellence across the industry. The Summer Bash, produced in partnership with over 15 associations, will bring the community together when the city is at its busiest.

Networking mixers throughout the year create space for the conversations that happen after the panels end. The connections made in those rooms often matter more than the presentations themselves.

This is the largest HSMAI chapter in the world. That distinction exists because the New York hospitality community shows up, engages, and builds something together. Great content. Strong community. Real connections between hoteliers who understand that this industry runs on relationships.

If you want to be in the know, this is where you need to be.

The Moment is Now

The numbers are strong. The events are historic. The opportunity is sitting right in front of every hospitality professional in this city.

New York City spent years talking about recovery. That conversation is over. The city is not catching up to where it was. It is preparing to become something it has never been before.

The world is coming this summer. New York is ready.
