How do I measure whether AI recommends my business?

Vivander Advisors monitors how AI answers a defined set of buyer questions about a business. This guide explains how to record mentions, citations, recommendations and accuracy, then investigate changes over time.

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Write the questions a real customer would ask without naming you. Run each one on each AI platform at least three times, across two different days. Classify every answer as invisible, named without being offered, negative, mentioned, cited or recommended. Count recommendation events per platform. One answer proves nothing, because AI responses change from run to run even at the lowest temperature setting. This is the method behind the Vivander Advisors AI Visibility Audit, and a smaller version can be run by hand in an afternoon.

I have watched a room go quiet when the second run came back different from the first. That is the moment the method starts to matter.

Why one screenshot is weather

AI answers are sampled, not looked up. Two runs of the same prompt on the same platform an hour apart return different shortlists.

Kirsten and colleagues tracked 4,706 queries across two months. The pages Google's AI Overviews cited for the same questions overlapped by 18 percent. Between 9 and 28 percent of decisions changed at temperature zero, the setting that is supposed to be deterministic. Schulte and colleagues published a recommended minimum of seven to eight repetitions per prompt in 2026.

I run at least three per prompt per platform across two days, apply a two-of-three rule, publish the stability figure, and state in every report that three is below the recommended minimum. A vendor showing you one screenshot is showing you weather and calling it climate.

What prompts should you test?

Prompts in the customer's words. Unbranded. Traced to something people actually search. A hotel's set covers leisure, group, wedding and corporate voices. Restaurant sets covers occasions: anniversary, group dinner, business lunch, late night. Fifty or more per persona is my standard, and each maps to a source keyword so nobody can say the set was written to flatter.

Branded prompts get a small separate set. They measure recognition and accuracy, which are real questions, and say nothing about discovery. about discovery. ChatGPT recognized 99.4 percent of 112 startups when named and surfaced 3.32 percent in unbranded queries, per Sharma's 2026 study.

Which platforms?

Six. ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode. They share between 11 and 18 percent of cited URLs. Each is its own channel with its own score. Averaging them hides the one your customers use.

How should you score an answer?

Read the whole response. Then four gates in order. Is the business named and resolvable to you. Next, is it offered to the asker as an option. Is it negated. And is it linked. The outcome is one of six values, and only mentioned, cited and recommended count as visible. Position and endorsement strength are recorded on the side. They matter, and they never enter the yes or no.

Two more counts fall out of the same rows for free. Ghost Rankings: you were named or cited and a competitor was recommended in the same answer. Cited-when-mentioned: of the rows where you were visible, the share carrying a link to your own domain. The first tells you whether you are losing shortlists you made. And the second tells you whether AI is describing you from someone else's page.

How do the rows become a score?

I publish the math. The Visibility Stack Score weights four layers, 30, 25, 25 and 20, with a crawlability gate on the first two. It sits on a five-band ladder from Invisible to Optimized at 20-point intervals, taken from my book. Any vendor reporting a score moving from 73 to 93 without a formula is reporting a feeling.

My own scores changed definition in 2026 when landmark, foil and negated rows left the numerator. The reports say so. That direction was toward lower numbers and more honest ones, and I would rather explain a drop than defend a flattering one.

What should never be in the score?

Reputation and accuracy. A business nobody cites has no negative citations and no wrong claims. Fold those into a composite and you reward invisibility. I report both as tables and work lists. Never as a number inside the score.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Fifteen unbranded prompts, three customer voices, three runs on two platforms across two days. Save every answer and every cited URL. You will know more by Friday than most dashboards will tell you in a year.

For your role. Whoever reads those answers has to be willing to mark the property as "named, not offered." That takes a certain honesty, and it is why I would rather the reader not be the person whose bonus depends on the number.

For your customers. They ran the prompt once. That one run was their whole experience of you.

What I do not know

My scoring weights are set on judgment and stated as provisional until three audits validate them. Inter-rater agreement on the six-value classification has not been sized, which is why every row in my reports carries the quoted passage so the client can check the call against the text.

Where this is going

A hotel marketing director showed me a dashboard reporting visibility in 71 percent of prompts. I read the same prompts with the landmark and foil rows removed. Recommendation events: 9 percent. The dashboard had been counting the property's name in directions to the resort the assistant recommended.

Measurement in this field is early and imperfect, and I say that in every report. What separates a measurement from a feeling is whether someone else can rerun it.

Seventy-one was a feeling. Nine was a number.
