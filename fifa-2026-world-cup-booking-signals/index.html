FIFA 2026 World Cup Booking Signals

Michael J. Goldrich · February 20, 2026

What the Numbers Are Already Saying: Months Before Kickoff

Most hotel revenue managers see the World Cup 2026 booking curves climbing and feel good about what is ahead.

The picture is more layered than that.

At the February 2026 New York HSMAI event, Bram Gallagher, Director of Economics and Forecasting at AirDNA, presented a detailed analysis of short-term rental and hotel booking behavior across all U.S. host cities. AirDNA’s data comes from a proprietary panel of several million travelers whose origins and actual stays can be traced through review activity. This goes well beyond flight data to show where people are sleeping, how long they stay, and where they come from.

What Gallagher surfaced goes far beyond “demand is up.” It reveals a set of signals that hotel leaders, vacation rental operators included, should be reading carefully right now.

Source: Bram Gallagher, “FIFA 2026 World Cup Booking Signals,” presented at NY HSMAI, February 2026, AirDNA.

The Booking Surge Is Real. So Is the Cancellation Surge.

Demand pacing across World Cup host cities is dramatically elevated compared to the same point in 2025. The New York / Jersey City / Newark market leads in absolute volume, already 79 percent ahead of where it sat at this time last year. Mexican host cities (Mexico City, Monterrey, Guadalajara) show the highest percentage growth, though they started from a comparatively small base.

Here is the part that deserves more attention from revenue managers.

Cancellations also spiked after the December 5th final draw, and they have remained elevated ever since. Gallagher’s analysis shows a clear negative correlation between cancellation policy strictness and booking volume. Listings with flexible cancellation terms are capturing far more reservations than those with rigid policies.

The implication is direct: a meaningful share of current bookings may be speculative. Travelers are reserving lodging before they have confirmed FIFA tickets, treating their reservation as a placeholder rather than a commitment. Flexible policies are enabling this behavior at scale.

For hotel revenue managers, this creates a real projection risk. Occupancy curves that look strong today may carry a significant volume of reservations that will dissolve when ticket outcomes become clear. Watching cancellation pacing alongside booking pacing is now a core part of reading this market accurately.

Budget Beats Luxury: A Reversal Worth Understanding

The broader lodging market throughout 2025 saw luxury perform exceptionally well. Occupancy and rate gains concentrated in the top tier, while budget product saw price compression. The gap between the highest and lowest price segments widened across the year.

World Cup demand is running in the opposite direction.

In the New York / Jersey City / Newark market, budget-tier short-term rentals are showing the strongest occupancy pacing. Luxury is the weakest-performing segment in early data. Gallagher attributes this to the total cost of attending: tickets are expensive, transatlantic flights carry significant price tags, and currency exchange rates create additional pressure for many international travelers.

World Cup visitors appear to be making deliberate choices to reduce lodging spend so they can preserve budget for the experience itself.

The positioning implication is clear. Hotels that leaned heavily into luxury messaging throughout 2025 may need to recalibrate for this demand segment. World Cup travelers are likely to respond more strongly to experiential and location-based communication: proximity to transit, neighborhood character, access to the city’s culture.than to premium amenity messaging.

Geography Shapes Demand in Ways That Vary Sharply by Market

Gallagher’s data reveals how differently host cities are positioned, and why the patterns diverge so significantly.

New York / Jersey City / Newark stands out as the market with the strongest combination of absolute demand, length of stay, and international appeal. Guests booking in this market are staying longer, often five to eleven nights or more,treating the trip as a multi-week New York experience rather than a single-game visit. Hosting the finals amplifies every one of those signals.

Vancouver faces severe supply constraints. With roughly five to six thousand hotel rooms and similarly limited short-term rental inventory, compression is already reaching 40 percent occupancy pacing. Regulations prevent supply from expanding. The market will see dramatic rate compression, but total visitor volume will be capped by available inventory regardless of demand.

Los Angeles sits at the opposite end of the spectrum. It is a massive, geographically dispersed market with enough local population that many event attendees will be residents who require no lodging at all. Rate compression in Los Angeles has historically been low even for major events. Gallagher noted that Taylor Swift tour dates, which caused rate spikes in every other market, produced almost no compression in L.A. The World Cup data is following the same pattern.

Atlanta presents an unusual case. Rates have moved, but demand volume has remained weak. The stadium sits adjacent to a cluster of hotels with very little surrounding mixed-use development, which limits short-term rental appeal for travelers who prefer walkable urban neighborhoods. The travelers who are booking in Atlanta appear to be a selective subset choosing well-located premium inventory close to the venue.

Miami is holding relatively stable, with international travel composition remaining stronger than most markets. Fort Lauderdale is emerging as a meaningful satellite destination for Miami-bound visitors planning extended stays.

International Travel Patterns Are Shifting: Many of Those Shifts Will Persist

This is where the analysis becomes particularly valuable for long-range hotel strategy.

Gallagher used AirDNA’s proprietary panel data to map origin countries for each host city. This reveals who is coming, from where, and how those patterns are changing relative to prior years.

Canada is down approximately 30 percent in travel to World Cup host cities compared to last year. This reflects a measurable response to current political and trade tensions. Germany and Switzerland are also showing notable declines. German media coverage of travel concerns has been significant enough to register in booking data. Swiss travelers are responding to tariff developments in ways that appear to be reducing outbound travel to the U.S.

Argentina is up sharply. Diplomatic relations and Lionel Messi’s presence in Miami are both contributing factors. Argentine travelers are concentrating especially in Miami, where the data shows some of the strongest growth from a single origin country.

Latin American markets broadly, including Colombia, Mexico, and Brazil, are showing increases. This pattern accelerated after the pandemic and has stabilized at a higher level than pre-COVID baselines. Dallas-Fort Worth, with its geographic and cultural proximity to Latin America, is seeing international travel growth even as other markets decline.

France is the top origin country for short-term rental stays in the New York / Jersey City market. French traveler propensity has held steady or increased slightly relative to other European markets. With France scheduled to play in New York on June 16, there is a specific and measurable marketing opportunity for properties across the metro area.

The strategic point Gallagher raised deserves emphasis: shifts in international travel composition tend to be durable. When COVID reorganized which countries were sending travelers to which U.S. markets, those new patterns largely held for years afterward. The same principle applies to the current shifts. A hotel that builds meaningful appeal with Argentine travelers in Miami, or French travelers in New York, is developing relationships that extend well beyond the tournament window.

What This Means for Hotel Revenue Strategy

The World Cup is a rare demand event for U.S. host markets. Treating it as a straightforward high-demand period to price up and wait out would miss the nuance in the data.

Several things are true at the same time.

Demand is elevated, and cancellation risk is embedded in that demand. Budget-seeking behavior is dominant among the traveler segment most likely to attend. International composition is shifting in ways that create specific geographic opportunities. Length-of-stay data suggests the most engaged visitors are treating host cities as multi-week destinations, which rewards hotels positioned as urban base camps rather than event-adjacent lodging options.

Hotels that understand the texture of this demand. Who is arriving, from where, for how long, and with what price sensitivity. These factorsare positioned to make better decisions across pricing, cancellation policy design, messaging, and channel strategy.

Hotels tracking total occupancy pacing alone are working with a partial view.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What does “demand pacing” mean in the context of World Cup bookings?

Short answer: It measures how far ahead of last year’s booking levels a market currently sits.

Elaboration: Demand pacing compares how many rooms or listings are already reserved for a future date versus how many were booked at the same point in the prior year. A market showing 79 percent positive pacing, like New York,has 79 percent more reservations on the books right now than it did at this same calendar point in 2025. Pacing is a directional signal, and cancellation behavior will ultimately determine how much of that pacing converts to actual occupied room nights.

Q: Why are cancellation rates elevated if demand looks so strong?

Short answer: Many travelers are reserving space before they have confirmed tickets, using bookings as placeholders.

Elaboration: The FIFA ticket distribution process for 2026 includes a random selection phase, which means fans know they want to attend but have no certainty about whether they will secure tickets. To protect their lodging options, they book. They gravitate toward flexible cancellation policies that allow them to exit without penalty. This creates a pool of reservations that may dissolve when ticket outcomes are confirmed. Revenue managers need to account for this speculative layer when building occupancy projections for the tournament period.

Q: How should hotels think about adjusting cancellation policies?

Short answer: Flexible policies are generating more bookings, but those bookings carry higher conversion risk.

Elaboration: AirDNA’s analysis found a strong negative correlation between cancellation policy strictness and booking volume. Listings with flexible terms are booking up faster. However, those reservations are more likely to cancel as the ticket situation clarifies. Hotels need to weigh that trade-off against their specific revenue strategy and market position. Stricter policies may produce fewer reservations in the short term but higher conversion rates as the event approaches. The right calibration depends on each property’s competitive set and demand profile.

Q: Why is budget-tier lodging outperforming luxury for the World Cup?

Short answer: The total cost of attending, including tickets, flights, and currency conversion, is pushing travelers to reduce what they spend on lodging.

Elaboration: World Cup attendance is expensive before a traveler ever books a hotel room. When overall trip costs are high, lodging becomes the variable that many price-conscious travelers try to control. This creates demand concentration in budget and economy tiers. Luxury properties in World Cup host markets are seeing weaker relative pacing than they experienced throughout the rest of 2025. Properties that typically attract premium discretionary travelers may benefit from messaging adjustments that emphasize location, access, and experiential value over amenity tiers.

Q: Which U.S. host city represents the strongest overall opportunity for hotels?

Short answer: New York / Jersey City / Newark, by a significant margin in absolute terms.

Elaboration: This market combines the highest absolute booking volumes, the longest average lengths of stay, the strongest international appeal, and the additional draw of hosting the finals. Mexico City leads in percentage growth, but from a much smaller starting base. Vancouver will see intense rate compression, but hard supply limits cap total visitor volume. For U.S.-based hotel operators, the New York metro market represents the clearest revenue opportunity with the most supportive demand characteristics across every measured variable.

Q: How is international travel shifting across World Cup host cities?

Short answer: Canadian and some European markets are down sharply; Latin American markets are growing.

Elaboration: AirDNA’s panel data shows Canadian travelers down approximately 30 percent to World Cup host cities compared to the prior year. Germany and Switzerland are also declining, influenced by media coverage of trade and political tensions. By contrast, Argentina is up significantly, driven by diplomatic relations and Lionel Messi’s presence in the U.S. Colombia, Mexico, Brazil, and India are all showing increases. Dallas-Fort Worth is seeing gains in international arrivals while other markets soften, attributed to its geographic and cultural proximity to Latin American source markets.

Q: Why does France matter specifically for New York area hotel strategy?

Short answer: France is the top international origin country for short-term rental stays in the New York metro, and France is playing games there in June.

Elaboration: AirDNA’s data identifies French travelers as the largest international source market for the Jersey City / Newark area over the past twelve months. French traveler propensity has held relatively stable compared to other European markets showing declines. With France scheduled to play in the New York area on June 16, there is a specific and data-supported marketing opportunity for hotels that can reach French-speaking audiences before and during that window. This is one clear example of how origin composition data translates directly into targeted marketing decisions.

Q: What is the speculative booking risk and how should revenue managers account for it?

Short answer: A portion of current reservations may cancel when FIFA ticket availability becomes clear, making current pacing numbers potentially misleading.

Elaboration: Speculative bookings inflate current reservation totals because travelers are hedging on ticket access. As FIFA’s random ticket selection process resolves, a share of those reservations, particularly in flexible-policy properties,will fall off. Revenue managers who build forecasts on current pacing without applying a cancellation discount risk overstating actual occupied room nights significantly. Tracking the rate of cancellations alongside new reservations is now an essential part of accurate World Cup revenue modeling.

Q: How does World Cup demand data affect hotel strategy beyond pricing?

Short answer: It shapes messaging, channel selection, cancellation policy design, and length-of-stay strategy simultaneously.

Elaboration: Pricing is one lever among several. The data suggests that messaging should emphasize location and experiential access over luxury amenities for the World Cup traveler segment. Channel strategy should account for which origin markets are growing, including Latin American travelers, French visitors, and Argentine fans,and where those travelers research and book. Length-of-stay data suggests concentrated demand for extended stays in major leisure markets like New York, which rewards minimum night requirements and packages designed for multi-week guests rather than single-game attendees.

Q: How does this booking data connect to AI-driven travel discovery and hotel visibility?

Short answer: AI booking platforms surface structured, data-rich content. Hotels visible in that layer will capture demand that others miss entirely.

Elaboration: Travelers increasingly use AI assistants and conversational search tools to research destinations, compare lodging options, and build itineraries. These platforms favor hotels whose digital presence is structured for machine readability: clear location data, neighborhood context, specific attributes, and content that answers the questions travelers are actively asking. A French traveler planning a New York trip around the June 16 match will likely use an AI tool to find lodging options. Hotels that have optimized their content for that discovery layer will appear in those results. Understanding demand signals like those Gallagher presented is the analytical foundation. Ensuring the right travelers can find your property when they search is the operational execution.
