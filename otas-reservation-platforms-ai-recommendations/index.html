Do OTAs and reservation platforms help AI recommend my hotel or restaurant?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

Yes. For hotel questions, Booking.com and Airbnb frequently outrank Reddit as cited sources across AI platforms, per Peec AI's March 2026 read of 30 million sources. With restaurants, OpenTable and TripAdvisor pages show up again and again in my citation data. An assistant that cannot get a rate, a room type or a tasting-menu price from your own site gets it from the OTA page and cites the OTA. The twenty-year fight between the direct channel and the intermediaries now has a third participant that reads both and prefers whichever one states the facts. In every hospitality audit at Vivander Advisors I read OTA and reservation pages as citation sources alongside the client's own site.

I ran the direct channel at three hotel companies. The competitor was always the OTA. It still is, in a way nobody planned.

Eleven citations, all Booking.com

A management company's flagship was cited by ChatGPT in eleven family prompts. Every citation pointed to Booking.com.

Its own rooms page had no rate, no room count, and no mention of the connecting rooms it sells every weekend. The assistant went where the facts were.

Six weeks after those facts went into text on the hotel's own site, four of the eleven citations pointed to the hotel's domain. Same prompts, same assistant. Different source.

Why OTAs get cited so often

They are structured, complete, retrievable and current. A Booking.com page states the rate, the room types, the amenities by name, the policies, the distance to landmarks, and carries thousands of dated reviews, all in visible HTML any crawler can read.

That is the page an assistant wants when a traveler asks for a beachfront resort with a kids' club under $600. Most hotel websites hold the same facts inside a booking widget the crawlers cannot read, behind tabs, or nowhere.

The OTA did not out-market you. It out-published you.

Does that mean the direct channel is lost?

No. It means the direct channel has a new entry requirement.

An assistant that has your rate and room types from your own page can cite your page, and that citation is a link the guest clicks. One that has them only from Booking.com cites Booking.com. My cited-when-mentioned rate measures exactly this: of the answers where you are visible, how many carry a link to your own domain. A low rate means the assistant is describing you from someone else's page, and the fix lives on your site.

Do reservation platforms matter for restaurants?

Yes. OpenTable and TripAdvisor pages are retrievable, structured and dated, and they carry the two facts diners ask about most: price and availability. In my Boston audits the venue recommended for anniversary prompts was cited from an OpenTable page that stated its tasting-menu price. The venue never recommended had its menu in a PDF.

Resy and Tock pages are less consistently retrievable. I record each surface's access state per audit rather than assume.

Should you optimize the OTA listing?

Make it accurate and make it agree with your site. The listing is a corroborating source, and corroboration works when the facts match. A listing that says "no parking" while the site says "valet available" is a conflict the assistant resolves by trusting the more specific page, which is usually the OTA. Room names, rate tiers, amenity names, policies: the same words on both.

What about Google Hotels and agentic booking?

Google's agentic booking across Search, Maps and AI Mode calls a booking endpoint and rewards the fastest real availability. A hotel whose direct path returns a bookable rate to the agent keeps the lead. One whose path ends in a phone call loses it to whoever answered first, and that is often the OTA. This is the Booking Handoff check in my audit.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Put the rate range, room types, amenities and policies on your own site in text, in the same words the OTA uses. One version of every fact, everywhere.

For your role. Distribution strategy used to be a channel-mix exercise. It is becoming a question of which page the machine can read. Who owns that in your org chart?

For your guests. They do not know or care which page the assistant read. What they see is three hotels and a link. Whether the link is yours or Booking.com's is decided by which page had the rate on it.

What I do not know

Whether OTA citation share for hotels is stable or a snapshot of early 2026. Shares swing by platform and quarter, and I date every figure for that reason.

Where this is going

For twenty years distribution was a question of where guests clicked. OTA. Metasearch. Brand.com. Each channel had a cost, a contract, a fight.

Agentic commerce changes the question. The new one is whether your hotel is legible to the agent before the guest ever names a brand. An agent reads structured signals and real availability, not pretty homepages.

The OTAs figured out machine-readable twenty years ago because they had to. Hotels get to figure it out now, on their own pages, or keep renting the fact sheet.
