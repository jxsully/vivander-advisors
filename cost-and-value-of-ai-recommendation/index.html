What does getting recommended by AI cost, and what is it worth?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

The diagnostic is scoped around your locations, buyer personas, competitors and depth of testing, and typically takes two to three weeks. Its first fixes cost an editor's time and nothing else. The value shows up in your own analytics, through the AI referral channel Google Analytics added in 2026 and the direct bookings that follow, and part of it cannot be measured at all, because an AI-influenced visit that arrives with no referrer is unattributable by definition. Anyone who gives you a percentage for that invisible share is inventing it. At Vivander Advisors I price the work as fixed scopes, publish the math behind every score, and refuse industry multipliers in favor of your own numbers.

The innovation budget was fake money. That era ended. Boards want a denominator now.

The CMO who asked for the multiplier

She needed a number for a budget request. The industry conversion multiplier. Everyone else was using one.

I declined and helped her baseline the AI Assistant channel in GA4 for a month instead. The number was small, real, and hers. Her request went through, because the CFO could rerun it.

Every AI conversation I have watched die in a boardroom died the same way. Heads nodding through the deck. Then someone asked what the number meant, and nobody had a denominator.

What does the work cost?

The Vivander AI Visibility Audit is scoped by locations, buyer personas and competitor set, with optional retests at 60 and 90 days only if the client signing up wants them. A focused assessment can cover access and content without the full prompt universe. Answer Engine Content is scoped separately according to the pages that need work and the depth of revision. We agree on deliverables and fees before work begins. Request a tailored proposal.

Most clients ship the first three items on the fix list with their own editor and no budget. Facts into text. Questions into headings. Answers first.

Monitoring platforms run from a few hundred to several thousand dollars a month. Useful for tracking. Useless for diagnosis, because they count mentions and do not read whether you were offered.

How is the value measured?

In your own data, three places.

Google Analytics 4 has carried a native AI Assistant referral channel since May 2026. It reports visits that arrive with an AI referrer attached. There are documented blind spots, and it is the only honest starting point.

Direct bookings by source, before and after, against the same prompt set.

The audit baseline: recommendation events per platform at day 0. Retests at 60 and 90 days happen only if the client wants them.

Adobe's Q2 2026 AI Traffic Report gives direction. AI-referred traffic to US retailers grew 393 percent year over year, and by March 2026 AI-referred visitors converted 42 percent better than non-AI traffic, where a year earlier they had converted at about half the rate. Cite it as Adobe's, with the date, for retail, as direction. Never as a claim about your hotel.

What cannot be measured?

The visit that arrives with no referrer. A guest asks ChatGPT, gets three names, types yours into a browser. That shows as Direct. It is Direct. Nothing carried the referrer. The undercount is real and the size is unmeasurable in principle. That "about 70 percent shows as Direct" figure that circulates has no basis, and I will not use it.

Two more to refuse. "AI search visitors convert at 23 times" is one vendor measuring its own single site. And any pipeline projection with an estimated or assumed input fails a CFO's first question, which is "how did you derive that," and the answer cannot contain the word roughly.

What is the cost of doing nothing?

Consideration lost before the guest reaches any website. When an assistant answers with three names and yours is absent, the guest never learns you exist and there is no bid to raise.

Booking Holdings reported on its October 2025 earnings call that generative AI features were converting. Google confirmed at I/O 2026 that hotels are next for its universal commerce protocol, with an agent payment layer alongside. The direct channel's competitor used to be the OTA. Now it is an answer that names four hotels.

How should a business decide whether to spend?

Build the return case before the price is in the room. Your current direct booking value. The share of your market's discovery that has moved to assistants, as best your own analytics can show. And the cost of the three cheapest fixes. Name the person who owns whether it worked and the result that will count as proof.

Most AI decisions that stall in hotel companies stall because nobody did that, and the price landed before the case did.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Turn on the AI Assistant channel in GA4 and baseline it for 30 days. Contact Vivander Advisors with questions about assessing your AI visibility. Then decide, with your own numbers.

For your role. If you are asked for the multiplier, the honest answer is that there is not one, and the useful answer is a baseline you can rerun. The second answer gets budgets approved. That first one gets them cut.

For your guests. They asked one question. Whether you were in the answer decided whether you had a chance to convert them at all.

What I do not know

What share of AI influence is invisible to analytics. That it is undercounted is documented. The size cannot be measured, and I say so in every report rather than ship a number a client could falsify.

Where this is going

Thirty-nine percent of leaders are investing in AI agents, and most cannot measure the return. That gap is where the next round of AI budgets will be won or lost, and it will be won by the teams that can show a number someone else can recheck.

The organizations winning right now are not the ones with the biggest budgets. They are the ones who recognized the shift early and measured it honestly.
