Does press coverage still matter when AI answers the question?

Michael J. Goldrich · September 2026

In aggregate, yes. Per placement, almost not at all. Journalism accounts for about 27 percent of AI citations across more than 20,000 outlets, per Muck Rack's May 2026 read of roughly 25 million links. That makes the average outlet worth about 0.001 percent. The ten most-cited domains across 30 million sources contain one legacy news brand, Forbes, and no wire service, per Peec AI. Neither the New York Times nor Reuters reaches the top three cited sources for any of 17 industries. Press works when it lands on the handful of outlets your platform reads for your category. At Vivander Advisors I identify those outlets per client rather than recommend coverage in general.

Forty placements in a quarter. Two cited. That was a real client, and the two were the cheapest.

Forty clips, two citations

A restaurant group's PR agency reported forty placements in a quarter. Good agency. Real placements.

I ran the audit. Two of the forty were cited by any platform for any prompt. Both were local lists. Each stated a price. The other thirty-eight sat on outlets no platform retrieved for the category.

The agency measured coverage. Engines measure something else.

Why "earned media drives AI" is the wrong sentence

The figure that circulates, that earned media drives 84 percent of AI citations, comes from a definition of earned media that includes Wikipedia, PubMed, government sources and third-party corporate blogs. Pull journalism out on its own and it is 27 percent, and it is atomized across twenty thousand doors.

The finding that changes strategy is not "get press." It is "get the right two placements and stop counting clips."

The documented exception is Axios, in ChatGPT's top three cited sources across 13 of 17 industries, the only journalism outlet to manage that on any platform. For hospitality, trade press and local editorial lists appear in my citation data far more often than national news.

Does local editorial matter for hotels and restaurants?

More than national. A "best anniversary restaurants in Boston" list on a local magazine's site is a structured, dated, retrievable page that names venues and says what they are for. In my restaurant audits those lists get cited on Perplexity and Gemini for occasion prompts more than any national outlet.

For hotels, editorial coverage that states specifics gets cited. The suite type, the rate tier, the kids' club. Coverage that calls you "an oasis of tranquility" does not, because there is nothing in it to extract.

Does old coverage still get cited?

Yes, and it cuts both ways. AI-cited pages average about 1,064 days old, per Ahrefs. A 2022 feature that states facts about your property sits in the middle of the citation age distribution. So does a 2019 article about a problem you fixed in 2020.

Suppression moves rank. It does not move citation. The answer to stale coverage is a newer, more specific, corroborated statement of the current fact on an outlet the platform reads.

Does paid or sponsored editorial work?

Within bounds. Current models do not reliably separate paid from organic coverage on a credible domain, so a sponsored placement on an outlet the platform trusts can accelerate citation presence while earned relationships develop.

Three bounds, every time. Disclosure is mandatory. A low-quality outlet adds nothing at any price, because the surrounding site's authority carries the value. And durability is unproven. It supplements earned coverage and never replaces it.

What about self-published "best of" lists?

Documented to earn citations briefly. I do not recommend them. The documented citation preference runs to third-party lists, the tactic's own advocates predict decay within quarters, and a business caught ranking itself first pays in credibility.

Write a how-to-choose guide instead. Openly authored. Honest about trade-offs. Your name early, as one option. It survives scrutiny and gets cited for the same questions.

Three things worth sitting with

For your business. Pull the cited sources from your audit and list every editorial outlet that appears more than once per platform. That is the whole target list. It is shorter than you think.

For your role. If PR reports clips and SEO reports rankings, nobody is reporting cited placements per platform. That report is the one that matters now, and it belongs to someone.

For your guests. The assistant read one local list and trusted it. Your national feature never entered the conversation.

What I do not know

Whether institutional and government sources, which dominate medical citations, transfer to hospitality. A tourism board or a CVB being cited for a hotel query has not been measured. I run the playbook because the sources are cheap and claimable, and I flag the result as provisional.

Where this is going

Shift PR from backlinks to mentions. I said that in 2025 and the evidence has only hardened. The engine does not count your coverage. It reads a small number of pages it trusts and repeats what they say.

A press release is a message to journalists. By contrast, a cited page is a message to the machine that answers your customer. They are different documents, and right now most businesses only write the first one.
