AI Keeps Getting the Business Wrong. Start With the Facts.

Michael J. Goldrich · October 15, 2026

A hotel stops accepting pets. Its website reflects the change. An old directory still says “pet friendly.” A travel article repeats the earlier policy. An AI assistant recommends the hotel to someone travelling with a dog.

The hotel gets a mention. The guest gets bad advice. The front desk gets the conversation nobody wanted.

This hypothetical example captures a practical problem in AI visibility: being named is only useful when the answer describes a business that actually exists today.

Vivander calls the continuing work on these facts and supporting sources signal optimization. Within the broader practice of generative engine optimization, or GEO, it connects the website foundation to the evidence surrounding the business.

Start with the facts that change a buying decision

Some discrepancies deserve attention before others. A slightly different company description may be harmless. An incorrect service area, cancellation rule or eligibility requirement can lead someone to make the wrong choice.

Start with a question: what would a customer need to know before contacting, booking or buying from this business?

  • What is available, and who is it suitable for?
  • Where and when is it available?
  • Which limits, fees or conditions could change the decision?
  • How does the customer take the next step?

For a restaurant, that could include private dining capacity and accessibility details. For a consultancy, it could include the industries served, project scope and delivery format. For software, it could include supported integrations and plan limits.

These are operational facts. Marketing needs the people responsible for the service to verify them.

Create a business fact record with evidence behind it

A useful fact record contains the approved statement, a supporting source, an owner and a review date. Add an effective date when a policy changes. Record qualifications rather than compressing every answer into yes or no.

“Accessible rooms available” is less useful than a description of the relevant features and how guests confirm availability. “Integrates with accounting software” is less useful than naming supported products and explaining the connection.

The record gives the team a reference for corrections. Publishing an appropriate summary can also help customers. Calling that summary a Fact Block does not make it a special instruction that every AI system will obey.

Google documents that Organization structured data can help it understand and distinguish an organization. That supports maintaining clear identity information; it does not establish a universal AI ranking benefit for every fact field.

Follow the contradiction back to its source

Save the AI answer before changing anything. Record the question, platform, date and cited links. Open those links and check whether they support the disputed statement.

A citation is a useful starting point, but the cited page may not contain the claim. If there is no supporting source, record the claim as unsupported rather than guessing where it came from.

Next, inspect the pages and profiles a customer is likely to encounter: the relevant service page, booking information, business listing, industry directory and any older coverage that still attracts attention.

Google’s local ranking guidance explicitly emphasizes complete, accurate business information. This is guidance for Google’s local results, rather than proof that all AI assistants use the same inputs or weights.

Correct the public record in a sensible order

Fix the business’s own authoritative pages first. An external publisher needs somewhere reliable to check a correction. Update visible text and any related structured data together.

Then correct profiles the business controls. For independent publications, send a specific correction request with the inaccurate statement, the current fact and a link to supporting evidence. Keep a record of the request and whether the publisher acted.

Historical coverage may have been accurate when written. Where appropriate, ask for an update or an explanatory note. A review describing an old experience should not be rewritten to pretend the experience never happened.

Prioritize sources implicated in consequential errors and sources customers actually use. There is little value in spending a week polishing an obscure listing while a primary booking page remains wrong.

Check for improvement without claiming causation

Repeat the original question under comparable conditions. Keep the wording, location context and search setting consistent. Save several observations over time rather than treating one corrected answer as the end of the issue.

If the wrong policy disappears after a directory update, the sequence is encouraging. It still does not prove that the directory caused the earlier error or that the correction caused the later answer. Other sources and platform changes may be involved.

The practical outcome is a cleaner public record and a documented check on the answer. If an error persists, investigate another source or an ambiguity in the fact itself.

Give maintenance an owner

Make fact updates part of the business change process. When a service, policy, location or booking rule changes, assign the website and listing updates at the same time. Review high-impact information more often than stable background information.

The first useful deliverable is small: ten decision-critical facts, their supporting sources and the person responsible for keeping each current.
